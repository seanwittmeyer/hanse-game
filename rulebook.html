Brewhouses of the Hanse

Rulebook · 4 pages, 2 sheets, print double sided (flip on the long edge)
Brewhouses of the Hanse
A merchant brewing house in Hamburg, about 1350
2–4 players
90 minutes
10–15 turns each
Brew the beer, stand your dice at sea, and land it where your buildings pay.

1The Goal

You run a brewing house at the Wharf of Hamburg. Every turn you source grain and hops, brew them into casks, age those casks, and send them to the Kontore of the Hanseatic League: by cart to Bruges, by Ship to London, Bergen and Novgorod.

Beer you cannot carry is worth nothing, so you are a merchant too. Your dice at sea, standing as posts on the shipping lanes and as Kontor buildings, decide what you may deliver and what it pays. The player with the most ★ at the end wins.

The heartEleven dice are your whole game. Each one becomes a cask, a post or a building, and never comes back; the first empty supply ends the game. Count your dice at sea: that is the quality of beer you may deliver.

3What You Own

  • 11 quality dice in your colour: 10 in your personal supply and 1 already standing at sea as your starter post. A die leaves the supply to become a cask, a post or a Kontor building, and never comes back.
  • A hand of 4 private building tiles, one per station, each with a stronger second face (§8).
  • A set of 3 Kontor building tiles: Warehouse, Kontorhaus, Guildhouse (§9).
  • A player board: 3 vessels for aging casks, 2 seats for Specialists, and the space for your supply.
  • Recipe cards, which are permission to brew a beer.
  • Goods: grain and hops . They are the only currency in the game.

4Setup

Each player takes

  • 3 grain and 2 hops . Your storage cap is 8 of each good for the whole game.
  • The Gruit and Hopped recipe cards, beside your board.
  • 11 quality dice, your 4 private building tiles as a hand, your 3 Kontor building tiles, and 1 score disc on space 0 of the score track.

The shared table

  • Lay out the Wharf and the sea board, side A for two players, side B for three or four. Set the hall die on its seat at Bruges showing 2.
  • Public Works: shuffle the tiles and deal 6 (two or three players) or 4 (four players) onto random slots. The rest go to the box. Nobody builds one, and nothing ever refills a slot (§8).
  • Deal 3 of the 4 export beers for this game, 4 recipe cards each. Return the undealt export’s cards and cask tiles to the box.
  • Stack the cask tiles in face-up stacks, one per beer. Every Brew of a Q2+ beer searches its stack and picks a tile; Gruit takes the top tile.
  • Shuffle the Ship deck: display of 3. Shuffle the Specialist deck: display of 4. No Ship is docked at setup. Set out the invitation tokens and the 3 Kontor chits; everyone starts with no invitation.
  • The starter post: in reverse turn order, each player stands their eleventh die showing 1 in their own seat on the first segment of either lane, W1 or E1.
  • Workers start off the board — each player’s first turn places theirs on any station (§6). Choose a first player, who stays first all game.
Why deal6 of the 7 Public Works stand and 3 of 4 exports are dealt, so no two wharves run the same economy. Read what was drawn before you plan.

6Your Turn, Step by Step

  1. Move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent station. Never diagonally, and never staying put — the move is the turn. Sharing a station with rivals costs nothing. Your first turn instead places your worker on any station.
  2. Work the station. It offers up to six stops: its PRIMARY, its ALTERNATE, a load at each of its two flanking slots, and your own private building standing on either.
  3. Resolve them in any order. Every stop is optional except the Harbor’s commission, and each may be used once.

7The Cask and Its Die

One die does five jobs. It is the cask’s age, its readiness, its value, its presence, and a beat of the game clock. Follow one Bock through its life:

Brew Set the die to the tile’s printed start. A Bock starts at 2.
Age Each step turns it +1. Never past the beer’s quality.
Ready Die reaches the quality, here 5. Now it can load.
Board A Bock needs five of your dice at sea. A wharf building may lift the die to 6.
Land Score the die + your building die there (§10). The die stays as presence.
Dice never turn on their ownAging only happens when something ages it: the Cellar, an Age +2 cask bonus, your Cold Store or Lagering Cellar, or the Braumeister. A forgotten cask sits at its start value forever.
LiftsOnly wharf buildings turn a die past its quality: the Malt Kiln, the Bonded Store, your Lagering Cellar. The cap is quality + 1, so 6 is the highest face in the game.
The eight bonusesGain 2 goods · Age +2 · Load 1 more · Brew 1 · Gain 1 recipe at its fee · Gain 1 Specialist · Build · Post. A bonus fires once, as its cask boards a Ship or is carted. Every Gruit tile prints Gain 2 goods, so Gruit is never searched.

8The Slots and the Two Building Families

Public Works — brown, the wharf itself

Nobody builds them, and none of them lasts. Setup deals 6 or 4 of them onto random slots — die-less furniture, passive on its own slot’s traffic, whoever’s traffic it is. Malt Kiln (×2): a loading die +1, cap quality + 1 · Customs House: your quality count reads +1 for the Ship docked here · Ropewalk: a load here may also load 1 cask onto a different Ship · Cooperage: +1 berth · Bonded Store: a loading die +1; at the sail every shipper gains 2 goods · Victualling Yard: the cask bonus fires ×2.

The tide. A sailing Ship takes its slot’s Public Work with it, boxed, for good. Nothing refills the ground: the wharf strips into the ground your private buildings take.

Private buildings — your colour ring, the family you build

Your hand of 4 tiles, one per station: a tier 1 face, its tier 2 behind. A tile stands only on a slot flanking its station and fires only when you work that station; one of yours per station, rivals never use it, the tide never takes it. Each prints its ★, scored while it stands: 2 on tier 1, 4 on tier 2. Three doors, one verb, BUILD: the Brewhouse’s alternate, a Build bonus, London’s prize.

  • Place a tier 1 onto an open flanking slot (11) — or onto a Public Work, replacing it (+1, the worn tile boxed). A docked Ship above is fine. Never onto a rival’s tile.
  • FLIP your standing tier 1 over in place to its tier 2 (21). It needs the tier 1 and nothing else; every tier 2 contains its tier 1.

9The Sea — Posts, the Chain and the Count

Two lanes leave Hamburg as a tree of segments: W1 the Wadden Coast then W2 the Dover Strait to London; E1 the Skagerrak to Bergen, and on through E2 the Sound to Novgorod. Every segment prints one post seat per colour, so posts never block: every player may hold every segment.

A post is a die of yours showing 1 in your seat on a segment. It costs an action and the die, never goods. Your posts grow from Hamburg: a new post always takes the lowest segment of its lane you do not yet hold. Each time any Ship sails through a segment, every post there turns +1, cap 6.

A lane is open once every segment of it holds anyone’s post, and an open lane is open to every player’s Ships.

A Kontor building is one of your three tiles set in a slot at a far Kontor and marked with a supply die showing 1, built only where your own post stands on every segment of that Kontor’s lane; one per player per Kontor. The die is your delivery modifier: every cask of yours landing there scores its die plus this die, and it turns +1 each time any cask lands there or you raise it.

The tile’s line fires on each landing of yours there: Warehouse — gain 11 · Kontorhaus — gain 1 more invitation · Guildhouse — raise one die of yours at sea +1.

The quality count
Count your dice standing at sea — posts and building dice, the starter included. That number is the quality of beer you may deliver anywhere. One carts a Gruit; two ships a Hopped; five ships a Bock. The Customs House and the Lodesman read +1.

10Ships, Loading and Landing

Ships are neutral hulls from a display of 3, each bound for a printed Kontor or wild: Cog, 2 berths, free · Hulk, 3 berths, 1. Nobody owns a hull; anyone may load any docked Ship. No Ship goes to Bruges.

To load

Take 1 Ready cask from your own vessels whose beer is Q2 or better, whose Q is at most your quality count, and whose Ship’s lane is open. Seat it in the lowest empty berth; its bonus fires after any sail it completes. A wild Ship is named by the first cask loaded: its owner chooses any open far Kontor and sets that Kontor’s chit on the hull.

To sail

A Ship sails the moment it is full, and never before (the Shipmaster excepted). The slot’s Public Work departs with it. Every post on its lane turns +1. Then each cask lands in boarding order. The hull returns to the deck; the display refills at once.

The landing

  1. Score the cask die + your building die at that Kontor. No building: the die alone. Nothing else.
  2. Park the die in the Kontor’s field, its tile under it. It never scores again.
  3. Every building die there turns +1. If it is yours, its tile’s line fires.
  4. Take 1 invitation, and the Kontor’s prize.

11Bruges — the Cart, the Yard and the Hall

Bruges is reached by cart only: the Cellar’s alternate sends one Ready cask by road (two with a Kaufhaus or the Carter), count permitting (a Gruit needs 1). No Ship, no lane, no invitation earned. The cask’s bonus fires as it is carted. It resolves at once at one of two doors:

A · The yard — goods and recipes
Any beer, and Gruit’s only door. Park the die on the yard track’s next open place, tile under it, and take the zone’s prize. Best (places 1–3): a recipe from the display, its fee waived, or 2 goods. Good (4–6): a recipe at its fee, or 1 good. OK (7 and beyond): 1 good. On side A the zones are two places each. A yard die is presence and a Flight beer; it carries no majority weight.
B · The hall — the guild of brewmasters
A Q2+ cask and 1 invitation. Spend the invitation. Score the cask die + the hall die. Park your die on the hall’s next open place, tile under it, and take the place’s printed prize if any (the first three pay 2 · 2 · 1 goods). Then the hall die turns +1, cap 6: each present raises the price of the next. No recipe. Gruit never. Hall full: the yard.

Invitations are tokens, face up, with no limit. The only way to earn one is to land a cask at a far Kontor: one per cask, a second with a Kontorhaus. The only way to spend one is the hall. The hall’s places are Bruges’ majority: 4 / 2 to the two players with the most dice there.

12Specialists, Goods and Storage

Specialists are private purple tiles, each an asymmetric power. You have 2 seats. They are earned, never bought: Bergen’s prize seats one free, and the Gain 1 Specialist cask bonus does the same. Never two of a kind. The display refills at end of turn; the Ship display at once.

Braumeister: turn start, age one cask +1 · Shipmaster: at the Harbor, sail one docked Ship carrying your cask, unfull (a wild one: you name its port) · Cellarman: your brews start +1 · Stevedore: each load, up to 2 casks · Agent: a rival lands where you hold a building, that die +1 more · Lodesman: your count reads +1 · Carter: your cart carries 2; the yard’s goods pay you +1 · Guildmaster: each present +2★ · Chronicler: each cask you land, at a Kontor, the yard or the hall, +1★ · Alderman: end, +2★ per Kontor with 3+ of your dice parked.

Grain and hops are the only currency, and your storage cap is 8 of each — anything gained past a cap is lost.

13Ending the Game

One clock: the first empty personal supply. The moment a player spends their last supply die, the final round is set. Finish the round so every player has equal turns, then score.

Where your eleven dice can be
in the supply, in a vessel, riding a berth, standing as a post, marking a Kontor building, or parked in a field, on the yard track or on a hall place. Every one is public.
Nothing sails at the endA die still aboard a docked Ship scores its pips only. Brews in your vessels score nothing. Round 18 backstops.
TiebreakMost ★ wins; then the quality count, then most goods.
