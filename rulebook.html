1The Brewing House

Hamburg was called the brewhouse of the Hanse. You are a merchant brewing house in the League, c. 1350.

The pivotal innovation of your age is hopped beer. Unlike perishable gruit ale, hopped beer survives a sea voyage — so for the first time, beer becomes a shippable export. You will source grain and hops, brew, push casks across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts (the kontore: Bergen, London, Bruges, Novgorod) for reach — or withdraw your finest into the brewers' Hall as permanent standing.

Every cask poses one question, again and again: do you keep it working in commerce for reach, or take it out of commerce to become heritage and pure reputation? That single tension is the whole game.

2The Core Idea — Reach vs. Standing

There are two kinds of value, and they never convert into one another:

  • REACH — volume and presence. A ready cask deployed to a slot spreads your presence across the trade routes to the kontore. This is the path of scale.
  • STANDING — scarcity and reputation. A ready cask enshrined at a kontor is taken out of commerce forever, becoming unspendable reputation. This is the path of prestige.

Both score, and they're coupled by the demand market: realizing a type either way pulls its value down, so the volume crowd erodes the prestige crowd's payout. With slots, vessels, and turns all scarce — and the same cask able to reach or enshrine, never both — leaning, and timing, is the game.

Three design constraints

No dice (fully deterministic) · No hidden draws (everything you acquire is taken from a face-up display — recipe cards included — never a blind deck) · No money (goods are the only currency; standing is never spent).

3Components

  • 1 Main board — the 2×2 action grid (A Market · B Harbor · C Hall · D Brewhouse), 8 perimeter slots, and the standing-stack area.
  • 1 Route board — home port and four routes to the kontore, with presence spaces, route-value tracks, and majority tracks.
  • 5 Player tableaus — each a private brewery: the brewing track (Load · Ferment · Age · Ready), vessel lanes, the Brewhouse Floor (4 multi-use slots, each a Room or a working Cask), the recipe-strip tuck along the bottom edge, a standing track + goal row, and printed starting Larder + Quay.
  • 1 Demand-market track — a marker per beer type; a cask's enshrine payout = its type's value, which drops as the type is realized and rises on recipe-buys / Fair pumps.
  • Goods cubes — grain (G) and hops (H), the only currency.
  • The tile supply (see the visualizer):
    • Cask tiles — the dual-role hero (working/reach ↔ standing), brewed from a shared pool with fixed counts (~L1×16 · L2×20 · L3×12 · L4×8 · L5×4); quality is printed on the tile, age is tracked by track position + one aging cube; a colored ownership disc marks a cask deployed in a shared slot.
    • Route Lane, Fair, Ship, Privilege, and Room tiles.
    • Recipe cards — a small face-up Market deck (frontier-gated), each dual-use: a big on-collect boon + a permanent brew strip.
  • Per player: 1 worker pawn, presence markers, standing markers.
  • 1 first-player marker.

4Anatomy of the Game

The Action Grid

Four action cells in a 2×2: A Market & B Harbor (top row), C Hall & D Brewhouse (bottom row). The two builders (Market, Brewhouse) sit on one diagonal and the two cash-outs (Harbor, Hall) on the other, so they never share a line — every line pairs one builder with one cash-out, and builders pump the market while cash-outs realize it. Each cell belongs to one row and one column — a line. Around the grid sit 8 perimeter slots, two capping each line.

The Lines & Slots

A line is a 4-stop pipeline: cap slot · cell · cell · cap slot. Each perimeter slot belongs to exactly one line — a row xor a column. Cells are generalists (each sits in two lines); slot tiles are specialists (each fires only when its one line runs).

The Route Board (Reach)

Home port at center; four routes radiate to the kontore. Casks reach a kontor as presence — but only Bruges is in direct-deploy range of the Harbor; the three far kontore (London, Bergen, Novgorod) are reached only by sailing a ship there (§9), or by upgrading direct-deploy with the Quay room. Each kontor rewards differently — on delivery (the ship's destination bonus, to the owner) and as a slotted route lane (its skim) — so there is no single best line of play:

The Hall & Standing (Standing)

Enshrined casks pile on the standing stack as tactile sediment (colored edges let you eyeball composition) — but nothing scoring-relevant is hidden there: each enshrine advances the owner's standing track by the type's market value, and the cask's goal sits face-up in the owner's row.

Your Tableau (the Brewery)

Not a copy of the grid — it is the vessel the brewing happens in. The brewing track carries casks Load → Ferment → Age → Ready across your vessels (start with 1, cap 3 — each is a parallel brew lane); the Brewhouse Floor is 4 multi-use slots, each holding a Room (permanent) or a working Cask (temporary) — and Extra Vessel is itself a Room, so going wide eats Floor depth (the forced-commit squeeze); your collected recipe strips tuck under the board's bottom edge (the types you may brew); the Larder and Quay provide the fallback trickle when the public board is crowded.

5Setup

  1. Place the main board and route board. Leave all 8 perimeter slots empty. Bruges begins open; the other three routes are closed.
  2. Set the standing stacks empty. Set each type's market-value marker to its start.
  3. Build the face-up Market display ⚙ from the tile supply (a row of available tiles, refilled as taken).
  4. Set the type value track markers to their starting values, and the type frontier at Hopped (only Gruit + Hopped recipes are available at the Market to start; the premium summit unlocks as types saturate). Deal the summit roster (Bock / Mumme / Broyhan / Keut) into the L3–L5 rungs in a random order ⚙.
  5. Each player takes a tableau, a worker, presence markers, standing 0, and a starting stake: 3 , 2 , the Gruit baseline (printed on the board, always brewable) + 2 random premium recipe cards tucked under the bottom edge ⚙. One vessel lane is open; all 4 Brewhouse Floor slots empty.
  6. Each player places their worker on any cell. Give the youngest brewer the first-player marker.

6A Turn

On your turn, in order:

  1. Move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent cell (A↔B, A↔C, B↔D, C↔D — never the diagonal). This is mandatory.
  2. Activate the row OR the column of your worker's new cell.
  3. Resolve the line's up-to-4 stops — cap · cell · cell · cap — in any order:
    • Each cell: take its public action. If a rival worker occupies that cell, you instead take your private tableau fallback for that action.
    • Each cap slot: if it holds an owned tile, it fires its line action — no matter who activated the line. A Route Lane skims its per-kontor benefit to its owner; a Ship lets the active player load a Ready cask aboard (loader takes a small benefit, the ship's owner skims a good) — and sails the instant it fills (§9); a deployed cask fires its action for the active player; a Fair offers the active player its paid pump.

Both cells always fire

Activating a line triggers both of its cells. Your worker's position only determines which line is legal and which cells are blocked to you (occupied by rivals). A turn therefore resolves up to two cell actions and two slot skims.

Play passes clockwise. When every player has taken a turn, the round ends and the first-player marker passes left.

8Brewing

Casks are brewed, not bought, from a shared pool with fixed global counts (~L1×16 · L2×20 · L3×12 · L4×8 · L5×4) — the rare summit casks are a contested resource, and a player's cap is "what's left to brew." Beer has two layers: a type (a global quality rung) and a recipe (a collected card whose brew strip is tucked under your board, with its own cost profile). You cannot brew a type you hold no strip for — Gruit is the universal baseline, always brewable. A recipe loaded onto your track crawls through it — your brews age 1 step automatically every turn, and the Brewhouse action gives a pool of advance points you allocate across vessels to push brews along faster, so more vessels = more throughput. A brewed cask's quality is printed on the tile; its age is tracked by its position on the Load→Ferment→Age→Ready track plus one aging cube (Gruit skips Age · L2/L3 1 pip · L4 2 · L5 3).

The type ladder is an anchored spine + a variable summit. L1 Gruit → L2 Hopped is fixed and historical — hops is the preservation tech that opens the sea routes. The premium tiers L3–L5 are dealt each game from the historical Hanse export beers — Bock (Einbeck), Mumme (Braunschweig), Broyhan (Hannover), Keut (Low Countries) — so the efficient climb differs every game. The Market only sells recipes for currently-unlocked types: the type frontier advances as a type's market saturates (its value drops and the next tier opens — one causal loop).

Gruit vs. hopped is a tempo decision

Gruit skips the Age step — fast and cheap, frees the vessel quickly — but it is perishable: it can only reach Bruges and can never be enshrined. Hopped beer and above take the extra Age step (tying up a vessel longer), but they travel far and can become standing. That single fact — perishable never becomes heritage — is the heart of the theme.

Recipe cards (dual-use)

Recipes are dual-use cards, acquired only at the Market from a small face-up deck (frontier-gated). Each card does two jobs, rhyming with the dual-role cask:

  • A big one-time boon on collect (≈ a free action), scaling by tier ⚙: L2 Stocked Pantry = +2 2 · L3 Brewmaster's Push = advance 3 across your vessels · L4 Grand Market = take 2 goods and buy one slot tile at −1 · L5 Master's Privilege = choose one (advance 3 / +2 2 / a free Market / a free Harbor deploy). Boons grant resources / tempo / a little presence only — never standing or raw points.
  • A permanent brew strip — the card tucks under the board's bottom edge (only its type + cost profile showing). Your fanned row of strips is your recipe book; you brew from any tucked strip forever.
  • Pumps its type +1 on collect (respecting the pump ≤ drop guardrail), and there is a soft cap of 6 strips: collecting a 7th forces you to discard a strip (you keep the boon you already banked).

The demand market

Each type sits on a shared value track, and a cask's enshrine payout = its type's value at that instant (banked on your standing track — this one number is the whole variable-value layer; there are no separate VP tokens). The market drops −1 every time a type is realized — deployed for reach or enshrined for standing — so the volume crowd flooding a type erodes the prestige crowd's payout. It rises +1 when you buy that type's recipe or pay at a Fair (§11). New tiers enter high, then erode. Guardrail ⚙: a pump (+1) never exceeds a realize-drop (−1), so the market can't be farmed into inflation. The whole reach-vs-standing tension becomes a timing decision: cash your type while it's high.

9Deploying & Reach

There are two ways a Ready cask becomes presence (reach), both taken at the Harbor:

Direct-deploy (near range)

  1. Take one Ready cask and deploy it into an empty perimeter slot, bound to a route whose quality gate it meets. Direct-deploy reaches Bruges only — unless you hold the Quay room, which upgrades it to any open route.
  2. Place 1 presence marker on that route (reach), and drop that type's market value −1.
  3. The deployed cask now is your presence on the route and fires its line action when the line runs — until it is enshrined (by you or a rival), at which point its presence leaves the board. The cask is the reach piece; enshrining converts it to standing.

Ships — single-use carriers to the far kontore

The far kontore (London, Bergen, Novgorod) are reached only by ship (the merchant's reach engine). A ship has a clean load → fill → sail lifecycle:

  1. Build (at the Market): a ship goes into a perimeter slot, assigned to a destination kontor, with capacity Cog 2 / Hulk 3, inheriting that route's quality gate.
  2. Load (whenever the ship's line fires, by anyone): the active player may put one Ready cask that meets the gate aboard. The loader takes a small benefit; the ship's owner skims a good (toll-baron). (The type's −1 realize fires when the cask becomes presence — on sail.)
  3. Sail (the instant a ship is full): every cask aboard drops as presence (reach) at the destination, the owner banks the per-kontor destination bonus (§4 table), and the ship tile moves to the shared Sailed-Ships track — it is consumed (single-use; build a new one to ship again). Relief valve: the owner may launch a partial ship early via a Harbor action.

Presence is clamped to each route's capacity (player-scaled — see §13). A route is only open if a Route Lane tile for it sits in a slot (Bruges excepted — always open). A route's end-game value is the sum of the Lane tiles players have slotted onto it — players author what scores. Most presence on a route claims its majority. You may also install a Ready cask into an open Floor slot as a working cask (free, but needs an open slot — if the Floor is full the Ready cask clogs its vessel): assign it to a station (Market, Brewhouse, or Harbor) and it soups that station up — whenever you use that cell (or its twin when a rival blocks it) the cask also fires its work action. It scores nothing itself and relieves vessel back-pressure; pull it out later to deploy or enshrine.

The merchant archetype

Routes + ships make a full reach engine to mirror the standing engine (rooms + summit brewing): open routes (author their value), build ships, skim every load + bank destination bonuses + deliver your own casks for presence, then recycle goods and Privileges into more lanes and ships. You must still brew to have casks to load.

10Enshrining & Standing

To enshrine, activate a line containing the Hall (cell C): the bottom row (Hall + Brewhouse) or the left column (Market + Hall). Then take a cask — a Ready cask from your brewery, or a deployed cask sitting in *any* shared slot — and place it on the standing stack:

  • Its owner advances their standing track by the type's current market value, the cask's goal flips face-up in the owner's row, and that type's market value drops −1.
  • If the cask was deployed, its presence (reach) leaves the board and its slot frees.

Any player may enshrine a deployed cask — the positive-interaction engine

On a Hall line you may enshrine a rival's deployed cask: you reclaim the slot you want; they get standing — but less than they were holding out for, and not on their timing. Nobody is attacked; nobody hoards a slot forever. This is also why a cask is the reach piece: enshrining it converts reach into standing (the markers never convert — only the tile transitions). The self-enshrine reason is timing: cash your type before reach-floods or a rival's enshrine tank its value.

11Slots, Skims & Tolls

  • The 8 perimeter slots hold a churning mix — deployed casks (your reach; transient, enshrined off), Fairs, Route Lanes, and ships (single-use; consumed when they sail). Every slot is a row-xor-column commitment. (Recipe strips tuck under your board, not a slot.)
  • When a line fires, its cap tiles resolve: deployed casks fire an ACTION (for the active player); a Route Lane skims its per-kontor benefit to the owner (Bruges +1 · Bergen a toll on rivals · London a Privilege advance · Novgorod +1 or a brew step); a Ship lets the active player load a Ready cask (loader benefit, owner skims a good) and sails when full; a Fair lets the active player pay 1 to pump a type +1, the fee going to the Fair's owner (the owner pays the supply when self-using). Because pumping is a line action, place your Fair on a Hall line to pump → enshrine high in one activation.
  • Occupancy: a cell with a rival's worker isn't blocked — you take your tableau twin (sharpened by your installed working casks) instead. The more brewery you've built, the less a block stings.

12Goods & Storage

grain and hops are the only currency — spent on tiles, brewing inputs, tolls, and Fair pumps. You may hold up to 8 goods ⚙ (raised by the Warehouse room). Standing is never spent.

13End of the Game

There is no turn limit. Two clocks race; whichever fires first ends the game.

  • Primary — the Sailed-Ships track fills. Every ship that sails (§9) moves to the shared, visible Sailed-Ships track, one slot per voyage. When the track fills, the game ends. The track length is the master length dial: ~6 / 8 / 10 / 12 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 players ⚙. This clock is self-accelerating — the more the table ships, the sooner it ends — and smooth (a Hulk's 3-presence burst is still just one slot).
  • Backup — N casks enshrined total (player-scaled ⚙). Any enshrine advances it, so the game can't deadlock; with only 4 Floor slots (vessels clog), players are forced to cash out, so one clock always moves.

This is a visible, player-steerable clock: a reach player races to sail ships fast and end the game; a standing player keeps a steadier pace to buy more enshrining turns. When either trigger fires, finish the current round so all players have had an equal number of turns, then score.

City saturation is no longer an end trigger

Route caps remain only to clamp presence and settle majorities — filling a city no longer ends the game. The type frontier is a separate, non-ending clock: as a type's market saturates, its value-track marker ticks down and the next premium tier unlocks, pacing the value economy without ending it. (Optional flourish ⚙: milestone slots on the Sailed-Ships track advance the type frontier / refill the Market.)

14Scoring

  1. Reach — for each route: your presence (deployed casks) × that route's slotted value.
  2. Majorities — the route-majority bonus at each kontor (most presence; ties split ⚙).
  3. Standing — your standing track total (sum of market values banked at each enshrine).
  4. Goals — resolve your enshrined casks' face-up goals against final state; only your best 3 score.

Highest total wins. Tiebreak: most goods, then most deployed casks.

The goal pool — a 3-way cycle (⚙)

Goals are how the axes interact rather than run parallel. The pool is a symmetric cycle: 3 reach-rewarding · 3 standing-rewarding · 3 engine-rewarding, all capped — and crucially no goal rewards the axis the enshrine act already paid, so a one-note player's goals score little. That's what makes blending pay. Only your best 3 count. See the visualizer for the full list.

152-Player Variant

With fewer workers the grid feels loose, so lock some perimeter slots (down to ~6 ⚙) to tighten placement competition. The Sailed-Ships track is shortest at 2p (~6 slots ⚙), so the clock stays brisk; player-scaled route caps still clamp presence and majorities. Everything else plays identically.

16Glossary

  • Cell — one of the four shared action spaces (A/B/C/D).
  • Line — a row or column; a 4-stop pipeline.
  • Slot — a perimeter space holding one owned tile; belongs to one line only.
  • Cap — the slot tile at either end of an activated line.
  • Deployed / Enshrined — a cask in a shared slot (reach = your presence) vs. on the standing stack (standing).
  • Kontor — a foreign trading post (Bergen/London/Bruges/Novgorod), a reach destination. Hall — cell C, where casks are enshrined for standing.
  • Skim — goods collected by a slot tile's owner when its line fires.
  • Presence — your markers on a route = reach.
  • Standing — earned, unspendable reputation = score.
  • Recipe card — collected at the Market; pays a big one-time boon, then its brew strip tucks under your board as a permanent recipe (soft cap 6). You brew only types you hold a strip for; Gruit is always brewable.
  • Brewhouse Floor — your 4 multi-use tableau slots, each a Room (permanent) or a working Cask (temporary). Extra Vessel is a Room that consumes a slot.
  • Ship — a single-use carrier built at the Market, assigned to a kontor (capacity Cog 2 / Hulk 3). Loaded when its line fires; sails when full (casks become presence, owner banks a destination bonus), then moves to the Sailed-Ships track.
  • Sailed-Ships track — the shared end-game clock; each voyage fills one slot. When full → finish the round → score.
  • Type / type frontier — a global quality rung (Gruit→Hopped→summit); the frontier is how far up the ladder the Market currently sells, advancing as types saturate.
  • Demand market — each type's current value; an enshrine banks that value as standing. Drops as a type is realized; rises on recipe-buys / Fair pumps.
  • Fair — a slot tile; its line lets the active player pay to pump a type's value (fee to the owner).

17Quick Reference

Your turn

  1. Move worker to an orthogonally adjacent cell (mandatory).
  2. Activate its row or column.
  3. Resolve 4 stops (cap · cell · cell · cap), any order. Both cells fire; rival-occupied cell → tableau fallback; owned cap tiles skim to owner.

The loop

Market (goods/recipes/tiles) → Brewhouse (brew) → Harbor (deploy a cask to a slot = presence/reach) → Hall (enshrine a cask = standing). Deploying and enshrining both drop the type's market value; recipe-buys and Fairs raise it. Standing banked = the market value at enshrine.

Game ends

Sailed-Ships track fills (each voyage = 1 slot) OR N casks enshrined → finish the round → score: Reach (presence × route value), Majorities, Standing (track), Goals (best 3).
