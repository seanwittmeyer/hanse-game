## SYNTHESIS (drafted from the above; the full write-up is in the session report)
1. Bruges = tempo, Novgorod = the quality jackpot, Bergen = charters/reaches more than hulls, London = the authorship engine. The Hall = everyone's endgame accelerator.
2. The demand/privilege lane is the game's ceiling (Q5+die = 9–12 pt casks) and its bottleneck: it needs deploy-routing skill (humans have it, traders don't) AND tile survival (the overbuild churn threatens it for everyone).
3. Overbuild economy runs hot in long games: dev 33–45 pts/table at 3p when the deck churns; self-overbuild is a costless +3 pump; the Flight is the working counterweight (both spam engines — TraderV 3p-1, Guild2 4p-5 — lost to it).
4. Enshrine chains end most games; trader-heavy tables can burn the clock in 9 rounds (below band). The Hall throttle is the pace dial to watch.
5. Floor: designed as a toll-dodge/Wild engine (humans use it exactly so); for weak heuristics it's a do-nothing attractor (3 confirmed endgame paralysis cases). AI fix, not a rules fix.
6. Interaction is the healthiest system: rival-loading, dockside pickup, Bergen gifting, London showers all fire constantly at every skill level.
7. Known AI artifacts to discount: Keut monoculture, deploy-under-ship-target mis-routes, unused Quaymasters, storage-full stasis, MC hoard-then-burst arcs.
