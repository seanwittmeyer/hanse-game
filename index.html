§6The Shared Action Grid

A 2×2 of four action cells, shared by all players. On your turn: move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent cell, then activate its row or column — a 4-stop pipeline (cap · cell · cell · cap). Hover a cell to light its two lines.

Build × cash-out: Market & Brewhouse (builders) sit on one diagonal; Harbor & Hall (cash-outs) on the other — never sharing a line. Builders pump the demand market; cash-outs realize it and pull it down. Each turn you deploy for reach OR enshrine for standing, never both.

  • Both cells on an activated line fire — the worker only gates which line is legal.
  • Each of the 8 perimeter slots belongs to exactly one line (row xor column). Slots hold a churning mix of deployed casks (your reach), Fairs, route lanes and ships. (Recipes live in your private book, not a slot.)
  • On a Hall line, any player may enshrine a deployed cask — the owner banks standing, the slot frees. Casks churn; nobody hoards.
  • Cells are generalists (in two lines); slot tiles are specialists (one line). Placing a tile commits it to a row or a column.
  • A rival worker on a cell → you take your tableau fallback for it instead.
  • An owned cap tile fires its skim to its owner whenever its line runs — by anyone.

§19The Route Board — Reach & Variable Paths

Home port central, four routes radiating to the kontore. Casks drop presence at a destination (reach + majorities) — reaching Bruges by Harbor direct-deploy, but London / Bergen / Novgorod via a ship (or the Quay room). Each kontor rewards a different thing, so there is no single efficiency line. End-game reach = your presence × the route's slotted value.

Quality gates the destination: a route only accepts casks of sufficient quality — the Quality → Destination interlock that keeps the three scoring axes from becoming a point-salad.

§11The Player Board — Brewhouse Floor & Brewing Track (private tableau)

The Brewhouse Floor — 4 multi-use slots. Each Floor slot holds either a Room (permanent depth) or a working Cask (a temporary engine) — never a dedicated place for each. Scarcity + multi-use: every parked engine-cask is a room you didn't build, and vice versa. Vessels stay separate (start 1, cap 3), but Extra Vessel is a Room, so a third brewing lane costs a Floor slot — you can't build wide and deep at once.

Installing a Ready cask as working is free, but needs an open Floor slot. If the Floor is full, the Ready cask clogs its vessel until you deploy or enshrine something — so Floor scarcity and brewing back-pressure are one tension. Your collected recipe-card brew strips tuck under the board's bottom edge (a 6-guide row); the Floor is for rooms and working casks only.

The Brewing Track below is the vessel the brewing verb acts on — casks crawl through it. Gruit is fast but perishable (never enshrined); hopped & premium take the extra Age step but travel and earn standing. Gruit/hopped is a tempo decision.

ACask Tiles — the Dual-Role Hero

Double-sided, and it lives in one of three states: working (a personal slot — engine), deployed (a board slot — it is your reach/presence and fires an action), or enshrined (the Hall — standing, banked at the type's current market value). Enshrining converts a deployed cask: its presence leaves the board. Beer is two layers — type (a global quality rung) + recipe (a collected instance in your book). The ladder is an anchored spine (Gruit→Hopped) + a variable summit (L3–L5 dealt each game). Click a tile to flip it.

A′The Demand Market — the value-over-time signal

Each beer type sits on a shared value track, and a cask's enshrine payout = its type's value at that instant (banked on your standing track — this one number is the whole variable-value layer; no separate tokens). It drops −1 every time a type is realized — deployed for reach OR enshrined for standing — so the volume crowd erodes the prestige crowd's payout. It rises +1 when you buy that type's recipe or pay at a Fair. New tiers enter high, then erode. The whole reach-vs-standing tension becomes a question of timing — cash your type while it's high.

Saturation does double duty: as a type floods, its value drops and the type frontier advances — the next premium tier's recipes enter the Market. One causal loop: flood gruit → gruit cheapens → the summit opens → the market tempts you upward. (This is a pacing clock, not an end-game trigger — see the Sailed-Ships clock below for the v0.6 end trigger.)

FThe Sailed-Ships Track — End Clock

Primary trigger — the Sailed-Ships track fills. Each voyage consumes one ship onto a shared, visible track; the more the table ships, the sooner the game ends — a self-accelerating, self-balancing reach clock. Slot count is the master length dial: ~6 / 8 / 10 / 12 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5p ⚙ (a Hulk's 3-presence burst is still just one slot). Backup trigger — N casks enshrined total (player-scaled ⚙): any enshrine advances it, so with only 4 Floor slots forcing cash-outs, one clock always moves. Whichever fires first → finish the round → score; no turn limit. City saturation is no longer an end trigger — route caps remain only to clamp presence and settle majorities.

BRoute Tiles — perimeter infrastructure

Placed in a slot. Two jobs: raise that route's end-game value, and fire a shipping skim. Players author what scores by what they place.

CShip Tiles — single-use destination carriers

The reach engine. A ship is built at the Market into a perimeter slot, tied to a destination kontor, with a capacity and the route's quality gate. A clean load → fill → sail lifecycle: when the ship's line fires, anyone may load a Ready cask that meets the gate (the loader takes a small benefit; the cask's owner skims a good, toll-baron-style). The instant it is full it sails — every cask aboard drops as presence (reach) at the destination, the owner banks a per-kontor destination bonus, and the ship tile is consumed onto the shared Sailed-Ships track (build a new one to ship again). (Owner's relief valve: launch a partial ship early via a Harbor action.)

Ships reach the far kontore. Harbor direct-deploy reaches Bruges only; London / Bergen / Novgorod presence comes through a ship (the Quay room upgrades direct-deploy to any open route — the builder's alternative to the merchant's ships).

Destination bonuses ⚙ (owner, on delivery): Bruges +2 G · London a Privilege · Bergen a toll / majority help · Novgorod a presence-or-standing kicker (the biggest). Mostly engine fuel — the merchant converts skims + bonuses + delivered casks back into routes and ships, not a separate point pile.

C′Recipe Cards — dual-use, the Lisboa tuck

Recipes are now dual-use cards, acquired only at the Market. Each rhymes with the dual-role cask: a BIG one-time boon on collect (≈ a free action, scaling by tier) plus a permanent brew strip (its type + cost profile) that tucks under the bottom edge of the player board, Lisboa-style — your fanned row of strips is your recipe book, brewable forever. Collecting still pumps that type +1 on the demand market. Gruit is the printed baseline (always brewable); start with Gruit + 2 random premium cards.

Soft cap = 6 tuck guides (the board edge). Collecting a 7th forces you to discard a strip (lose that brewable type) — softened because you already banked the card's boon. Boons grant resources / tempo / small presence only — never standing or raw VP, so the cash-out cells keep their job.

DPrivilege Tiles — Steelyard rewards

Gained chiefly via the London route. Ongoing perks held by the player.

ERoom / Upgrade Tiles — tableau engine

Where depth lives — and they upgrade the blocked-cell trickle. Rooms now sit on the 4-slot Brewhouse Floor, shared with working casks: a room is a Floor slot you can't use for a working cask, and vice versa. Extra Vessel is a Room (costs a slot, unlocks a brewing lane); Quay upgrades Harbor direct-deploy to any open route (the builder's alternative to ships — Harbor is otherwise Bruges-only).

FThe Hall & Standing — retired: shared-stack actions

Deprecated in v0.5. The old "top-of-stack = a live action" mechanic is gone — the standing stack is now tactile sediment (colored edges to eyeball it), while the scoring-relevant value lives on each player's standing track and goals sit face-up in a personal row. Nothing is hidden in the pile. The ideas below are kept only as optional setup-bonus flavor. ⚙
