Brewhouse of the Hanse

Learn to play in one page — then go brew.
Components · Full Rulebook · Play

1The story

It's around 1350, and the Hanseatic League runs the trade of northern Europe. You've just learned to brew with hops — and unlike old gruit ale, hopped beer survives a sea voyage. For the first time, beer is something you can export.

You are a merchant brewing house: a family firm in a League city. You'll buy grain and hops, brew casks, ship them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts — the kontore at Bergen, London, Bruges, and Novgorod — and build a name for your house.

2Your goal — and the one big choice

You win with the most points, and there are two ways to earn them that pull against each other. Every cask you brew poses the same question:

Reach

Keep the cask working — ship it out and spread your presence across the trade routes. The path of scale & volume.
vs

Standing

Enshrine the cask at a kontor — take it out of commerce forever as pure reputation. The path of scarcity & prestige.

You can't do both with the same cask, and your space and time are tight — so the game is about how far you lean toward reach or standing (or thread between them). That single tension is the whole game.

3How you get there — the loop

Everything flows through four actions. Think of it as a little factory:

1

Market

Take grain & hops, or buy a tile (a recipe, a trade route, an upgrade).
→
2

Brewhouse

Load a recipe and brew it down your track: cheap gruit is fast; hopped beer takes longer but travels & can be enshrined.
→
3

Harbor

Ship a finished cask: it loads as cargo into a ship on a route (or sails on its own) and drops a presence marker. That's reach.
→
4

Kontor

Or enshrine a ready cask straight from your brewery: it goes to the reputation stack. That's standing.

The rhythm: the Market and Brewhouse build you up; the Harbor and Kontor cash you out. Acquire → brew → then either ship for reach or enshrine for standing.

One more thing — value & tokens. Every sale (ship or enshrine) also mints you VP tokens based on how valuable that beer type is right now. But each sale of a type nudges its value down — so selling a hot type early pays, and flooding it tanks it. Tokens are bonus points at game end. It's a gentle race layered over the big reach-vs-standing choice.

4The grid — where you act

All four actions sit on a shared 2×2 grid. On your turn you move your worker to a neighbouring cell, then fire that cell's row or column — which triggers both cells on that line. So a turn always does two things.

The two builders sit on one diagonal and the two cash-outs on the other, so they never share a line. The neat result: every line pairs one builder with one cash-out — so each turn you build and cash out, and you ship for reach or enshrine for standing, never both at once.

Around the grid are 8 slots, and you fill them with owned tiles — route lanes and ships. Each pays its owner a little skim when its line fires (even on someone else's turn): a lane pays goods, and a ship loads cargo — including casks rivals ship to its route, who then pay you a toll. Park a ship on a busy route and you tax the table; when it fills, it sails and frees the slot. (Recipes you buy don't go in a slot — they go straight into your private recipe book.)

5Your player board — the brewery

The grid is shared; your brewery board is yours alone. It's where brewing actually happens and where you specialise.

Brew & vessels

Casks crawl your track (Load→Ferment→Age→Ready). You start with one vessel and can build up to three — more vessels = more casks brewing at once.

Rooms & twins

Just four room slots force you to choose: go wide on vessels, or deep on upgrades. Rooms also power your twins — private versions of Market/Harbor/Brewhouse you fall back to when a rival blocks that cell. Build them up and a crowded cell becomes a chance, not a wall.

6Word primer

Hanse / KontorThe Hanseatic League — the medieval trade network. A kontor is one of its foreign trading posts (Bergen, London, Bruges, Novgorod).
Gruit vs. HoppedTwo beers. Gruit is cheap, fast, local — and can never be enshrined. Hopped beer takes longer to brew but travels and earns standing.
Goods grain and hops. Your only currency — spent on tiles and brewing. (There is no money in this game.)
CaskA brewed beer, shown as a double-sided tile: a cargo face (loaded into a ship, earning reach) and a standing face (enshrined, earning reputation).
LineA row or column of the grid. Activating one fires both its cells plus the slots at each end.
SlotOne of the 8 spaces around the grid, holding an owned tile — a ship or a route lane. A fired slot pays its owner a skim.
PresenceYour markers along a trade route. More presence on higher-value routes = more reach points.
StandingEarned, unspendable reputation from enshrined casks. It only ever goes up — it's score.
EnshrineMove a ready cask from your brewery onto the shared Kontor stack — taking it out of commerce for permanent standing (instead of shipping it for reach).
Route & quality gateThe four sea lanes. Richer, farther routes (like Novgorod) only accept higher-quality beer.
GoalA scoring condition printed on a cask's standing face — it rewards you at game end for the shape of your empire.
TwinThe private version of a grid action on your brewery board, used when a rival blocks the public cell.
Recipe bookYour private list of recipes. You can only brew a beer type you hold a recipe for; you collect more from the Market. Gruit is always in the book.
VP tokensBonus points minted on every sale, scaled by the beer type's current value (which falls as that type is sold). Worth 1 point each at game end.

That's the whole game. Ready?
