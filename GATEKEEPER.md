# Gatekeeper Review — v2.8 "Deploy First" (KEY v92)

*Standing review-board verdict, July 2026. Evidence: 15 narrated sim games (2–4p, trader/Guildmaster/Cellarmaster mixes) + 3 human games — see `playtests/logs/REVIEW-NOTES.md`. Focus per the design brief: slot fluidity (too crowded vs overbuild-too-fluid), the Floor as a potential alternative victory path, the wharf-line-vs-Floor decision (cf. Deep Regrets), option tension, AP and scoring complexity.*

---

## 1. The verdict

**Not yet — a conditional yes.** The core is real: the dual-role cask and the shared-hull logistics are a signature no comp owns, the interaction layer fires at every skill level, and loss is mostly legible (you can name the die you wasted, the tile you lost, the hull you gifted). But the playtests surface three structural leaks that a skeptical heavy-euro buyer would catch in two plays: **the slot economy oscillates between clogged and thrashing** (and takes the demand lane — the game's advertised ceiling — down with it), **the endgame degenerates into a Q3-enshrine treadmill** (an all-trader 4p table burned the clock in 9 rounds, below the pace band), and **the scoring stack runs five systems where three would do**. All three are fixable with structure levers, not value retunes. Fix them and this is a strong yes; ship today and it's a pass.

## 2. The panel

**The Critic — yes with reservations.** The cask's three lives (maturing → deployed → delivered) and "your rival can ship your barrel" earn the game's existence next to GWT and Distilled; nothing in either comp feels like watching your Bock get loaded toward the wrong port. The reservation: the demand lane is the stated payoff and it is structurally unreliable — value tiles die 1–2 rounds after placement in long games (3p-3, 3p-4), so the engine the box advertises mostly strands. And overbuild-for-+3 is dressed-up busywork scoring: 33–45 developer points per table is churn masquerading as development. *Flips to yes when: privileges live long enough to pay, and the wharf-vs-Floor choice becomes a real fork.*

**The Buyer — wait.** The hook is sellable in one sentence ("brew, age, and ship one barrel through a harbor your rivals also use"), and Hanseatic brewing is an ownable niche neither GWT's Western nor Distilled's spirits touches. But a con demo that ends in a Keut-enshrine race, or a 4p table with two seats taking null turns, kills word of mouth. *Flips to yes when: the teach lands in 20 minutes because scoring is three numbers, and the demo table's last round is a race to ship, not a race to opt out of shipping.*

**The Optimizer — no, today.** Legible loss: largely passes — the human logs show players routing dies deliberately and naming their mistakes. But three exploit lines are open: (1) **self-overbuild is a costless +3 pump** (the Cellarmaster chained it; Survey makes it free); (2) **zero-point deliveries** (Q2 → Novgorod = 0) are a legal clock-sealing tempo tool — "sell beer for nothing to end the game"; (3) **Q3 enshrine spam** is the fastest clock conversion in the game and compresses it below its own pace band. Rival-loading kingmaking is real but healthy — it's chosen, priced (1G), and visible. AP flag: the 3-line choice × up-to-6 optional stops × free resolution order is at the top of medium weight, and the churn makes board-reading worse — a board that thrashes every round must be re-read every round. *Flips to yes when: the three exploits close.*

**The Bridge Player — yes-ish.** Warm start, grey-not-hide UI, and the production line (Source→Brew→Age→Ship) teach themselves. Two first-game bruises: "my deployed cask got shipped somewhere dumb and apparently that's fine" needs one clean teach sentence, and the natural refuge for a struggling player — the Floor — is currently a trap for exactly the players who need it (three confirmed endgame-paralysis cases; the bots demonstrate what a confused human does with it). *Flips to yes when: "I'm behind, I go home and work my brewery" is a genuinely good turn.*

Panel spread: Optimizer-no with Critic/Bridge-yes is the "great bones, exploitable surface" pattern — a tuning problem, not an identity problem. That is the most fixable spread.

## 3. Strengths (mechanical, evidenced)

- **The interaction layer is the best subsystem in the game.** Rival-loading with destination choice, dockside pickup, Bergen Reach-gifting, London benefit showers — all fired constantly in 15/15 sim games and both human sessions, at every skill level. The kiln-lift Q5 that cashed *because a rival loaded it* (4p-5) is the game working exactly as designed: owner routes, rival triggers, both profit.
- **The Flight is the working counterweight to spam.** Both degenerate engines in the set — Keut monoculture (3p-1: most deliveries, lost by 15) and Hopped-spam-plus-churn (4p-5: 19 delivery points, dev 12, lost) — were beaten by breadth. A scoring rule that quietly polices monoculture without a ban is good design.
- **The ceiling payoff exists and is dramatic.** Bock Q5 = 12 at Novgorod (3p-4) — base 6 plus a maxed die — is the single-cask firework the design promises. Humans chase it naturally (Sean's Bock assembly line, three in flight at once).
- **The relief valves prevent deadlock.** Zero deadlocks in 15 games; Charter and Enshrine kept tight rings moving. (Their overuse is the problem — see below — but the valves themselves are load-bearing.)
- **Loss is legible.** Post-game, every sim loser's failure is nameable from the log (stranded Bock, gifted Bergen hull, monoculture flight-0), and the human games show players adjusting lines mid-game. This is the depth-pull foundation; protect it.

## 4. Weaknesses and cut corners

- **The slot economy has no prices, so it has no equilibrium.** Squatting is free (low-Q casks and dead Novgorod hulls sit for ~10 rounds; "no open slot to deploy onto" at 3p+) and eviction is free (Survey/London freebies force overbuilds; the displaced owner gets +3, the actor pays nothing; self-overbuild pays the actor). Result: crowded early, thrashing late — the same missing lever both times.
- **The demand lane's reliability is a corner cut.** The privilege → deploy → die → ship chain needs the tile to survive ~2–3 rounds and the owner to route correctly. Churn attacks the first; the sims show even correct routing dies to displacement. Humans execute the routing (it's not a comprehension problem) — the *board* betrays them.
- **The Hall is a treadmill, not a ladder.** Repeat enshrines at the same quality (two Mumme Q4=7s in one game; chains of Keut Q3=5) mean the "prestige" lane rewards repetition, and every enshrine ticks the shared clock — so the least interactive action in the game is also its fastest ending.
- **Developer points are a fifth scoring system nobody asked for.** They exist as displacement compensation, but at 25% of table scores (3p-3/3p-4) they distort behavior: players *want* to be displaced, and want to displace themselves.
- **Null turns exist.** All-optional stops mean a bad line can be six dead stops. The sims exaggerate it, but the human logs show "no Ready cask / nothing to load" beats too — the turn floor is too low for this weight class.

## 5. The depth dial

Too thin: the Floor (a consolation line, never a strategy — unique assets, no unique payoff). Too hot: the overbuild economy (fluid past the point of meaning) and the enshrine pace at trader-speed tables. Just right: the brewing ladder (hops-led Bock at 2G3H is reachable and humans live on it), the interaction layer, the Flight, the majorities. **Skill converts**: the Cellarmaster won 5 of 8 mixed games on breadth-plus-Hall; humans out-route every bot tier on the demand lane. Loss is legible today; the churn is the one thing eroding it — a player whose privilege died to a free Survey overbuild the turn before their ship came has a legitimate "the board randomed me" complaint.

## 6. The comp face-off

**vs Great Western Trail:** Hanse *wins* interaction decisively — GWT's deliveries are near-solitaire; Hanse's shared hulls make every sail multiplayer. Hanse *loses* board coherence: GWT's co-built track is *accretive* (buildings persist; the puzzle deepens), while Hanse's late-game wharf thrashes, so the "moving target" reads as noise instead of evolution. Engine-build arc: draw. Why keep Hanse and shelve GWT: the barrel-logistics fantasy and the interaction; no honest answer exists yet on late-game board texture — that's the fix list.

**vs Distilled:** Hanse *wins* depth already — coupled decisions, a starved goods economy, and real interaction versus Distilled's card-market variance. The five-alarm test (is Hanse less deep than Distilled?) passes comfortably. Hanse *loses* warmth and ritual: Distilled's batch-building has a tactile ceremony that Hanse's demand-die arithmetic doesn't match, and Distilled teaches faster. Theme fit: draw — both are fused, differently. Why keep Hanse: it's the one that rewards mastery.

**vs Deep Regrets** (named at runtime — not a weight comp, an architecture lesson; from model knowledge, online verification unavailable this session): Judson Cowan's push-your-luck fishing game runs on a short calendar of days where each day you commit **whole** — put out to sea (acquire: push-your-luck fishing, risk, regrets) or stay in port (convert: sell the catch, refit, shed regrets). Three things make that fork sing: **(1) both modes are always good** — port is never a wasted day because your hold is full of value that only port can convert; **(2) neglect accrues visibly** — fish *rot*, so the value of a port day rises every day you postpone it; the clock does the pressuring, not the rules text; **(3) the commitment is chunky and irreversible** — one decision per day, six-ish days, so each choice is a felt fraction of the game. That is the user's "too many good options" tension manufactured by structure: you always need both, you can only have one *now*, and waiting has a price you can see. The Floor currently has the chunkiness (a whole turn) but neither the exclusive value nor the mounting pressure — which is exactly why it reads as a dodge, not a destination.

## 7. Pull diagnosis

Intended engine: **depth** (the comps say so; the sim-gate discipline says so). Actual engine today: depth, *leaking toward variety* — the building-deck churn and export-draft swing (a Bock-less draft monocultured an entire table, 3p-5) inject variance the depth engine has to fight, and the strand-prone demand lane means the ceiling line is part coin-flip. If optimizers conclude the best lane is unreliable, they churn out, and retention quietly shifts onto the expansion spine — which exists (Specialty Beers, Jopenbier, Trade Roads) but should be the dessert, not the meal. The fixes below are precisely "defend the depth engine."

## 8. The path forward

### Must fix (shelf-readiness blockers)

**A. Price the slot economy — one lever, both failure modes.** Squatting and eviction both need a cost. Proposal set (test together): overbuild costs the actor **the tile price + 1G eviction fee**; **self-overbuild banks nothing** (the Wild flip is the compensation); **Survey/London freebies place only onto empty slots or the recipient's Floor** (a freebie should never be an eviction warrant). Expected result: tiles live 3+ rounds, dev points fall from ~25% of scores to a garnish, privileges cash, the late board becomes readable (AP drops with churn). *Test: re-run the 15-game matrix; privileges-cashed-per-authored should triple; dev points < 10% of table scores; 3p-3/3p-4-style carousels gone.*

**B. Make the Hall a true ladder — one rung per quality tier per player.** You enshrine your *masterwork*, not your fourth Keut. Kills Q3 spam (the second Keut enshrine banks nothing), restores the prestige lane's scarcity story, forces breadth (syncs with the Flight), and directly throttles the treadmill ending. *Test: all-trader 4p lands back in the 12–25 band; enshrines ≤ ~40% of clock ticks.*

**C. Deliveries must bank ≥ 1★.** A cask that would score zero can't be delivered. Closes the "sell beer for nothing to end the game" tempo exploit; costs nothing thematically (no kontor buys worthless beer). *Test: zero-point deliveries impossible; confirm no new deadlock at the Novgorod gate (Charter/Enshrine remain the outs).*

### Would elevate it above the comps

**D. Make the Floor the second pole — the Deep Regrets move.** The Floor already has the fork's chunkiness and the right assets (private: immune to toll, hijack, and overbuild; scales with held casks and flipped tiles; thematically "home" vs "harbor"). Give it the other two properties:
   - **Exclusive value — the Taproom.** A Floor-only outlet: *serve* a Ready cask at your own taproom for a small quality-scaled ★ value — below export value, **no clock tick**, no majority presence, but it counts for the Flight. This is the "my hull got hijacked, my slot got squatted — fine, I serve it at home" release the user identified, and it's the base-game-native version of what Trade Roads explored. It gives behind-players a floor without adding a scoring *category* (it banks plain delivery ★).
   - **Mounting pressure — freshness.** Low-quality beer sours: Gruit/Hopped (maybe Keut) must move — ship, serve, or enshrine — within N rounds of Ready or degrade. This is the rot clock that makes "when do I go home" a timing decision rather than a preference, *and* it independently fixes low-Q slot squatting (failure mode #1), *and* it is the single best theme-fusion candidate available: hops preserve — the historical reason hopped beer conquered the Hanse. The quality ladder becomes a preservation ladder; aging-as-value gets its dark twin.
   - Together these make wharf-vs-Floor the round-defining fork: wharf turns spend and score, Floor turns charge and serve, souring casks force the return trip in both directions. *Test: PERSONAS runs show a taproom-leaning lane winning 15–25% at 3–4p; human tables report the "I need both, I can only have one now" sentence unprompted.*
   - *The ambitious variant (flagged, not recommended yet):* move demand-die setting onto the Floor entirely (the die becomes the cask's story, set at home). It would solve privilege-stranding at the root but guts the living-slots keystone — a v3.0-scale rebuild. Park it; revisit only if A+D underdeliver.

**E. Retire developer points as a scoring line.** With A in place, displacement compensation can become goods + the Wild flip only (goods are the game's only currency — on theme). Scoring drops from five systems to four; delivery arithmetic (base + die) plus majorities plus Hall plus Flight is a clean teach. *Test: end-of-game score explanation fits on the player aid's existing space; teach time drops.*

### Nice to have

- **Stale-hull valve** for the Novgorod freeze (a hull unloaded for a full round may be re-commissioned to a new destination, original commissioner keeps nothing) — only if A+ freshness don't already thaw it.
- **Draft guarantee**: the export draft always includes at least one Q4+ recipe (prevents the Keut-monoculture table texture of 3p-5).
- **Turn-floor guarantee**: any line with zero firable stops lets you source 1G — never a literal null turn. (Cheap insurance for the Bridge Player; may be unnecessary once the Floor is good.)

### If Sean does only one thing

**A — price the overbuild churn.** It rescues the demand lane (the game's ceiling), stabilizes the late board (GWT face-off), shrinks a whole scoring system (E falls out of it), and reduces AP (a stable board reads once). Every other fix lands better on a board that holds still long enough to mean something.

### Disposition (v2.9 "Ground Rent", 2026-07-06 — designer-ruled)
- **A — shipped, modified:** ground rent = a flat `1 G` (no tile-price component); the displaced +3★
  moved to **end-game, per flipped building still on the floor** (shared 4 slots with Specialists;
  full → boxed, nothing banks) — this also **implements E** (developer points retired as an in-game
  line). Value 3★ kept for now; **2★ is the fallback dial** if the lane runs hot.
- **B — rejected:** the Hall stays repeatable (one-rung-per-tier read as merging it with the Flight;
  the Hall is the alternative path and should stay a competitive destination — values are the dial).
- **C — reframed as a bug and fixed:** every kontor has a ≥1★ value floor (Novgorod's Q-scale floors
  at 1); zero-point deliveries are impossible.
- **D — Taproom tabled** (conflicts with the Hall's and the Floor's identities); **freshness shipped
  as SPOILAGE:** a Ready Q4+ cask may displace a deployed Q1 (the stale ale is boxed) — buffs the
  climb, unclogs the low-Q squatters, keeps the thematic spoilage.

### What only the table can settle

Whether freshness reads as pressure or bookkeeping (the sim can't feel annoyance); whether one-rung-per-tier Hall feels fair at 2p (fewer casks to diversify with); whether the Taproom's no-clock-tick property makes turtling viable at 2p; whether the eviction fee makes the building market feel too illiquid in the first 3 rounds. These four go on the next human-playtest card.
