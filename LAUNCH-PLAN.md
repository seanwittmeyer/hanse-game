## 1 · Where the game actually sits (comps, honestly)

**Weight class:** medium euro, GWT/Distilled band (BGG weight ≈3.0–3.4 expected). 2–4p,
~19–23 rounds at strong play (corpus), realistically **75–120 min** at the table.

**The mechanical comp set a reviewer will name:**

| Comp | What they'll say it shares | What Hanse does differently |
|---|---|---|
| **Hansa Teutonica** (BGG top-150, Big Box 2022) | the Hanse itself; network/majority race | Hanse has *production* — HT is pure network; we brew the thing we ship |
| **Great Western Trail** | deliver-cycle engine; player-authored shared board | GWT deliveries are near-solitaire; Hanse's shared hulls make every sail multiplayer |
| **Distilled** ($549k KS, 8,060 backers; Cocktails exp. $631k/5,897) | craft-production fantasy; age-then-sell arc | Hanse is board-driven and openly interactive vs Distilled's card-market solitaire |
| **Brass: Birmingham** (#1 BGG all-time) | brown-industry theme redeemed by presentation; shared infrastructure | aspiration comp, not a peer — cite for the *presentation* playbook |
| **Concordia / Orléans** | the "elegant middle-weight with one big idea" lane | the dual-role cask is that one big idea |

**Lineage honesty (what a veteran reviewer will actually trace):** "your placed piece is an
action rivals can use" is a ~20-year euro lineage — **Caylus (2005) → Le Havre (2008) → GWT's
building tolls (2016)**, with Hansa Teutonica's displacement a structurally different cousin.
Claiming category novelty would be factually weak. What the research confirms **is** novel:
1. the **three-state consumable lifecycle** of one physical piece (private maturing asset →
   public contestable action → consumed on delivery) — the ancestors are all static fixtures;
2. the **rival-loads-your-cask inversion** (they pick the port, you bank the score, they take
   a fee) — sharper agency inversion than GWT's passive toll;
3. running the shared-piece mechanism on a **fully cashless goods economy** — none of the
   ancestors do.

That's the hook: *"Brew a barrel, park it on the wharf, and pray your rival ships it
somewhere good — your pieces are everyone's actions."* Market it as **the tightest synthesis
of the shared-action-piece lineage in years — now cashless, consumable, and Hanseatic** —
openly owning the Caylus/GWT/Hansa DNA. The lineage sits dormant (no notable 2023–26 KS euro
revived it), which is an opening without a hype tailwind to ride.

**Verified white space:** no Hanseatic-themed OR beer-themed game has crowdfunded above
~$20k in 2020–2026 (best-ever beer-euro KS: Beer Empire 2017, $19.7k; the one in-window
Hanse campaign, *Hanse: Kontoriate* 2024, raised €3.2k). Nearest new beer euro: *Fantasy
Brewers* (Schacht, Essen 2025) — fantasy-themed, mechanically unrelated. **Brewhouses would
be the first medium-heavy, premium-production, KS-scale entry in either category in six
years** — first-mover in a proven-adjacent niche (Distilled/Luthier prove craft-production
converts at $550–790k), not a crowded one.

**Positioning verdict:** the risk isn't derivativeness; it's the **"brown euro" execution
trap** — the research is unambiguous that the community punishes drab presentation of
historical-trade themes, not the themes themselves (Brass #1 all-time, Concordia ~#26 and a
2025 BGG Hall of Fame inductee, Nucleum, Kutná Hora, The White Castle all prove the lane
converts when the graphic identity is confident). Cautionary anchors: Distilled itself sits
at BGG #276 ("good, not huge"), and a trusted-but-niche heavy-euro name with no crossover
(a typical Lacerda campaign) predictably lands ~$385k — theme+art are what move us above
that band, not mechanics alone.

## 3 · The angle

**Campaign title-line:** *Brewhouses of the Hanse — brew, age, and ship your beer through a
harbor your rivals also work.*

**The three-beat pitch (page + video, in order):**
1. **The fantasy (theme):** 1350. Hopped beer just made ale seaworthy — the craft-brewery
   startup moment of the medieval world. You run a house, not a spreadsheet: kettle → cellar
   → wharf → the kontor cities. (Craft-production fantasy = the Distilled/Luthier lane, the
   strongest-converting niche in the current euro market.)
2. **The hook (mechanism):** *your barrel is a piece everyone can play.* Deploy a cask and
   it's cargo, action, and bait at once; rivals can ship it — you score, they steer. Shared
   ships, no money, everything on the table. One line of rules text, endless table talk.
3. **The reassurance (trust):** two fulfilled campaigns, on-time fulfillment record, 5-tier
   in-game AI already built (the automa story writes itself), a free browser demo
   (`play.html`) linked from the page — *try the whole game before you pledge* (almost no
   euro campaign offers this; it's our cheapest conversion asset).

**Name check:** keep "Brewhouses of the Hanse" but buy the search field: an existing indie
"BrewHouse" (Rascal Games) owns the naive query, and the "Hansa/Hanse" namespace is crowded
(Hansa Teutonica is the default benchmark; a 2024 "Hansa Guilds" exists). Mitigations: file
intent-to-use trademark pre-launch, own "brewhouses" (plural) + "Hanse game" SEO, and lean
the logo/key art on the cask-and-cog imagery so the thumbnail is unmistakable. Adjacent
benefit: Hansa Teutonica's audience is *exactly* our audience — being benchmarked against a
17-year-old classic with zero production values is a fight our art budget wins.

## 4 · Art direction — the Bosley/O'Toole question

The research says the two named artists monetize **different audiences**: Bosley = cozy
crossover breadth (Everdell 13.5× goal), O'Toole = heavy-euro collector velocity (On Mars
$100k in 12.5 min). Hanse is **medium** weight — and the best-performing mid-weight
historical euros (Brass, White Castle, Kutná Hora, Nucleum) won on a third register:
**confident, legible, craftsmanship-forward** — neither cute nor Lacerda-ornate.

**Recommendation: hire toward the warm-craftsman register with O'Toole-grade information
design.**
- If **Bosley**: brief him *away* from critters toward his warm-light architectural work —
  tavern glow, harbor dusk, the brewhouse interior. The cozy tag ("cozy" is now a live 2026
  marketing descriptor) widens the funnel to the Everdell/Distilled crossover crowd without
  costing euro credibility — *provided* the graphic layer stays crisp.
- If **O'Toole**: the game's icon-first component grammar (one-read dice, printed this-or-
  thats, the Three Coins board) is squarely his infographic-luxe strength; brief warmth INTO
  the palette (amber/copper/foam, not slate/navy) so it doesn't read Lacerda-heavy and scare
  the medium-weight buyer.
- Either way: **name the artist in the campaign title-line** (the proven O'Toole/Eagle-
  Gryphon move), commission 4 kontor cityscapes + 10 cask/beer portraits as the ad-creative
  library, and spend real money on the **rulebook** — rulebook quality is a named gatekeeper
  kill-criterion for KS euros.

**Table presence plan:** the wharf cross + hull-as-carrier (cask cubes + demand dice riding
printed berth wells) is the overhead-shot money piece. Deluxe tier: screen-printed wooden
casks, metal contract seals, dual-layer player rows. The demand die *in* the ship well
photographs like Brass's canal tiles — build the key visual around a loaded Hulk.

## 6 · Funnel & campaign mechanics

- **Pre-launch (4–6 months):** Meta ads → KS pre-launch page direct (≈$3/follower — half the
  cost of landing-page routing); $1 reservation funnel (35–45% deposit→backer; Kelp hit 54%);
  goal ≥15k emails + 8–10k followers before launch. BGG: claim the game page early, fill the
  **Crowdfunding Countdown** fields (it's a free automatic placement — confirm with BGG ads
  whether a paid featured tier exists), run BGG banners at launch (BGG's audience *is* the
  euro buyer; historical publisher ROI ~2.5–3.7×).
- **Launch:** 30 days, Tue 9am ET. Everything embargoed to hour zero — previews, how-to-play,
  the browser demo, first gatekeeper reviews if earned. Expect ~35% of raise in the first
  3 days, ~45% in the last 3; program the mid-campaign trough (automa reveal, kontor art
  drops, expansion-toggle previews — Specialty Beers/Jopenbier/Trade Roads are *already
  designed and simulated*, which is an absurdly strong mid-campaign content bank).
- **Platform:** Kickstarter for reach (our list lives there via PDX/RDJ), **BackerKit for
  pledge manager + late pledge** (platform data says late-pledge/PM dollars now rival
  campaign dollars in aggregate). Charge real shipping at the PM, estimates only on the page.
- **Pricing sanity:** $69 core AOV target with deluxe pulling blended AOV toward $85–90 —
  right in the comp band ($66–$100). MSRP story: $79 retail core so the KS price reads fair.

## 7 · Risks the plan must carry

1. **Tariff climate (live, unresolved).** The US Section 122 10% surcharge sunsets 2026-07-24
   (ten days from this writing) with replacement tracks in play; 2025 peaked at 104% on China
   manufacturing. Plan: quote CN + VN/EU dual-source, price the pledge with a 15% landed-cost
   buffer, communicate the Cephalofair way (dedicated tariff updates), never surcharge
   mid-campaign — surcharge at PM only if forced.
2. **Category softness at the premium end.** KS tabletop is 3 years declining; Gamefound grew
   +19% and just bought Indiegogo — revisit platform choice 90 days out if Gamefound's euro
   cohort keeps compounding.
3. **The "brown euro" execution trap.** The theme is an asset only above a quality line;
   below it, we're "another medieval trade game" next to a 17-year-old classic. The art
   budget is not a place to save money. (See §4.)
4. **Balance/finishing risk — see the gameplay analysis.** The corpus verdict is that the
   engine is sound and the strategy ceiling is real, but the launch assumption "refined
   balance" still has open items (2p pace drift toward the round ceiling; Hall-coin
   dominance in strong play; specialists' pick-rate; the null-ish turn floor for trailing
   players). None are structural; all must close before the preview wave, because GWT-weight
   reviewers find exactly these seams in two plays.
5. **Name collisions** ("BrewHouse", the Hansa shelf) — handled by trademark filing + key-art
   distinctiveness (§3), but monitor.
