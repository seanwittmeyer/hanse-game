1The Brewing House

Hamburg was called the brewhouse of the Hanse. You are a merchant brewing house in the League, c. 1350.

The pivotal innovation of your age is hopped beer. Unlike perishable gruit ale, hopped beer survives a sea voyage — so for the first time, beer becomes a shippable export. You will source grain and hops, brew, ship casks across the Baltic and North Sea, and build standing at the great foreign trading posts (the kontore): Bergen, London, Bruges, and Novgorod.

Every cask poses one question, again and again: do you keep it working in commerce for reach, or take it out of commerce to become heritage and pure reputation? That single tension is the whole game.

2The Core Idea — Reach vs. Standing

There are two kinds of value, and they never convert into one another:

  • REACH — volume and presence. A ready cask shipped spreads your presence across the trade routes (and loads as cargo into ships you and rivals run). This is the path of scale.
  • STANDING — scarcity and reputation. A ready cask enshrined at a kontor is taken out of commerce forever, becoming unspendable reputation. This is the path of prestige.

Both score. They pull against each other because your ships' holds, brewing vessels, and turns are all scarce — and the same cask can ship or enshrine, never both. Leaning is the game.

Three design constraints

No dice (fully deterministic) · No cards (all content is tiles; acquisition is from a face-up market, never a hidden draw) · No money (goods are the only currency; standing is never spent).

3Components

  • 1 Main board — the 2×2 action grid (A Market · B Harbor · C Kontor · D Brewhouse), 8 perimeter slots, and the Kontor stack area.
  • 1 Route board — home port and four routes to the kontore, with presence spaces, route-value tracks, and majority tracks.
  • 5 Player tableaus — each a private brewery: the brewing track (Load · Ferment · Age · Ready), vessel lanes, room slots, the recipe book, the VP-token bin, and printed starting Larder + Quay.
  • 1 Type value track — a marker per beer type; every sale mints VP tokens off it, then it ticks down.
  • Goods cubes — grain (G) and hops (H), the only currency.
  • VP tokens — the spendable 3rd resource, minted on sales (§9–10), banked 1 VP each at game end.
  • The tile deck (~150 tiles, ~49 unique designs — see the visualizer):
    • Cask tiles — the dual-role hero (cargo ↔ standing).
    • Route, Ship, Recipe, Privilege, Room, and public Kontor tiles.
  • Per player: 1 worker pawn, presence markers, standing markers.
  • 1 first-player marker.

4Anatomy of the Game

The Action Grid

Four action cells in a 2×2: A Market & B Harbor (top row), C Kontor & D Brewhouse (bottom row). The two builders (Market, Brewhouse) sit on one diagonal and the two cash-outs (Harbor, Kontor) on the other, so they never share a line — every line pairs one builder with one cash-out. Each cell belongs to one row and one column — a line. Around the grid sit 8 perimeter slots, two capping each line.

The Lines & Slots

A line is a 4-stop pipeline: cap slot · cell · cell · cap slot. Each perimeter slot belongs to exactly one line — a row xor a column. Cells are generalists (each sits in two lines); slot tiles are specialists (each fires only when its one line runs).

The Route Board (Reach)

Home port at center; four routes radiate to the kontore. Shipping places your presence along a route. Each kontor rewards differently, so there is no single best line of play:

The Kontor Stack (Standing)

A single shared stack of tiles, mixing public Kontor tiles and players' enshrined casks. The top tile defines the current Kontor action; enshrining a cask places it on top, burying the previous.

Your Tableau (the Brewery)

Not a copy of the grid — it is the vessel the brewing happens in. The brewing track carries casks Load → Ferment → Age → Ready; room slots hold installed upgrades; the Larder and Quay provide the fallback trickle when the public board is crowded.

5Setup

  1. Place the main board and route board. Leave all 8 perimeter slots empty. Bruges begins open; the other three routes are closed.
  2. Seed the Kontor stack with 2 public Kontor tiles ⚙.
  3. Build the face-up Market display ⚙ from the tile supply (a row of available tiles, refilled as taken).
  4. Set the type value track markers to their starting values, and the type frontier at Hopped (only Gruit + Hopped recipes are available at the Market to start; the premium summit unlocks as types saturate). Deal the summit roster (Bock / Mumme / Broyhan / Keut) into the L3–L5 rungs in a random order ⚙.
  5. Each player takes a tableau, a worker, presence + standing markers, 0 VP tokens, and a starting stake: 3 , 2 , and the Gruit baseline + 2 random premium recipes in their recipe book ⚙. One vessel lane is open.
  6. Each player places their worker on any cell. Give the youngest brewer the first-player marker.

6A Turn

On your turn, in order:

  1. Move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent cell (A↔B, A↔C, B↔D, C↔D — never the diagonal). This is mandatory.
  2. Activate the row OR the column of your worker's new cell.
  3. Resolve the line's up-to-4 stops — cap · cell · cell · cap — in any order:
    • Each cell: take its public action. If a rival worker occupies that cell, you instead take your private tableau fallback for that action.
    • Each cap slot: if it holds an owned tile, that tile fires its line action and its owner collects the skim — no matter who activated the line.

Both cells always fire

Activating a line triggers both of its cells. Your worker's position only determines which line is legal and which cells are blocked to you (occupied by rivals). A turn therefore resolves up to two cell actions and two slot skims.

Play passes clockwise. When every player has taken a turn, the round ends and the first-player marker passes left.

8Brewing

Casks are brewed, not bought. Beer has two layers: a type (a global quality rung) and a recipe (a collected instance of a type, kept in your private recipe book, with its own cost profile). You cannot brew a type you hold no recipe for — Gruit is the universal baseline. A recipe loaded onto your track crawls through it; the Brewhouse action advances all your brews at once, so more vessels = more throughput.

The type ladder is an anchored spine + a variable summit. L1 Gruit → L2 Hopped is fixed and historical — hops is the preservation tech that opens the sea routes. The premium tiers L3–L5 are dealt each game from the historical Hanse export beers — Bock (Einbeck), Mumme (Braunschweig), Broyhan (Hannover), Keut (Low Countries) — so the efficient climb differs every game. The Market only sells recipes for currently-unlocked types: the type frontier advances as a type's market saturates (its value drops and the next tier opens — one causal loop).

Gruit vs. hopped is a tempo decision

Gruit skips the Age step — fast and cheap, frees the vessel quickly — but it is perishable: it can only reach Bruges and can never be enshrined. Hopped beer and above take the extra Age step (tying up a vessel longer), but they travel far and can become standing. That single fact — perishable never becomes heritage — is the heart of the theme.

Value & VP tokens

Each type sits on a value track. Every sale — whether you ship (reach) or enshrine (standing) — mints VP tokens equal to that type's current value, and then that type's value ticks down (the market saturates). So the first to sell a hot type cashes in high; floods tank it. VP tokens are a spendable 3rd resource (spend sinks ⚙) or banked 1 VP each at game end. Tokens are a modifier on top of a cask's printed value — they never replace it, so they reward timing without destabilizing the two real paths. Both reach and standing mint them, so the value race rewards either archetype.

9Shipping & Reach

  1. At the Harbor, take one Ready cask off your track.
  2. Place 1 presence marker on a route whose quality gate the cask meets, and mint VP tokens for the sale (the cask's type value).
  3. The cask becomes cargo: if a ship supporting that route has room, it loads aboard (preferred); a rival's ship collects a 1 toll. When a ship is full it sails (the owner takes a dividend; its slot frees). If no ship has room, it's a basic shipment — you still place your presence; the cask is simply delivered. (Casks are cargo, never slot tiles — ships are the booster, not a gate.)

Presence is clamped to each route's capacity (player-scaled — see §13, End of the Game). A route is only open if a Route Lane tile for it sits in a slot (Bruges excepted — always open). A route's end-game value is the sum of the Lane tiles players have slotted onto it — so players collectively author what scores. Most presence on a route claims its majority.

10Enshrining & Standing

To enshrine, your worker must activate a line containing the Kontor (cell C): the bottom row (Kontor + Brewhouse) or the left column (Market + Kontor). Then:

  • Take a ready cask straight from your brewery and place it on the top of the Kontor stack, flipped to its standing face.
  • That cask is now out of commerce — it could have shipped for reach, but you took the standing instead. You bank its standing value, mint VP tokens for the sale, its goal becomes live for end-game scoring, and it sets the new top-of-stack action.

The spine of the game

Enshrining is a triple choice on one tile: bank points now, commit to a scoring direction (the goal), and reshape the shared Kontor action for whoever banks next. The Harbor (ship → reach) and the Kontor (enshrine → standing) sit on opposite diagonals and never share a line, so each turn you ship or enshrine, never both — that's the reach-vs-standing fork, structural and per-turn. Reach (presence) and standing (enshrined casks) accumulate separately and never convert.

11Slots, Skims & Tolls

  • The 8 perimeter slots hold a churning mix of owned tiles — Ships (route-bound cargo containers that fill then sail away) and Route Lanes (permanent value). They compete for the same space — every slot is a row-xor-column commitment. (Recipes are not slot tiles — they go to your private book; casks are cargo inside ships, not slot tiles either.)
  • An owned slot tile fires its line action whenever its line is activated by anyone; the owner collects (the skim). Parking a tile on a high-traffic line taxes every rival who runs it.
  • Occupancy: a cell with a rival's worker is not blocked outright — you simply take your tableau fallback for that action instead. The more brewery you have built, the less a block stings.

12Goods & Storage

grain and hops are the only currency — spent on tiles, brewing inputs, and any tolls. You may hold up to 8 goods ⚙ (raised by the Warehouse room). Standing is never spent. VP tokens (§8) are a separate spendable resource minted by sales — not goods, and not subject to the storage cap.

13End of the Game

The game enters its final round the moment 2 of the 4 kontor cities are saturated — a city is saturated when its route's presence spaces are full. Route capacities are player-scaled ⚙: base {Bruges 2 · London 1 · Bergen 0 · Novgorod 2} + player count (so at 2p they are 4 / 3 / 2 / 4).

This is a visible, player-steerable clock: a reach player races to slam a second city full and end the game; a standing player, wanting more turns to enshrine, delivers to the empty cities to keep them from filling. Finish the current round so all players have had an equal number of turns, then score.

The value clock (separate)

The type frontier is a second, non-ending clock: as a type's market saturates, its value-track marker ticks down and the next premium tier unlocks. It paces the value economy through the game; it does not end it.

14Scoring

  1. Reach / Destination — for each route: your presence × that route's slotted value.
  2. Majorities — the route-majority bonus at each kontor (most presence; ties split ⚙).
  3. Standing — the sum of your enshrined casks' standing values.
  4. Goals — resolve each enshrined cask's goal against the final board state; only your best 3 score.
  5. VP tokens — +1 VP per banked token (the value-economy layer, minted by both paths through the game).

Highest total wins. Tiebreak: most goods, then most casks in transit (cargo loaded in ships).

The goal pool — a 3-way cycle (⚙)

Goals are how the axes interact rather than run parallel. The pool is a symmetric cycle: 3 reach-rewarding · 3 standing-rewarding · 3 engine-rewarding, all capped — and crucially no goal rewards the axis the enshrine act already paid, so a one-note player's goals score little. That's what makes blending pay. Only your best 3 count. See the visualizer for the full list.

152-Player Variant

With fewer workers the grid feels loose, so lock some perimeter slots (down to ~6 ⚙) to tighten placement competition. The player-scaled route caps already tighten the 2-of-4-cities clock at 2p (4 / 3 / 2 / 4). Everything else plays identically.

16Glossary

  • Cell — one of the four shared action spaces (A/B/C/D).
  • Line — a row or column; a 4-stop pipeline.
  • Slot — a perimeter space holding one owned tile; belongs to one line only.
  • Cap — the slot tile at either end of an activated line.
  • In transit / Enshrined — a cask loaded as cargo in a ship (reach) vs. on the Kontor stack (standing).
  • Kontor — a foreign trading post; also the shared score-stack cell.
  • Skim — goods collected by a slot tile's owner when its line fires.
  • Presence — your markers on a route = reach.
  • Standing — earned, unspendable reputation = score.
  • Recipe book — your private, permanent collection of recipes; you brew only types you hold a recipe for.
  • Type / type frontier — a global quality rung (Gruit→Hopped→summit); the frontier is how far up the ladder the Market currently sells, advancing as types saturate.
  • Value track / VP token — each type's current value; every sale mints VP tokens off it (then it decays). Tokens bank 1 VP each.

17Quick Reference

Your turn

  1. Move worker to an orthogonally adjacent cell (mandatory).
  2. Activate its row or column.
  3. Resolve 4 stops (cap · cell · cell · cap), any order. Both cells fire; rival-occupied cell → tableau fallback; owned cap tiles skim to owner.

The loop

Market (get goods/recipes/tiles) → Brewhouse (advance/load brews) → Harbor (ship: presence + tokens = reach) → Kontor (enshrine: standing + tokens). Every sale mints VP tokens off the type's value track, then that value decays.

Game ends

2 of the 4 cities saturated → finish the round → score: Reach × value, Majorities, Standing, Goals (best 3), VP tokens (+1 each).
