Brewhouses of the Hanse

A theme-first medium euro. Walk one loop — Source → Brew → Age → Ship — on a shared grid. A brewed cask matures in your brewery, sits on the shared wharf as a public action, then ships to a destination for points. The kontore pay volume; the Hall pays prestige.

§1The Shared Action Grid — the Loop

A 2×2 of four action cells, shared by all players. On your turn: move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent cell, then activate its row or column — a 4-stop pipeline (cap · cell · cell · cap). Hover a cell to light its two lines.

The four cells ARE the loop, and the clockwise circuit walks it: A Market (Source) → B Brewhouse (Brew) → D Cellar (Age) → C Harbor (Ship) → repeat. Every line is two adjacent loop-steps, so each activation is a useful segment. Cells are never blocked — interaction lives on the wharf, not the cells.

  • Both cells on an activated line fire — the worker only gates which line is legal.
  • Lines pair two loop-steps: Market+Brewhouse Brewhouse+Cellar Harbor+Cellar Market+Harbor.
  • The 8 perimeter slots = the wharf. They hold a churning mix of deployed casks (your reach + a public action), owned ships, and a few neutral buildings seeded at setup. (Recipes & upgrades live on your private board, not a slot.)
  • One fire rule: when a line runs, the active player may use each building on it — a cask's action, a ship's load, or a neutral building. All on your turn (no out-of-turn skims).
  • Base verbs always work; wharf buildings only add — so a busy ring makes your turns bigger and you're never stalled.

§4The Wharf — the Perimeter Ring

The 8 shared slots are the only contested space in the game. A Ready cask deploys here (you pick the slot) and becomes three things at once — your cargo-in-waiting, your private inventory, and a public action-building. Ships you build sit here too, and sail away when full. The transience is the churn: casks arrive Ready and leave when shipped; ships fill and sail.

Non-destructive interaction lives here. On your turn you may load a rival's wharf cask onto your ship — they still score the delivery and pick its benefit, but on your timing, and you take the freed slot + the sailing. Your private brewery is never touchable. A 1350 brewmaster doesn't fully control where their casks end up.

§6The Brewery — Vessels, Maturation & Upgrades (private, untouchable)

The grid is the verbs; your brewery is the noun they act on — your kettles and cellar. It holds your vessels (start 2, cap 4 — brewing throughput), the maturation track each cask crawls, your recipes, and your upgrades. Nothing here is contestable.

Maturation length is set by type — Gruit 1 · Hopped 2 · export 2–3 steps ⚙. When a cask reaches Ready it deploys to an open wharf slot; if the wharf is full it clogs its vessel until you free a slot (the back-pressure that keeps the loop moving). (Warm start: each house begins with 2 vessels, one built Cog bound to Bruges, and one Ready Gruit in a vessel (deploy it turn 1).)

ACask Tiles — the Dual-Role Hero

The one piece that does everything, living three lives: maturing (in a private vessel) → on the wharf (a shared slot — cargo + a public action) → delivered (shipped to a destination → it scores and leaves). Beer is two layers — type (a global quality rung, Q1–Q5) + recipe (permission to brew it). The ladder is an anchored spine (Gruit → Hopped) + a variable export (L3–L5 dealt each game). The signature action is what makes choosing a type an engine choice, not just a cost — you're deciding which action-building to put on your circuit. Higher types carry stronger actions (the climb).

FDestinations — Where You Ship Is the Lean

The two old value tracks are gone; where you ship is the volume-vs-prestige choice. Every destination is open from the start and differentiated by a quality gate (the only climb-limiter — better places want better beer) and a unique benefit. The cask's owner always scores the delivery and picks any offered benefit — even when a rival shipped it.

Reach = your delivered casks across the kontore (value + majorities — the volume lean). Prestige = casks shipped to the Hall (the scarcity lean). They never convert; you lean, reading the board. Delivering to London / Bergen / Novgorod lets you take an Upgrade from the Market's face-up display (free) — so shipping makes you stronger, closing the loop deliver → upgrade → brew better → deliver better. You don't fully know which upgrades will be on offer when your cask arrives.

BShip Tiles — Owned Single-Use Carriers

The merchant fantasy. You build and own ships (at the Market) into a wharf slot, bound to a destination (inheriting its quality gate). Lifecycle load → fill → sail: load via the Harbor verb (always) or a bonus free load when the ship's line fires; the instant it is full it sails — every cask aboard is delivered, and the ship is consumed onto the Sailed-Ships track (the end clock). Build another to ship again.

You may load your own Ready casks or a rival's wharf cask aboard your ship. Relief valves: launch a partial ship early, or Charter — pay 2 G ⚙ to ship one Ready cask (vessel or wharf) on an immediate single-cask voyage (no tile, an outside hull). The Charter advances the clock like any voyage but is strictly worse per cask than owning a Cog/Hulk, so it never displaces the fleet; it just keeps the tight ring from deadlocking. A full Hulk is a 3-cask burst — still just one Sailed-Ships slot, so the clock stays smooth.

CNeutral Buildings — the Shared Base

A small set placed in perimeter slots at setup (shared, permanent — 2–3 in play per game ⚙). They keep the wharf alive from turn 1: whoever runs their line may use the action, free. This is the “everyone has the base; your casks & ships are the better versions” floor.

DRecipe Tiles — Permission to Brew a Type

A small face-up Market supply. A recipe is only a brewable type + its brew cost — the v0.6 on-collect boons, the 6-card tuck, and frontier-gating are cut. Acquired at the Market; permanent (brewing never consumes it). Start with Gruit + Hopped (fixed, symmetric — so gaining a export recipe means more); collect export recipes to climb to the rich destinations.

EUpgrade Tiles — the Private Brewery Engine

Where depth lives — installed on your brewery board. Rooms are permanent capability; Modifiers are asymmetric perks (these subsume v0.6's Privileges). They sit in a face-up Market display (option B): buy one (pay its cost) or earn one free by delivering to London / Bergen / Novgorod — the same display, so what you can grab depends on what is face-up. Recipes & ships are a separate always-available supply.

GGoal Tiles — the Variety Layer

A small pool (shared draft or per-player ⚙) rewarding different shapes, so the winning engine varies by game — the Great Western Trail blend ideal. Only your best few score ⚙, and they're authored so a one-note rush fills few of them.

§8The Sailed-Ships Track — End Clock & Scoring

End: each voyage — a ship sailing or a Charter — moves one slot onto the shared, visible Sailed-Ships track; when it fills, finish the round → score. Self-accelerating (the more the table ships, the sooner it ends), the master length dial — it scales steeply (the clock fills faster with more players): ~7 / 11 / 15 / 19 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 players ⚙ (≈ +4 per player), with a MAX_ROUND ceiling (≈25 ⚙) backstopping the ~12–25-round target. A volume player can race the clock; a prestige player keeps a steadier pace to buy more delivering turns.

1 · Deliveries

Sum your delivered casks' values by destination — kontore trade value + the Hall's prestige (∝ quality).

2 · Majorities

The route-majority bonus at each kontor (most delivered casks; ties split ⚙). Bergen rewards this most.

3 · Goals

Your best few goal tiles ⚙. Tiebreak: most goods, then most casks on the wharf.
No separate reach/standing tracks — the volume-vs-prestige tension lives entirely as a lean across destinations, coupled by shared casks, scarce wharf slots, scarce ships, and the one shared clock.
