1The Brewing House

Hamburg was called the brewhouse of the Hanse. You are a merchant brewing house in the League, c. 1350.

The pivotal innovation of your age is hopped beer. Unlike perishable gruit ale, hopped beer survives a sea voyage — so beer becomes a shippable export. You will source grain and hops, brew casks, mature them, and push them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts (the kontore: Bergen, London, Bruges, Novgorod) — or withdraw your finest into the brewers' Hall. Whoever has shipped the most valuable casks when the last ship sails wins.

Source → Brew → Age → Ship → (repeat)

  • You brew casks, mature them, and ship them to destinations for points. Where you ship is the key decision: VOLUME the kontore (value + majorities) vs PRESTIGE the Hall (quality, out of commerce).
  • Both stations on an activated line fire — the worker only gates which line is legal. (Hover a station to light its two lines.)
  • Lines pair two stations: Market+Brewhouse Brewhouse+Cellar Harbor+Cellar Market+Harbor.
  • The 8 slots hold a churning mix of deployed casks (cargo + a public action), neutral ships, and 2–3 neutral buildings. Base actions always work; slot buildings only add.

Three rules constraints

No dice (fully deterministic) · No hidden draws (everything you acquire is taken from a face-up supply) · No money (goods are the only currency; delivered points are score only).

3Components

  • 1 Main board — the Wharf: a 2×2 of four stations (A Market · B Brewhouse · D Cellar · C Harbor) ringed by 8 slots, plus the shared Sailed-Ships track (the end clock).
  • 1 Destination board — the four kontore + the Hall, each with its quality gate, delivery value, benefit, and (kontore) a majority track (§4, §10).
  • 5 Player brewery boards — each private & untouchable: vessel lanes (start 2, cap 4), the maturation track, a recipe rack, upgrade slots, and goods storage (cap 8).
  • The tile supply — cask tiles (a shared pool with fixed counts; an ownership disc marks one on a slot or delivered), ship tiles (the neutral ship deck — Cog · Hulk with destinations printed), neutral building tiles, recipe tiles, upgrade tiles (Rooms + Modifiers), and goal tiles. Each family is shown in its section below.
  • Goods cubes — grain (G) and hops (H), the only currency. Per player: 1 worker, ownership discs. 1 first-player marker (fixed — it does not pass).

4Anatomy of the Game

The Stations & Lines

Four stations sit in a 2×2: A Market (Source) & B Brewhouse (Brew) on top, C Harbor (Ship) & D Cellar (Age) below. You don't simply circle the board — you move where the board is best. Eight slots ring the stations, two beside each line. A line is its two stations plus any tiles in their two slots (slot · station · station · slot); each slot belongs to exactly one line. (Recipes & upgrades live on your private board, not a slot.)

The Destinations (where value lives)

The four kontore + the Hall are all open from the start. Each wants a certain quality of beer and pays differently — so there is no single best line of play. Delivery value is flat per kontor for Q1–Q3, and an export premium ⚙ adds +1 for a Q4 cask, +2 for a Q5, at any kontor (the Hall scales with quality throughout) — so the high climb pays wherever it ships.

The majority is a big end-game motivator — every kontor pays a tiered, ranked majority (Lacerda-style): by delivered-cask count, 1st / 2nd / 3rd place take a kontor's tiers — Bruges 5/3/0 · London 6/4/2 · Novgorod 8/5/2 · Bergen 10/6/3 ⚙ (Bergen the rich anchor). Per-cask delivery values are kept low so the points live in the majority race; the Hall's prestige is Q×2.5 to balance the tilt toward the kontore. 2-player games skip 2nd (winner-take-all); ties split the occupied tiers. Benefits pair off: Bruges & Bergen give goods (liquidity); London & Novgorod give an Upgrade (the engine). The cask's owner always scores the delivery and takes any benefit.

Your Brewery (private, untouchable)

Your brewery board holds the maturation track (casks crawl Brew → … → Ready across your vessels — start 2, cap 4, each a parallel lane), a recipe rack, and upgrade slots (Rooms & Modifiers, mostly earned by delivering). Rivals can only act on your casks once they're on a slot.

5Setup

  1. Place the main board and destination board. Shuffle the ship deck; deal 2 ships to slots and turn the next face-up by the Market. Seed 2–3 neutral buildings ⚙, all spread across different lines so no single line is the obvious opening. All five destinations begin open.
  2. Deal 3 of the 4 export beers ⚙. Each carries a fixed quality — Broyhan Q3 · Keut Q3 · Mumme Q4 · Bock Q5 — so dropping one varies the ladder: drop a Q3 → the full Q3→Q5 climb; drop Mumme → no Q4; drop Bock → no Q5. Recipes & ships are an always-available Market supply; for upgrades, shuffle all Upgrade tiles into a face-down deck and deal 4 ⚙ face-up — the Upgrade display (refills from the deck as tiles are taken, §11).
  3. Set the Sailed-Ships track to its player-scaled length (it scales steeply): ~7 / 11 / 15 / 19 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 players ⚙.
  4. Each player takes a brewery board, a worker, ownership discs, and a starting stake: the Gruit + Hopped recipes (printed), 2 open vessels (lanes 3–4 locked), and one Ready Gruit in Vessel 1 (deploy it turn 1).
  5. Starting goods (with seat compensation): 3 , 2 , plus +1 for every seat after the first ⚙ — so seats P1…P5 start with 3 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 4 G. (Turn order is fixed, which gives the first player a small edge; the free opening placement + this +1 G neutralize it.)
  6. Each player places their worker on any station — your opening placement is free (no occupancy toll, §6). Give the first-player marker to any player (it does not pass).

Warm start

Starting with 2 vessels, two shared hulls already on the Wharf, and a Ready cask means a first voyage is reachable by turn 2–3.

6A Turn

Turn 1 only: place your worker on any station (free) and activate one of its two lines (no move). From turn 2, in order:

  1. Move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent station (A↔B, A↔C, B↔D, C↔D — never the diagonal). Mandatory. If you move onto a station a rival already occupies, pay 1 ⚙ to the supply (the occupancy toll — capped at what you hold, never blocks).
  2. Activate the row OR the column of your worker's new station.
  3. Resolve the line's up-to-4 stops — slot · station · station · slot — in any order:
    • Each station: take its action. Stations are never closed — a rival's worker never stops you using one.
    • Each slot: if it holds a building, you may use its action — a cask's signature action, a ship's free load, or a neutral building. All resolve on your turn, for you — no out-of-turn gains.

Both stations always fire

Activating a line triggers both stations; your worker only determines which line is legal. Because base actions are always available and slot buildings only add, you are never stalled — a populated Wharf makes your turns bigger.

Play passes clockwise. The first player is fixed for the whole game — turn order does not rotate. The free opening placement and the +1 G per later seat (§5) are the seat balancers.

8The Cask & Brewing

The tile the whole game turns on, moving through three states: maturing (private vessel) → on a slot (cargo + a public action) → delivered (shipped → it scores and leaves). Beer is two layers — a type (a quality rung) + a recipe (permission to brew it). Casks are brewed, not bought, from a shared pool with fixed counts, so the rare export casks are contested. A loaded recipe crawls the maturation track — casks age 1 step automatically every turn, plus the Cellar pool.

9The Slots

The 8 slots hold deployed casks (cargo + a public action), the shared ships (neutral hulls; they sail away and return to the deck), and 2–3 neutral buildings seeded at setup. When a line fires, the active player may use each building in its two slots: a cask fires its signature action, a ship offers a free load, a neutral building gives its base action. All resolve on the active player's turn. When a rival uses your cask's action, you still own the cask and score it when shipped.

The race for cargo space

Everyone loads the same neutral ships — your own casks only (the old "ship a rival's cask" rule is cut in v0.11). The contest is space and timing: claim berths on rich routes, complete a load to take the dispatch fee, or launch early (your cask must be aboard) to deny a rival's fuller send-off.

10Ships & Destinations

Shared hulls (v0.11). Ships are neutral: hull (Cog 2 / Hulk 3) and destination printed on the tile, drawn from a shuffled ship deck that feeds a ship market of 3 ⚙ face-up at the Market. Commission (Market, 2 G ⚙): place any one of the three face-up ships on an open slot (then refill) — so you pick the hull bound where your cargo wants to go, and no single ill-bound tile stalls the yard. Lifecycle load → fill → sail: load your own casks via the Harbor action or a bonus free load when the ship's line fires; the instant it is full it sails — every cask owner scores + takes the benefit, the filler gains the 2 G dispatch fee ⚙, the Sailed-Ships marker advances, and the hull returns to the bottom of the deck.

Charter Voyage

The Harbor always offers a Charter (no tile — an outside hull): pay the charter fare — a shared market price printed under the Sailed-Ships track ⚙, set by how many voyages the table has delivered (2-player row: 2·2·3·2·2·4·5·5 — cheap early, 4–5 G in the closing stretch; one price for everyone) — to send one Ready cask — from a vessel or a slot — on an immediate single-cask voyage to a destination it qualifies for. It delivers normally and advances the clock, but is strictly worse per cask than a Cog/Hulk — so filling the shared ships stays the better plan; the Charter just keeps the tight slots from ever deadlocking.

Delivery Benefits

Delivering to London / Novgorod hands you a brewery upgrade from the display at no goods cost (Bergen pays its big majority instead; Bruges pays goods; the Hall pays prestige). The cycle reinforces itself: deliver → earn an upgrade → brew better → deliver better.

11The Brewery & Upgrades

Your brewery holds your vessels (start 2, cap 4), your recipes, and your upgrades — where strategies diverge. Upgrades come from the Market's face-up Upgrade display (a row of 4 ⚙ dealt from a shuffled face-down deck, refilling as taken): buy one (pay its goods cost) or earn one free by delivering to London / Novgorod. Buying Upgrades (4–5 G)

Delivering to London / Novgorod hands you a display tile free — on top of the voyage's value. Buying yields only the upgrade, so it is priced at a comparable 4–5 G ⚙: a real option for bootstrapping, but the primary path is deliver → earn an upgrade → brew better.

12Goods & Storage

grain and hops are the only currency — recipes, brew inputs, ships, upgrades, loader benefits. You may hold up to 8 goods ⚙ (raised by the Warehouse room). There is no money and no spendable prestige — delivered points are score only.

13End of the Game

The game ends when the shared Sailed-Ships track fills — a visible, steerable clock:

  • Every voyage fills one slot on the track — a ship sailing (full or launched early) or a Charter.
  • Track length scales with player count: ~7 / 11 / 15 / 19 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 players ⚙ (it fills faster with more players).
  • Backstop: a round limit (≈25 ⚙) ends a slow table even if the track hasn't filled.
  • When it fills → finish the current round (so everyone has had equal turns) → score (§14).

Steer it: a volume player races to sail ships fast and end the game; a prestige player keeps a steadier pace to buy more delivering turns.

14Scoring

No separate reach/standing tracks. You score from what you delivered:

  1. Delivery value — sum your delivered casks' values at each destination (kontore = trade value, kept low, plus the export premium ⚙: Q4 +1, Q5 +2; the Hall = prestige, scaling with quality, Q×2.5).
  2. Majorities (a big chunk of the score) — at every kontor, by delivered-cask count, 1st / 2nd / 3rd place take that kontor's tiers: Bruges 5/3/0 · London 6/4/2 · Novgorod 8/5/2 · Bergen 10/6/3 ⚙. 2-player games skip 2nd (winner-take-all); tied players split the sum of the tiers they occupy ⚙.
  3. Goals — you hold 3 goal tiles (dealt at setup; hand cap 4 ⚙ via the Towncrier); your best 2 score ⚙.

Highest total wins. Tiebreak: most goods, then most casks on slots.

152-Player Variant

With fewer workers the slots feel loose, so lock some slots (down to ~6 ⚙) to keep the race for cargo space tight. The Sailed-Ships track is shortest at 2p (~7 slots ⚙), so the clock stays brisk. Majorities are winner-take-all at 2p (2nd place is skipped). Everything else plays identically.

Station · Slot · Line
A station is one of the four action spaces; a slot is one of the 8 spaces ringing them (a deployed cask, ship, or neutral building); a line is a row or column = its two stations plus the tiles in their two slots.

Seat compensation
Later seats start with +1 G each — the fixed-turn-order balancer.
Cask · Deploy
A brewed beer in one of three states: maturing (vessel) → on a slot (cargo + a public action) → delivered (shipped → scores). Deploy = set a Ready cask onto an open slot.
Ship · Charter
A Ship is a neutral hull (Cog 2 / Hulk 3) with its destination printed, from the shared ship deck; anyone loads their own casks; it sails when full (the filler takes the dispatch fee) and returns to the deck. Destination · Kontor · Hall
Where a ship delivers. Kontore pay trade value + majorities (volume); the Hall pays prestige (scarcity).
Majority
Tiered, ranked by delivered casks at a kontor (1st/2nd/3rd take the tiers; 2p skips 2nd). Concentrated at Bergen.
Recipe · Upgrade · Building
Permission to brew a type · a Room/Modifier on your brewery (mostly earned by delivering) · a shared base action seeded on a slot.
Sailed-Ships track
The shared end clock; each voyage fills one slot. When full → finish the round → score.

18Quick Reference

Your turn

  1. Move to an orthogonally adjacent station (mandatory) — pay 1 G to the supply if a rival is already there.
  2. Activate its row or column.
  3. Resolve the line (slot · station · station · slot), any order. Both stations fire; stations never closed; use the buildings in the line's two slots (on your turn).

At the Wharf

Market (gain goods, or buy a recipe / ship / upgrade) → Brewhouse (Brew a cask) → Cellar (Age to Ready → deploy to a slot) → Harbor (Ship → sails when full → delivers).

Where to ship

Bruges (+1, goods, maj 5/3/0) · London (+2, an upgrade, maj 6/4/2) · Bergen (+2, goods, maj 10/6/3 — the anchor) · Novgorod (+4, an upgrade, maj 8/5/2) · the Hall (Q×2.5, prestige). Majorities are the big motivator (delivery values kept low); gate by quality; each cask owner scores & takes the benefit when the shared hull sails.

Game ends

The Sailed-Ships track fills (each voyage = 1 slot) → finish the round → score: Deliveries + Majorities (tiered; 2p skips 2nd) + Goals (best 2 of your 3).
