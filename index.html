1The Brewing House

Hamburg was called the brewhouse of the Hanse. You are a merchant brewing house in the League, c. 1350.

The pivotal innovation of your age is hopped beer. Unlike perishable gruit ale, hopped beer survives a sea voyage — so beer becomes a shippable export. You will source grain and hops, brew casks, mature them, and push them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts (the kontore: Bergen, London, Bruges, Novgorod) — or enshrine your finest, locally, into the brewers' Hall. Whoever has shipped and enshrined the most valuable casks when the last ship sails wins.

Source → Brew → Age → Ship → (repeat)

  • You brew casks, mature them, and ship them to destinations for points. Where you ship is the key decision: VOLUME the kontore (value + majorities) vs PRESTIGE the Hall (quality, out of commerce).
  • Every station is a printed THIS-or-THAT — exactly two actions, one choice per visit. Both stations on an activated line fire — the worker only gates which line is legal. (Hover a station to light its two lines.)
  • Lines pair two stations: Market+Brewhouse Brewhouse+Cellar Harbor+Cellar Market+Harbor.
  • The 8 slots are a living, composable ring: deployed casks (cargo + a public action), neutral ships, and owned slot tiles that modify the cask or ship docked on them. Slot locality: a slot’s stop acts on that slot — an empty slot’s stop deploys a cask here, a ship’s stop loads this hull, a cask’s stop fires its action; only the stations reach the whole wharf. Half your line evolves — the two stations are fixed, the two slots grow as you build. Base actions always work; slot tiles only add.

Three rules constraints

No dice as randomizers (fully deterministic — the demand die is a marker, set to a printed number) · No hidden draws (everything you acquire is taken from a face-up supply) · No money (goods are the only currency; delivered points are score only).

3Components

  • 1 Main board — the Wharf: a 2×2 of four stations (A Market · B Brewhouse · D Cellar · C Harbor, each with its two printed action faces) ringed by 8 slots, plus the shared Sailed-Ships track (the end clock).
  • 1 Destination board — the four kontore, each with its quality gate, printed value, benefit, and majority track (§4, §10).
  • 1 Hall shelf board (new, v3.0-A) — four quality-gated shelves (Q2/Q3/Q4/Q5), each printing its row ★ (3/5/7/9) and a row of bonus spaces (one honor icon each), claimed with the enshriner’s cube (§10).
  • 4 Player boards — each private & untouchable: a vessel row of 4 printed slots (2 open at start, the rest under unlock covers), a Specialist row of 4 printed slots (2 open at start, covers on the rest), a flip shelf of 2 (displaced slot tiles land here face-down), the Flight/unlock strip (one space per beer type — brewed marks unlock; delivered marks score), a recipe rack, and goods storage (cap 8).
  • The tile supply — cask tiles (a shared pool, min 6 per type; an ownership disc marks one on a slot or delivered), hull tiles (the neutral ship deck — Cog 2 / Hulk 3 with destinations, gates, and numbered berth wells printed; a berth holds a cask cube + its demand die), slot tiles (~27 — blue Privileges + green Buildings/works, §9), Specialist tiles (purple — 7 designs × n−1 copies, §11), recipe cards, and charter contracts.
  • Goods cubes — grain (G) and hops (H), the only currency. Per player: 1 worker, presence discs (12 ⚙ — the printed supply), cask cubes (~8 ⚙ — ride the berth wells + mark Hall shelf spaces), demand dice (d6 — set to ONE printed number, once, at departure). 1 first-player marker (fixed — it does not pass).

4Anatomy of the Game

The Stations & Lines

Four stations sit in a 2×2: A Market (Source) & B Brewhouse (Brew) on top, C Harbor (Ship) & D Cellar (Age) below. You don't simply circle the board — you move where the board is best. Eight slots ring the stations, two beside each line. A line is its two stations plus any tiles in their two slots (slot · station · station · slot); each slot belongs to exactly one line. (Recipes & improvements live on your private board; buildings sit on the slots.)

The Destinations (where value lives)

The four kontore + the Hall are all open from the start. Each wants a certain quality of beer and pays differently — so there is no single best line of play. A kontor delivery is worth the destination’s printed value + the one demand die the cask carries — set once, at departure, to the single number printed on the owner’s Privilege under it (one read, nothing recalculated). The Hall is a shelf board: enshrining claims an open space on a shelf your quality reaches — the row ★ (3/5/7/9) plus that space’s printed honor — the visible prestige race.

The majority is the stable, board-agnostic floor — every kontor pays a tiered, ranked majority by delivered-cask count, 1st / 2nd / 3rd: Bruges 4/2/0 · London 5/3/1 · Novgorod 8/5/2 · Bergen 9/5/2 ⚙ (Bergen the rich anchor). Base delivery values are low so the in-game points live in the demand you build; the Hall's prestige is a fixed ladder 3/5/7/9 by quality. 2-player games skip 2nd (winner-take-all); ties split the occupied tiers. Each kontor's benefit is distinct: Bruges 2 goods (liquidity) · London take a Building (the engine) · Bergen a free Reach, +1 presence (feeds its rich majority) · Novgorod refine a maturing cask +1 age — and Novgorod's value scales with quality (Q3·2 / Q4·4 / Q5·6). The cask's owner always scores the delivery and takes any benefit.

Your Brewery (private, untouchable)

Your player board holds the vessel row (casks crawl Brew → … → Ready — 2 of 4 printed slots open at start, the rest under Flight-unlock covers), the Specialist row (2 of 4 open), the flip shelf (2), the Flight/unlock strip, and a recipe rack. A cask held in a vessel powers your Floor (§11 — a stay-home turn runs its action) — so it works before it ships, and hold vs deploy is a live decision. Rivals can only act on your casks once they're deployed on a slot.

5Setup

  1. Place the main board and destination board. Shuffle the ship deck; deal 2 ships to slots (spread across different lines) and turn the next three face-up by the Market (a ship market of 3). Deal 2 neutral green Buildings from the deck onto open slots (they serve any dock; no owner — overbuilding one still costs the 1 G ground rent, pays nobody, and the tile is discarded). The remaining slots start empty — players author them. The four kontore and the Hall shelf board begin open.
  2. Deal 3 of the 4 export beers ⚙. Each carries a fixed quality — Broyhan Q3 · Keut Q3 · Mumme Q4 · Bock Q5 — so dropping one varies the ladder: drop a Q3 → the full Q3→Q5 climb; drop Mumme → no Q4; drop Bock → no Q5. Recipes & ships are an always-available Market supply; shuffle the Privileges & Buildings deck and deal 4 ⚙ face-up — the Wharf display (refills as tiles are taken, §9); shuffle the Specialist deck (n−1 copies of each of the 7) and deal 4 face-up at the Cellar. Place the Hall shelf board (active spaces per shelf ≈ n+1 ⚙). Set out each quality's cask-action pile face-up (you brew toward the visible top action, §8).
  3. Set the Sailed-Ships track to its player-scaled length (it scales steeply): ~6 / 10 / 13 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 players ⚙.
  4. Each player takes a player board, a worker, discs/cubes/dice, and a starting stake: the Gruit + Hopped recipes (printed), 2 open vessel slots (of 4 printed — the rest under Flight-unlock covers; the Coppersmith also opens one), 2 open Specialist slots (of 4), 2 charter contracts, and one Ready Gruit in Vessel 1 (deploy it turn 1). (No starting building — slot tiles are always bought/earned from the display and placed at once.)
  5. Starting goods: 3 , 2 — equal for every seat ⚙ (v1.7 removed the old +1 /later-seat compensation — under strong play it over-corrected). Turn order is fixed; the first player's natural turn-1 edge (choose the line, place + activate the warm Gruit, no toll) plus the free opening placement balance it.
  6. Each player places their worker on any station — your opening placement is free (no occupancy toll, §6). Give the first-player marker to any player (it does not pass).

Warm start

Starting with 2 vessels, two shared hulls already on the Wharf, and a Ready cask means a first voyage is reachable by turn 2–3.

6A Turn

Turn 1 only: place your worker on any station (free) and choose one of its lines (no move). From turn 2, in order:

  1. Move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent station (A↔B, A↔C, B↔D, C↔D — never the diagonal) — OR stay home and work your Floor (§11: the worker keeps its station; you skip the wharf entirely this turn; a Floor with no live stop is not a legal choice).
  2. If you moved, choose the station's row or its column. The 1 occupancy toll ⚙ is paid to the supply only when you activate a line while a rival shares your station — the Floor is never tolled.
  3. Resolve the line's stops — a public line's slot · station · station · slot, or your Floor's cask actions + Wilds — in any order; every stop is optional:
    • Each station: take its action. Stations are never closed — a rival's worker never stops you using one.
    • Each slot — LOCALITY (v3.0-A): its stop acts on THAT slot. An empty (or tile-only) slot's stop deploys a Ready cask HERE — or, if the tile prints an action (Rope Walk · Grain Exchange · Mission Quay), take that instead. A ship's stop loads this hull. A cask's stop fires its printed action (as modified by the tile under it) — or over-deploy onto it: your own lower Ready cask is tapped on the way out (its action fires once, then it's boxed); anyone's Q1 under your higher cask has soured (boxed, no action; a maturing Staithe cask is safe except this Q1 rule). All resolve on your turn, for you — no out-of-turn gains.

Both stations always fire

Activating a line triggers both stations; your worker only determines which line is legal. Because base actions are always available and slot buildings only add, you are never stalled — a populated Wharf makes your turns bigger.

Play passes clockwise. The first player is fixed for the whole game — turn order does not rotate. Starts are equal (v1.7 removed the +1 G/later-seat compensation); the seat balancer is the free opening placement plus the first player's natural turn-1 edge (§5).

Load and Dispatch take a DEPLOYED cask — a public showing is the price of every sale. Every gate check uses effective quality (kiln/Duckstein lifts count for gates and points; the Customs House instead relieves the ship's boarding gate).

8The Cask & Brewing

The tile the whole game turns on — half your impact is what you brew and how you use each cask. Three states: maturing (private vessel — it powers your Floor, working before it ships) → on a slot (cargo + a public action, contestable) → delivered (shipped or dispatched → it scores and leaves). So hold vs deploy is a real call: a held cask keeps its action on your Floor but clogs the vessel; deploying frees the space and makes it public cargo — and parking one is a reservation with a payoff: over-deploy your own higher cask onto it later and the lesser cask is tapped on the way out (its action fires once, then it's boxed). Anyone's Q1 under a higher cask sours (boxed, no action). Beer is two layers — a quality rung + a recipe (permission to brew it); casks are brewed, not bought, from a shared pool, so the rare export casks are contested. Casks age 1 step automatically every turn, plus the Cellar pool.

9The Slots

The 8 slots are a living, composable ring of deployed casks (cargo + a public action), shared ships (neutral hulls that sail away and return to the deck), and owned slot tiles you place. A slot stacks tile → ship → cargo (or tile → cask): a tile modifies the occupant docked on it. Three tile types, three colours: a PRIVILEGE (bright blue) prints ONE die number — the owner’s cask departing that slot carries its demand die set to exactly that number (a rival docking there banks nothing); a BUILDING / work (green) serves whoever docks — and several now print an action on their slot’s stop (Rope Walk · Grain Exchange · Mission Quay), so authored lines are strictly richer; the third type — the purple SPECIALISTS — lives on your player board (§11). No payments between players; the contest is squatting on a rival’s privilege (deny it), overbuilding it, and the occupancy toll. When a line fires, the active player uses what its two slots hold, on their turn. Slots never lock — overbuild costs the ground rent: placing a tile on a built slot costs the builder 1 G ⚙ (paid to the stores; can’t pay → occupied slots aren’t legal targets) and displaces the tile there — it flips face-down to its owner’s flip shelf (max 2; full → returned to the box) as a Wild stop on their Floor. Flips score nothing — a flip is engine, never points. The 8 slots are a constant contest over prime positions — but eviction has a price.

The ONE-READ demand die (v3.0-A): when your cask departs a slot under your Privilege (loaded, dispatched, enshrined), set its d6 to the single number printed on the tile. That’s the whole procedure — no premium arithmetic, no sail-time bump, no cap rule (no tile prints above 6); conditions (a kontor match, a Q4+ gate, no-presence-yet) are printed on the tile and read at departure. The die rides the cask’s berth well on the hull and returns to the pool after delivery. The quality climb pays where quality is read: Novgorod’s printed scale, the Hall’s shelf gates, and the quality-keyed Privileges (Connoisseur · Burgomaster). A quality work (Malt Kiln / Hop Yard) rides a +1-quality marker instead — it serves anyone. Delivery = printed value + the die — nothing is remembered in your head.

The race for cargo space

Everyone loads the same neutral ships — and you may also load a rival's cask, choosing its destination (the owner scores it + banks the destination benefit when the ship sails; you take a 1 G loader bonus now and free the slot). Commissioning grants a free load — any player’s deployed cask, or a Ready cask straight from your vessels; placed onto a qualifying cask, that pickup IS the free load; loading commits a cask but pays nothing yet; filling the ship sails the hull — and only then does each cask deliver, its owner banking that destination's benefit (goods / a Building) and its points, resolved in load order. Filling earns no bonus — the contest is berths on the rich routes and assembling a full cargo.

10Ships & Destinations

Shared hulls. Ships are neutral: hull (Cog 2 / Hulk 3) and a kontor destination printed on the tile, drawn from a shuffled ship deck (20 hulls ⚙ — 5 each for Bruges / London / Bergen / Novgorod; 11 Cog / 9 Hulk) that feeds a ship market of 3 ⚙ face-up at the Market. The hull is the carrier component: it prints its destination, quality gate, and numbered berth wells — each well holds the owner’s cask cube + the cask’s demand die (if set) + any +1Q marker. Ships sail only to the four kontore — the Hall is never a ship destination (it is reached locally, by Dispatch, below). Commission (Market, 2 G ⚙): place any one of the three face-up ships on an open slot and take the free load — any player’s deployed cask (a rival’s → the rival-loading rules) or a Ready cask straight from your own vessels (the one universal vessel-direct door past deploy-first) — or onto a slot whose cask QUALIFIES for the hull’s printed port: the dockside pickup — that cask boards as the commission’s free load (no second load; a hull filled by the pickup sails immediately). Everything is paid on delivery: a ship sails only when it is full — there is no early launch — and only then does each cask deliver, its owner banking both that destination’s benefit and its points (printed value + the die), in load order. Loading banks nothing on its own — it is a commitment, and filling-and-sailing is the payoff. Filling earns no bonus. Each voyage advances the Sailed-Ships marker, and the hull returns to the bottom of the deck.

Dispatch — the Hall shelf board

The Hall is not a ship destination — it is the free route of the Harbor’s DISPATCH. Send a deployed Q2+ cask of yours (no ship, no fare): choose any OPEN space on a shelf whose gate your cask’s effective quality meets — a Bock may take a Common-Shelf space for its honor instead of the High Board’s glory. Bank the row ★, take the space’s one printed honor, and mark the space with your cube — the trophy and the record (the cask retires to the box). Like every voyage, it advances the Sailed-Ships clock.

Dispatch — a kontor (the charter route)

The Harbor’s DISPATCH also sails one of your deployed Ready casks alone to a kontor it qualifies for: spend a charter contract + a flat 2 fare ⚙ (the Quaymaster also reaches your vessels). Each house starts with 2 contracts and can buy more at the Market (1 G each, unlimited supply) — the scarce contract is the throttle. It delivers normally and advances the clock, but is strictly worse per cask than a Cog/Hulk — filling the shared ships stays the better plan; the kontor Dispatch keeps the tight slots from ever deadlocking (Move → Market → buy a contract → dispatch is always a legal escape).

Delivery Benefits — when gained, owner’s choice

Each kontor pays a distinct benefit, resolved by the cask’s owner the moment it delivers — whoever’s turn it is: Bruges 2 goods, any mix (liquidity) · London choose a Privilege/Building from the Wharf display and place it now — or take a free Specialist instead (the engine loop: deliver → author → deliver better) · Bergen a free Reach (+1 presence at a kontor you’ve delivered to, feeding its rich majority) · Novgorod refine — you choose which maturing cask ages +1, and its value scales with quality (Q3·2 / Q4·4 / Q5·6). The Hall pays its shelf row ★ + the claimed space’s honor (the Dispatch above).

11The Player Board & the Floor

Your board is private and safe: the vessel row (2 of 4 printed slots open at start), the Specialist row (2 of 4), the flip shelf (2), the Flight/unlock strip, and your recipes. The SPECIALISTS — the third tile type (purple) — are one-time buys for goods at the Cellar (2–4 G ⚙), hired free by the Q3+ Gain 1 specialist cask action, or taken as London’s benefit. Scarce supply: they form a shuffled deck of n−1 copies of each of the 7 types feeding a face-up display of 4 at the Cellar — only what’s face-up is buyable, so the table competes. Grain Factor vs Hop Gardener is a real choice: grain runs operations, hops climbs the ladder (Mumme/Bock lean hops).

The Floor — stay home to work it (v3.0-A)

Instead of moving, you may stay home: the worker keeps its station and your whole turn happens on your board — an Age pool of 3 ⚙ across your vessels (the Lagerkeeper adds +2) · every vessel cask’s printed action (the cask stays) · one Wild per flipped tile (max 2). Never tolled — but a Floor turn is a turn not spent on the wharf; that positional price is the tension. A Floor with no live stop is not a legal choice.

Your strip has one space per beer type this game. Mark a beer the first time you BREW it — from your 2nd distinct beer on, each new mark grants ONE UNLOCK: remove a cover from the vessel row (to 4) or the Specialist row (to 4), your choice. Distinct beers DELIVERED still score the Flight ladder (§14) — one strip, two mark states: brewed = unlocked, delivered = scored.

12Goods & Storage

grain and hops are the only currency — recipes, brew inputs, ships, buildings, loader benefits. You may hold up to 8 goods ⚙. There is no money and no spendable prestige — delivered points are score only.

13End of the Game

The game ends when the shared Sailed-Ships track fills — a visible, steerable clock:

  • Every dispatch fills one slot on the track — a ship sailing (only when full), a kontor Dispatch (charter), or a Hall Dispatch (enshrine).
  • Track length scales with player count: ~6 / 10 / 13 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 players ⚙ (it fills faster with more players).
  • Backstop: a round limit (≈25 ⚙) ends a slow table even if the track hasn't filled.
  • When it fills → finish the current round (so everyone has had equal turns) → score (§14).

Steer it: a volume player races to sail ships fast and end the game; a prestige player keeps a steadier pace to buy more delivering turns.

14Scoring

No goal tiles, no separate tracks — you score from what you delivered: two in-game sources, two end-game.

  1. The Hall — shelf rows (in-game): each enshrined cask banked its shelf’s row ★ (3 / 5 / 7 / 9) — and took its space’s printed honor when placed. The visible prestige race.
  2. Kontore — value + die (in-game): each delivered cask scores its destination’s printed value + the one demand die it carries (set once, at departure, to the Privilege’s printed number) — never less than 1★. The demand lane.
  3. Majorities (end-game) — at every kontor, by delivered-cask count, 1st / 2nd / 3rd take the tiers: Bruges 4/2/0 · London 5/3/1 · Novgorod 8/5/2 · Bergen 9/5/2 ⚙. 2-player games skip 2nd; ties split the occupied tiers. The stable floor.
  4. The Flight (end-game) — your range: count the distinct beers you delivered and score (beers − 1)², min 3 → 3→4 · 4→9 · 5→16 · 6→25 (the 6th rung exists with the Jopenbier capstone — it counts). Brewing those beers also unlocked your Floor along the way (§11) — the range lane pays twice.

Flipped tiles score nothing — a displaced slot tile on your flip shelf is a Floor Wild (engine), never points.

Highest total wins. Tiebreak: the combined quality of casks you’ve deployed on slots (built but not yet delivered), then most goods.

The four paths to win (each a complete lane — you needn’t braid): Prestige (the Hall’s shelves — row ★ + honors) · Demand (route casks through your Privileges) · Volume (kontor majorities) · Range (the Flight — and its unlocks grow your house). An expert braids two.

152-Player Variant

The Sailed-Ships track is shortest at 2p (~6 slots ⚙), so the clock stays brisk with fewer workers on the board. Majorities are winner-take-all at 2p (2nd place is skipped). Everything else plays identically — there's no separate slot-locking rule.

★Expansions (three opt-in toggles · mix freely)

Specialty Beers

A modular, opt-in expansion, switched on by a New Game checkbox. Off → the base game is exactly as above. On → three new specialty beers join the export draft, so you deal 3 of 7 (vs the base 3 of 4), with a guarantee of ≥1 base Q4+ (Mumme/Bock) so the quality climb still matters. The point: turn the roster from a quality ladder you climb into characters you main. Each specialty beer is pinned — its slot-action is fixed & printed (the base climb beers still draw from the steerable pile, so the Orléans-lite agency is untouched; this simply generalizes Gruit's pin) — and carries one always-on signature, so leaning a beer pays during play, not just at scoring.

The Trade Roads (Overland · the big one — replaces the kontor majorities)

An inland trade network — a tree rooted at Hamburg (home). Two roads lead out: West to the Bruges gateway (which branches to London, Bergen, and the Rhineland) and East on the long deep haul to Novgorod. Bruges is the lone no-slots gateway; every other node — the towns AND the London/Bergen/Novgorod kontore — carries Staple-Right slots (the kontore claim theirs when your caravan reaches them, on top of their base sea benefit). You grow it with the deliveries you already make (no new station, no new action; it rides the Harbor's Ship). When it's on, the kontor majorities turn OFF — the contest moves inland.

  • Movement is per VOYAGE — ONE node per owner. Each ship that sails to a kontor (full keystone — the demand die, the Flight; or a kontor Dispatch) advances your caravan one node toward it. A 3-cask Hulk = one step. Casks never buy depth — only quality (the gate) and repeated voyages (the clock) do.
  • Each cask claims a Staple Right. Every cask aboard then claims an open slot at the node reached, in load order (first loaded picks first) — scoring the node's ★ + a one-shot bonus. A 2-cask voyage to your frontier town = two slots. Never nothing: a full town pays the line's small overflow.
  • Quality gates depth. You step onto the next node only if a cask aboard meets its quality gate — voyages buy distance, quality unlocks the deep towns. Reach (breadth) and quality (depth) both win.
  • Slots are flavoured by LANE (2–4 per node, scaling n+1 with player count; deep ◆ = high-gate terminals — Pskov scarce at one slot, Frankfurt a rich 4-slot outlet): Rhineland = craft (a recipe · a brew action · +2 age) · London = infrastructure (a Building from the display · a free Specialist · goods) · Bergen = logistics (a charter contract · goods · a vessel) · the East = depth/value (a vessel · +1 quality · big points). Frankfurt is points + a free Enshrine (a free Hall Dispatch — the shelf board applies); Pskov is just a delivery — one slot +8★, every later delivery +5★. The East pays the most in points; the Rhineland fuels the climb that gets you there.
  • Bruges = the knowledge on-ramp (the one no-slots node): a delivery there lets you take a recipe OR 2 goods. The Rhine Charter — a Q4+ Charter — skips Bruges, leaping the caravan straight to Cologne.
  • The map: West — Bruges → Cologne → Frankfurt (◆); → Antwerp → London; → Tønsberg → Bergen. East — Visby → Tallinn → Novgorod → Pskov (◆ · Q5). Scoring (replaces majorities): the inland ★ = node delivery points + the slot bonuses you claimed, banked as you play; the Hall stays the non-delivery lane.

Built on a small expansion spine (a registry + hooks) so each module is one clean, self-contained block. All numbers ⚙. See RULES.md §12 · DESIGN.md §9.

16Glossary

The Wharf
The whole core area: four stations (Market · Brewhouse · Cellar · Harbor) ringed by 8 slots.
Station · Slot · Line
A station is one of the four action spaces; a slot is one of the 8 spaces ringing them (a deployed cask, a ship, and/or an owned building); a line is a row or column = its two stations plus the tiles in their two slots.
Occupancy toll
1 G ⚙ paid to the supply when you activate a public line while a rival shares your station (the Floor is never tolled; opening placement free). Never blocks.
Seat compensation
Removed in v1.7. Starts are equal for every seat; the first-player balancer is the free opening placement + P1's natural turn-1 edge.
Cask · Deploy · Over-deploy
A brewed beer in one of three states: maturing (vessel) → on a slot (cargo + a public action) → delivered (shipped/dispatched → scores). Deploy = set a Ready cask onto a slot (a slot’s own stop deploys HERE; the Brewhouse deploys anywhere). Over-deploy: onto your own lower Ready cask — it’s tapped on the way out (action fires once, then boxed); onto anyone’s Q1 — it has soured (boxed, no action).
Ship · Dispatch
A Ship is a neutral hull (Cog 2 / Hulk 3) with its destination, gate, and berth wells printed; anyone loads eligible casks (loading commits, pays nothing yet); it sails only when full — then every cask delivers in load order and the hull returns to the deck. Dispatch (Harbor) sends ONE deployed cask alone: to the Hall shelf board (free) or to a kontor for a charter contract + a flat 2 G fare (start with 2; buy more at the Market, 1 G) — less efficient, but the deadlock guard.
Destination · Kontor · Hall
Where a ship delivers. Kontore pay trade value + majorities (volume); the Hall pays prestige (scarcity).
Majority
Tiered, ranked by delivered casks at a kontor (1st/2nd/3rd take the tiers; 2p skips 2nd). Concentrated at Bergen.
Recipe · Privilege · Building
Recipe = permission to brew a type · Privilege (blue) = an owned slot tile printing ONE die number — the owner’s departing cask carries the die at exactly that value; rivals bank nothing · Building / work (green) = an owned slot tile that transforms the cask/ship docked on it, for whoever docks (some print an action on their slot’s stop). No payments between players at delivery.
The Floor
Stay home instead of moving (v3.0-A): your whole turn on your board — the Age-3 pool, every vessel cask’s action, a Wild per flipped tile (max 2). Never tolled; a null Floor is not legal. A held cask works before it ships — hold vs deploy is the call. Flipped tiles are engine only — they score nothing.
The Flight unlock track
One strip, one space per beer type. Brewed (first time) = a mark; from the 2nd mark on, each grants a Floor-slot unlock (vessel row or Specialist row, to 4 each). Delivered = the second mark state — distinct beers delivered score the Flight ladder.
The Hall shelf board
Four quality-gated shelves (Q2→3★ · Q3→5★ · Q4→7★ · Q5→9★). A Hall Dispatch claims an open space on a shelf your quality reaches: bank the row ★ + the space’s printed honor + mark it with your cube. All qualifying shelves full → the best row’s ★ anyway (never nothing).
Sailed-Ships track
The shared end clock; each voyage fills one slot. When full → finish the round → score.

18Quick Reference

Your turn

  1. Move to an orthogonally adjacent station — or stay home and work your Floor (Age-3 pool · every vessel cask's action · a Wild per flipped tile; never tolled).
  2. If you moved, choose the row or the column. A line while sharing the station costs 1 G to the supply.
  3. Resolve the line's stops, any order, all optional. Both stations fire (each a printed this-or-that, one choice). A slot's stop acts on THAT slot — deploy here · load this hull · this cask's action (or over-deploy onto it).

At the Wharf

Market (Source 2 goods / Acquire 1 tile) → Brewhouse (Brew — the top of a quality pile / Deploy → any slot) → Cellar (Age 3 pts / Upgrade — 1 Specialist) → Harbor (Load 1 cask — sails when full / Dispatch 1 deployed cask → the Hall shelf board free, or a kontor for a contract + 2 G). Or stay home on your Floor.

Where to ship

Bruges (+1, 2 goods, maj 4/2/0) · London (+1, a Privilege/Building placed at once OR a Specialist, maj 5/3/1) · Bergen (+1, free Reach, maj 9/5/2 — the anchor) · Novgorod (scales 2/4/6 by quality, refine, maj 8/5/2). A kontor delivery = printed value + the one demand die (set at departure to your Privilege's printed number; never less than 1★). The Hall: a Dispatch claims a shelf space — row ★ 3/5/7/9 by gate + the space's printed honor + your cube.

Game ends

The Sailed-Ships track fills (each dispatch = 1 slot — a full sail, a kontor Dispatch, or a Hall Dispatch) → finish the round → score: Hall shelf rows + kontor deliveries (value + die) + Majorities (tiered; 2p skips 2nd) + the Flight (range; 6→25 with Jopenbier). Flipped tiles score nothing.
