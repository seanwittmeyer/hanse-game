Brewhouses of the Hanse

Learn the basics, then play.
2–4 players · ~45–60 min at 2p · v1.4 “Deploy” · Rulebook & Components · Play

1The story

It's around 1350, and the Hanseatic League runs the trade of northern Europe. You are a merchant brewing house — a family firm in a League city. You buy grain and hops, brew casks of beer, and ship them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts — the kontore at Bergen, London, Bruges, and Novgorod.

Whoever has shipped the most valuable casks when the last ship sails wins. It all happens at the Wharf: you Source → Brew → Age → Ship, again and again, until the clock runs out.

3The stations — and how you move

The four steps are four stations in a shared 2×2. On your turn you move your worker one step to a neighbouring station, then fire that station's row or column — which triggers both stations on that line, plus any tiles in their two slots. You go where the work and the board are best — you're not just circling a track.

The production order is Market → Brewhouse → Cellar → Harbor, but you can't do all four in a turn — you choose which two actions (a line) to resolve and move where you need to. Stations are never closed, but moving onto a station a rival already occupies costs you 1 good — a gentle nudge to spread out (your opening placement is free).

Around the stations are 8 slots — a living ring. Your Ready casks, the shared ships, and the buildings you place sit here; a building modifies the cask or ship docked on it (worth more, or changed). Half your line evolves: the two stations are fixed, but the two slots grow as you build — so landing a building beside a line you fire often is the heart of the game. (Stations + slots = the Wharf.)

4Your Casks

Each brewed cask moves through three states:

① Maturing

In a vessel on your private board, ageing toward Ready. Safe — no rival can touch it here.

② On a slot

Once Ready, you set it out on a shared slot. Now it's two things: your cargo waiting for a ship, and a bonus action anyone who fires its line can use. Higher-quality beers give stronger actions.

③ Delivered

Loaded onto a ship and shipped to a destination — it scores there and leaves the board.

When you brew you choose from the top of each quality's pile, so you see the action you'd get (Source goods, Age, Load, Reach for presence, Convert, Survey a building into your hand, or — on the best casks — Wild) and brew toward your engine. And a cask held in a vessel powers your Floor — it works before it ships — so the key call is hold it (keep its action private) or deploy it (free the vessel, make it public cargo; a rival may even ship it for you).

5Ships & the slots

Ships belong to no one — each carries casks to one of the four kontore (Bruges, London, Bergen, Novgorod), printed with its destination and drawn off a shuffled ship deck, with 3 ships face-up at the Market to choose from. Pay 2 goods to commission any one of them onto an open slot — and you load one of your Ready casks aboard for free (the commissioner's reward). A Cog carries 2 casks, a Hulk 3. You load your own Ready casks — at the Harbor or free when the ship's line fires — and loading is just a commitment: it pays nothing yet. A ship sails only when it is full (there is no launching one early) — and only then is every cask aboard delivered: each cask's owner banks that port's reward (goods, or a Building) and its points, resolved in load order. Then the hull goes back under the deck. Filling a ship earns nothing extra — you just chose the cask that completed it.

Charter Voyage. Ships get used up and the slots are tight, so the Harbor always offers a Charter: spend a charter contract plus a flat 2-goods fare to send one Ready cask (from a vessel or a slot) on a single trip to a kontor now. Each house starts with 2 contracts and can buy more at the Market (1 good each, always available) — the scarce contract is the throttle. It scores like any delivery and advances the clock, but it's only one cask, so owning ships is the more efficient option. The Charter is the backup when your slots are jammed (Move → Market → buy a contract → charter is always a legal escape).

Everything pays on delivery: loading a cask banks nothing on its own — it's a commitment. Only when the ship fills and sails does each cask deliver, its owner taking both that port's reward (goods or a Building) and the points, resolved in load order. So loading is the setup and filling-and-sailing is the payoff. Commissioning a ship (with its free load) secures a berth — the classic merchant move. Your private brewery is always safe.

6Choosing Destinations

Every destination requires a certain quality of beer, and each pays differently. A kontor delivery is a small base + whatever value-buildings you routed the cask through (the demand you build); the Hall rewards quality with a fixed prestige ladder:

Volume

Ship to the kontore — Bruges, London, Bergen, Novgorod — for trade value and majorities (whoever delivers the most casks to a kontor scores its majority — a big chunk of the score; every kontor pays one, with Bergen the richest). kontore
vs

Quality

Climb the ladder and Enshrine your finest at the brewers' Hall — a local Harbor action, no boat, no fare: present a Ready cask (Q2+) from a slot for prestige (a printed ladder by quality, Q2→3 up to Q5→9). It also builds a Flight: the more distinct beers you deliver, the bigger the end-game bonus (your full range — all five of your beers completes it). the Hall + the Flight

Both happen at the Harbor — ship a cask to a kontor, or Enshrine one at the Hall — to different ends: go wide (many kontore, majorities) or go deep (the full quality range). You may pursue either as the board develops. Each kontor pays its own benefit on delivery: Bruges gives 2 goods (wants Q1), London lets you take a Building to your hand (Q2), Bergen grants a free Reach (+1 presence; Q2, the richest majority), and Novgorod lets you refine a maturing cask (+1 age) — and Novgorod's value scales with quality (Q3→2, Q4→4, Q5→6). The Hall pays prestige only.

7Your Brewery and the Floor

Your private board is your brewery: your vessels (start with two), the recipes you can brew (start with Gruit and Hopped; acquire exports for higher tiers), and a few small improvements. The big "get-ahead" pieces are now the buildings you place out on the slots (§3, §5).

The Floor — your private line

The casks held in your vessels power your Floor: instead of working the shared grid, you can run their actions privately — no fight for position. The more you've built (vessels, held casks), the stronger it gets. So a held cask earns its keep before it ships — and Extra Vessel finally pays.

Buildings — your public engine

Buy a building at the Market and place it on a slot, or earn one free by delivering to London. They make your line and your deliveries pay more: deliver → author a building → grow your board → deliver better. A value building marks its bonus with a reusable d6 demand die that rides the cask in the ship's berth — its pips are the ★ you bank on delivery (max 5); the die returns to the pool afterward (a stranded cask banks nothing). Transform buildings (Malt Kiln / Hop Yard) instead ride a +1-quality marker, and the ship value buildings (Rich Berth / Festkeller) resolve live when the ship sails.

8How a game ends — and how you win

There's a visible clock: the shared Sailed-Ships track. Every voyage adds one slot — a ship sailing, or a cask enshrined at the Hall; when it fills, the round finishes and the game ends. The more ships sail, the faster the game ends. A volume player can push to end it quickly; a prestige player keeps a steadier pace to get more delivering turns.

Then everyone tallies up from what they delivered:

Deliveries & majorities

Add up the value of your delivered casks at each destination (kontore = base + your value-buildings; the Hall = a fixed prestige ladder), plus majority bonuses (a big part of the score) — at each kontor, the players with the most casks score, ranked 1st/2nd/3rd (Bergen pays the most; in a 2-player game only 1st place scores).

Kontor deliveries (the demand)

Each delivered cask scores a small base + the value-buildings it shipped through — the demand you authored, banked as the demand die's pips (max 5) when the ship sails. Route your beer to where you've built, and a delivery can pay big. (Novgorod also scales its own value by quality: Q3→2, Q4→4, Q5→6.)

The Flight — your range

The Flight rewards how complete your range is: count the distinct beers you delivered — 3 → 4, 4 → 9, 5 (all your beers) → 16 (two beers, the free starters, score nothing). There are always five beer types (Gruit + Hopped + the 3 dealt exports), so the full flight is always reachable. Most total points wins.
