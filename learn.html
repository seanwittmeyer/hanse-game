Brewhouses of the Hanse

Learn the basics, then play.
2–5 players · ~45–60 min at 2p · v0.9 “The Wharf” · Rulebook & Components · Play

1The story

It's around 1350, and the Hanseatic League runs the trade of northern Europe. You are a merchant brewing house — a family firm in a League city. You buy grain and hops, brew casks of beer, and ship them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts — the kontore at Bergen, London, Bruges, and Novgorod.

Whoever has shipped the most valuable casks when the last ship sails wins. It all happens at the Wharf: you Source → Brew → Age → Ship, again and again, until the clock runs out.

3The stations — and how you move

The four steps are four stations in a shared 2×2. On your turn you move your worker one step to a neighbouring station, then fire that station's row or column — which triggers both stations on that line, plus any tiles in their two slots. You go where the work and the board are best — you're not just circling a track.

The production order is Market → Brewhouse → Cellar → Harbor, but you can't do all four in a turn — you choose which two actions (a line) to resolve and move where you need to. Stations are never closed, but moving onto a station a rival already occupies costs you 1 good — a gentle nudge to spread out (your opening placement is free).

Around the stations are 8 slots. Your Ready casks, your ships, and a couple of shared town buildings sit here; the buildings in the two slots on the line you fire give you bonus actions. (Stations + slots together are the Wharf.) More tiles on a line = bigger turns.

4Your Casks

Each brewed cask moves through three states:

① Maturing

In a vessel on your private board, ageing toward Ready. Safe — no rival can touch it here.

② On a slot

Once Ready, you set it out on a shared slot. Now it's two things: your cargo waiting for a ship, and a bonus action anyone who fires its line can use. Higher-quality beers give stronger actions.

③ Delivered

Loaded onto a ship and shipped to a destination — it scores there and leaves the board.

The key decision: leave a cask on its slot (keep using its action) or ship it now (score it, free the slot, before a rival ships it for you).

5Ships & the slots

You build and own your ships (at the Market) — a Cog carries 2 casks, a Hulk carries 3. Each is bound to a destination when you build it. You load a Ready cask from a slot onto a ship at the Harbor — or for free whenever a ship's line fires. The instant a ship is full, it sails: every cask aboard is delivered, and the ship is used up and moves to the Sailed-Ships track (the game's clock). Build another to ship again.

Charter Voyage. Ships get used up and the slots are tight, so the Harbor always offers a Charter: pay 2 goods to send one Ready cask (from a vessel or a slot) on a single trip now. It scores like any delivery and advances the clock — but it's only one cask, so owning ships is the more efficient option. The Charter is a backup option when your slots are jammed.

Shipping a Rival's Cask: on your turn you may load a rival's deployed cask (on a slot) onto your ship. They still score it (and pick its bonus) — but on your timing, and you freed the slot and advanced your ship. Your private brewery is always safe.

6Choosing Destinations

Every destination requires a certain quality of beer, and each pays differently. Kontore reward volume; the Hall rewards quality:

Volume

Ship to the kontore — Bruges, London, Bergen, Novgorod — for trade value and majorities (whoever delivers the most casks to a kontor scores its majority; Bergen pays by far the most). kontore
vs

Prestige

Send your finest to the Hall — a destination that pays prestige scaled to quality, outside of trade. the Hall

Both use the same action — ship a cask — to different destinations. You may pursue either approach as the board develops. Some destinations also hand you a brewery upgrade when you deliver.

7Your Brewery and Upgrades

Your private board is your brewery. It holds your vessels (you start with two; more vessels = more casks brewing at once), the recipes you can brew (you start with Gruit and Hopped; acquire export recipes to reach higher tiers), and your upgrades.

Rooms & modifiers

Upgrades like an Extra Vessel, an Aging Cellar (faster maturation, and unlocks the top beer), or perks like "when you gain hops, take an extra." Different players build different breweries.

Earned by delivering

You mostly earn upgrades by shipping: delivering to London or Novgorod lets you take one from the Market's face-up upgrade row at no goods cost (or pay to buy one). Delivering improves future turns: deliver → earn an upgrade → brew better → deliver better.

8How a game ends — and how you win

There's a visible clock: the shared Sailed-Ships track. Every ship that sails adds one slot; when it fills, the round finishes and the game ends. The more ships sail, the faster the game ends. A volume player can push to end it quickly; a prestige player keeps a steadier pace to get more delivering turns.

Then everyone tallies up from what they delivered:

Deliveries & majorities

Add up the value of your delivered casks at each destination (kontore = trade value; the Hall = prestige), plus majority bonuses — at each kontor, the players with the most casks score, ranked 1st/2nd/3rd (Bergen pays the most; in a 2-player game only 1st place scores).

Goals

You hold 3 goal tiles that reward different strategies — most distinct beers, most to one kontor, most ships sailed, and so on. Your best 2 score. Most total points wins.
