# Gatekeeper Review #2 — v4.4/v4.5, off human playtest #24 (2026-07-31)

> The standing review board (the `/brewhouse-gatekeeper` skill), convened by the designer after
> playtest #24 (2p, v4.4, round 20, human 98 vs Cellarmaster 74) with four felt problems on the
> table: scores high / banking unclear · buildings unexciting & dice under-manipulated · auto-aging
> devalues Age · actions/arcs/goals disconnected. v4.5 ("Empty Tray": tray-commitment trigger,
> 12-die pool) shipped between the playtest and this review. Evidence base: the full #24
> diagnostic dump + the canonical docs; sim reads are marked as such. No fabricated table data.

## 1 · The verdict

**Not yet — a pass at this build, from three of the four gatekeepers, and the reasons converge on
one sentence: the game is named for a component it barely lets you play.** The die-is-the-cask
spine is a genuinely ownable signature — no comp has it — but in the current build exactly ONE
building touches a die, aging happens to you automatically, and a third of the winning scores
passed through logistics (commissions, +3★ builds) rather than beer. Playtest #24's board tells
the story: eight slots fully built, six hulls docked, FIVE of them empty at round 20, the
Cellarmaster's bank (30★) out-earning its deliveries (19★). The chassis is close, the teach is
clean, and the human's winning line (Kiln'd Bocks through Novgorod/Bergen) was real, legible
skill — but the decision surface the pitch promises is not yet on the table. The fixes are
cheap and mostly additive; this is a "yes after the dice pass," not a redesign.

**The banking question, answered plainly (it was asked directly):** a delivery banks the die's
pips **once**, at the moment it parks — Novgorod's +2 is added at that same moment. The parked
die is never scored again for its pips; thereafter its *body* counts for the end-game majorities,
presence, and the clock. There is no double-dip. "Bank" on the score table = the OTHER in-play
income: +3★ per building raised, ★=berths per commission, 1★ per bump. The confusion is
structural, not the reader's fault: two different kinds of income share one word — see §8.4.

## 2 · The panel

**The Critic — NO.** The signature idea deserves to exist; the building deck doesn't. Six of ten
designs are *echo verbs* — Granary is a worse Market, Mission Quay a worse Cellar, the Annex a
worse Brewhouse; they add line-efficiency, not decisions. The one exciting tile (Malt Kiln) is
exciting precisely because it's the only one that touches the die. And auto-aging turns the
game's central resource into a countdown timer: dice ready themselves while you do something
else. *Flips my vote:* a building/specialist set where most tiles manipulate dice, and aging
that is chosen, not ambient.

**The Buyer — YES, on a condition.** "Your beer is a die: it ages up, sails away, and lands
showing its points — and your dice are the whole game clock" is a one-sentence hook I can sell
to someone who owns both comps; the empty-tray clock (v4.5) makes it even cleaner. The condition:
the demo has to *show* dice being cranked, swapped, and cashed, or the hook writes a check the
table doesn't cash. Component count is small, box cost sane, niche theme real. *Flips my vote
to a hard no:* shipping with the pitch-promise gap open.

**The Optimizer — NO at 2p, leaning yes at the system's ceiling.** The human's win was legible
(name the line: Kiln + gate-4 Novgorod at +2 = 6–8★ a cask — that IS skill converting), and the
empty-tray clock now rewards runway management. But: (1) the **commission mint** — 1G → 2–3★,
repeatable, no beer required — is a dominant-adjacent line the CM half-fell into (bank 30 >
deliv 19), and it *floods the wharf with hulls*, which (2) dissolves the **berth race**, the
game's one big interaction: with six docked hulls at 2p nobody ever races for a berth, and the
game drifts multiplayer-solitaire. (3) Goods are not starved (the human hit storage-full three
times in the last stretch); a loose economy caps the depth read. *Flips my vote:* commission ★
re-priced, hull scarcity restored, and one economy notch tighter.

**The Bridge Player — YES.** One action per station, the die as the single number to track, a
clean first game, and the app teach is now genuinely short. My complaint is the designer's own:
mid-game I don't know *what I'm working toward* — the majorities are invisible until the end,
the Flight auto-completes (both seats maxed it in #24 — a wash, not a race), and the load bonus
my cask happens to carry was dealt to me. Give me a visible order to chase and I'm all the way
in. *Flips my vote higher:* printed mid-game goals.

**The spread:** Critic no / Buyer yes — the classic *hook with a hollow core* pattern. That's
the headline: the theme and clock will sell the first table; the dice-decision surface has to
be built before the second table sticks.

## 3 · Strengths (mechanical, not vibes)

- **The die-as-cask unification** is real design capital: maturation, gate, value, presence,
  and clock in one component means every rules-read is a component-read. Nobody else owns this.
- **The empty-tray clock (v4.5)** is the right trigger: commitment (not parked-out) makes the
  runway a resource you *manage* instead of a fuse you watch, and it's read straight off the
  tray in front of you. The #24 stall (dice stranded on unfilled hulls, end untriggerable) is
  structurally cured.
- **The Kiln→Novgorod line proves the skill ceiling exists.** 45 delivery points on lifted dice
  through the gate-4 port was a *plan*, executed over many turns, visible to the opponent. This
  is the game the whole build should be pointing at.
- **Commission + free load + the Skute** is elegant tempo — the old charter subsystem as two
  components and zero rules.
- **The fee-rides-the-item economy** (v4.2) reads off the tile and prices the shortcuts without
  a rules table. Keep it.

## 4 · Weaknesses and cut corners

1. **The commission mint** (must-fix). ★=berths for 1G, repeatable, uncapped, beer-optional. It
   inflates scores, funds a beer-free line, and — worse — every minted hull *reduces* berth
   scarcity, killing the berth race that is supposed to carry interaction. #24's end state
   (5 empty hulls docked) is the mechanism failing on camera.
2. **Echo-verb buildings** (must-fix, the designer's felt "not exciting"). A building whose
   action is a smaller copy of an adjacent station adds routing convenience, not a decision.
   The deck's center of mass must move to the die.
3. **Auto-aging** (must-fix, the "no intention" root). +1/turn ambient aging means ready-dates
   arrive on rails; the Cellar visit is a leftover, Age+2 bonuses fire into "nothing to age,"
   and the tempo skill of *timing a cask against a hull* is mostly pre-decided. It also feeds
   the goods/score flood by making every brew cheap in actions.
4. **No mid-game arcs.** Majorities are end-game and low-visibility; the Flight is a
   both-players-max-it wash at 2p; prizes go *dead* late (Bruges "holds every recipe → 2 goods"
   fired repeatedly in #24; Bergen prize downgraded twice in one sail once the 4-tile specialist
   deck emptied). Between rounds ~8 and the clock, the game offers no printed "this is what I'm
   chasing."
5. **The load bonus is dealt, not chosen** — drawn at brew from a randomly-refreshing pile top.
   Steerable in name, random in feel; it's the largest single source of the "random" feeling
   named in the debrief.
6. **2p specialist supply is literally thin**: n−1 = 1 copy each = a 4-tile deck that empties
   by mid-game and takes the Bergen prize's texture with it.
7. **Score-word collision**: "bank" covers both beer income and infrastructure income; the
   totals (98/74 at 2p) read high and opaque. (The click-for-breakdown modal helps the app;
   the fix for the *game* is shrinking the infrastructure share — see §8.)

## 5 · The depth dial

**Too thin:** the building deck (as decisions), the specialist roster (as a supply and as
powers), mid-game goals. **Too loose (the inverse of too punishing):** the goods economy and
the commission mint — nothing in #24 ever *hurt*; the game currently under-punishes rather than
over-punishes, which is the more dangerous side for this weight class. **Where skill converts:**
lift-and-gate routing (proven), runway management (new, promising), berth timing (currently
drowned by hull flood). **Is loss legible?** For the winner, yes. For the loser — partially:
"I commissioned and built while he shipped lifted Bocks" is nameable, but the *system* validated
the losing line by paying it 30★, which muddies the lesson. Tighten the mint and the loss
becomes fully diagnosable — that's the depth-pull threshold.

## 6 · The comp face-off

**vs Great Western Trail:** Brewhouses **wins** on clock elegance (empty tray vs GWT's abstract
end-tile), component unification, and teach length. It **loses** — decisively, today — on what
buildings mean: GWT's buildings re-author the shared track and create arcs (each loop is a plan);
Brewhouses' buildings mostly discount adjacent stations. It **draws** on delivery-as-spine.
*Why keep Brewhouses, shelve GWT?* — today, only theme and length. After the dice pass + orders
(§8), the honest answer becomes "same legible-loss depth in 60 fewer minutes, on a component
GWT doesn't have."

**vs Distilled:** Brewhouses **wins** on determinism (no card-flip on the sale) and on the
clock. It **loses** on product identity — Distilled's signature spirits make every batch feel
authored; Brewhouses' casks differ by number and a dealt bonus. The recipes-as-characters gap
is real but is *expansion-shaped* (the tabled Specialty Beers module is exactly this fix).
**Critical finding: Brewhouses is currently at risk of being *less deep* than Distilled** —
the five-alarm threshold in the dossier — because Distilled's build-a-batch choices outnumber
a v4.4 turn's real choices. The §8 program (aging-as-choice + dice buildings + orders) flips
this: those three each add a coupled decision to every mid-game turn.

## 7 · Pull diagnosis

Intended engine: **depth pull** (the docs say so — legible scoring, the squeeze, "no pure path
wins"). Actual engine at v4.4: drifting toward **comfort pull** — a loose economy, ambient
aging, points-for-logistics, low 2p interaction: absorbing, never stinging. That drift is why
the table felt "disconnected and random": comfort-tuned subsystems (auto-age, dealt bonuses,
mint) sitting under a depth-tuned spine (gates, lifts, majorities) — subsystems at war with
the intended engine. The fix direction is all one way: tighten, choose, print the goal. The
theme hook is strong enough that the variety-pull trap (sell on theme, live on expansions) is
a real commercial temptation — resist it until the depth core is proven; the expansion seams
are already cut and waiting.

## 8 · The path forward

**MUST-FIX before the next table (cheap, this week):**

1. **Kill the commission mint.** Commission ★ = **berths − 1** (Skute 0 · Cog 1 · Hulk 2) ⚙.
   Commissioning becomes logistics with a garnish, not an income line; hull flood self-corrects
   because a hull you won't load is now nearly pointless. *Test:* winner bank ≤ ~25% of total;
   ≤2 empty docked hulls at game end; sim seat-spread holds.
2. **Cut auto-aging.** Dice turn only when something turns them: the Cellar (raise to **4
   points** ⚙ to compensate), Mission Quay, Age+2 bonuses, and the aging specialists. Age
   becomes a station you *route for*; ready-timing vs a hull becomes a decision; Age+2 bonuses
   stop firing into thin air; the score flood slows. This is the single change that most
   restores "intention." *Test:* Cellar/Age line-share rises materially in sim + at the table;
   pace stays ≥12 rounds (the 12-die pool is the companion dial — 13 if too fast).
3. **The dice pass — rebuild the building deck's center of mass around the die.** Keep the four
   load-lifts; replace most echo-verbs (Granary ×1, Mission Quay ×1, the Annex) with
   manipulators, e.g. ⚙: **Racking Hall** (slot stop: swap the dice of two of your maturing
   casks), **Assay House** (slot stop: turn one maturing die ±1, never above quality — the
   Gauger returns as die-surgery), **Hop Exchange** (on load here: pay 1H → die +1, cap 6 —
   a second, *priced* lift that stacks a route with a Kiln), **Tollhouse** (on load here: die
   −1 → bank +2★ — cash pips for track, a real fork). Each is one line, one icon, read off the
   tile. *Test:* verify checks per tile; sim crash-free; at the table, count die-manipulations
   per game (#24's count was ~2; target: most turns touch one).
4. **Specialists: supply + dice powers.** 2p deck = **2 copies/type** ⚙ (n, not n−1). Add two
   dice-facing designs ⚙ — **Braumeister** (each of your turns: +1 age point to one maturing
   die — the *earned* replacement for the cut auto-age) and keep Cellarman/Stevedore; Grain
   Factor/Hop Gardener stay the economy pair. *Test:* Bergen prize never goes dead before the
   clock at 2p.

**WOULD-ELEVATE (the next design pass, before wide playtesting):**

5. **Ladings (order tiles) — the arc the game is missing.** A small deck, face-up row of 3 ⚙:
   each names a kontor + a condition + a reward ("**Bergen · die 5+ → 4★**", "**Novgorod ·
   Bock → 5★**", "**deliver the same beer to two kontore → 3★**"). Claimed on a qualifying
   delivery, refill at end of turn. This prints the mid-game goal, directs brewing/aging/routing
   (intention), makes the dealt load-bonus randomness matter less, and shifts score weight from
   flat mint to earned targets. It is also the legibility answer: "I lost the Bergen lading by
   one age point" is a nameable loss. *Test:* a table where both players can state their
   current target unprompted.
7. **Pile-top agency.** Cheapest version: the brew pile shows the top **two** bonuses, brewer
   picks ⚙ — randomness becomes a draft, Orléans-style authored variance. *Test:* the "random"
   complaint disappears from debriefs.
8. **Dead-prize patch:** Bruges with all recipes held pays **1 age point + 1 good** ⚙ (stays
   on-theme, stays alive); Bergen with an empty display pays a bump die instead of goods.

**NICE-TO-HAVE:** Novgorod +2 re-read after the mint nerf (it drove 20 of the winner's 45 —
correct, but watch it becoming the only line); Flight differentiation at 2p (both maxing is a
wash — consider exports-only counting ⚙); a printed "what I'm worth" recap card.

**If Sean does exactly one thing: cut auto-aging (item 2).** It is the keystone touching every
felt complaint at once — Age regains intention, the dice become something you *do* rather than
watch, the aging buildings and Cellarman/Braumeister become exciting on contact, the score
flood slows, and the ready-vs-hull timing race that the whole spine was built for finally
happens on the table. Items 1 and 3 are its natural same-week companions.

**What only a table can settle:** pace at 12 dice without auto-age (sim bots don't hold dice);
lading magnitudes; whether commission at berths−1 still gets picked often enough to keep the
ship market turning. Everything else above is testable in the harness first.
