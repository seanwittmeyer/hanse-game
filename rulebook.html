1The Brewing House

Hamburg was called the brewhouse of the Hanse. You are a merchant brewing house in the League, c. 1350.

The pivotal innovation of your age is hopped beer. Unlike perishable gruit ale, hopped beer survives a sea voyage — so beer becomes a shippable export. You will source grain and hops, brew casks, mature them, and push them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts (the kontore: Bergen, London, Bruges, Novgorod) — or withdraw your finest into the brewers' Hall.

The whole game is one production loop you walk over and over — Source → Brew → Age → Ship — getting a little stronger each lap. Whoever has shipped the most valuable casks when the last ship sails wins.

2Core Loop

Everything you do is one loop, walked on a shared grid:

Source → Brew → Age → Ship → (repeat)

Each turn you brew casks, mature them, and ship them to destinations for points. Where you ship is the key decision:

  • VOLUME — ship to the kontore (Bruges, London, Bergen, Novgorod) for trade value and majorities. Kontore reward volume.
  • PRESTIGE — send your finest to the Hall, out of commerce, for prestige scaled to quality. The Hall rewards quality.

Both use the same verb — ship a cask — to different destinations. You may pursue either approach as the board develops. They compete for the same casks, wharf slots, ships, and end clock.

Three rules constraints

No dice (fully deterministic) · No hidden draws (everything you acquire is taken from a face-up supply, never a blind deck) · No money (goods are the only currency; there is no spendable prestige — delivered points are score only).

3Components

  • 1 Main board — the 2×2 action grid (A Market · B Brewhouse · D Cellar · C Harbor), the 8 perimeter slots = the wharf, and the shared Sailed-Ships track (the end clock).
  • 1 Destination board — the four kontore (Bruges · London · Bergen · Novgorod) and the Hall, each with its quality gate, delivery value, benefit, and (kontore) a majority track. Delivered cask tiles sit at their destination.
  • 5 Player brewery boards — each private & untouchable: vessel lanes (start 2, cap 4), the maturation track (Brew → … → Ready), a recipe rack, upgrade slots (Rooms + Modifiers), and goods storage (cap 8).
  • The tile supply (see the visualizer):
    • Cask tiles — single working face: type · quality · a signature action; brewed from a shared pool with fixed counts (~L1×16 · L2×20 · L3×12 · L4×8 · L5×4); a colored ownership disc marks a cask once it's on the wharf or delivered.
    • Ship tiles (Cog · Hulk), neutral building tiles, recipe tiles, upgrade tiles (Rooms + Modifiers), and goal tiles.
  • Goods cubes — grain (G) and hops (H), the only currency.
  • Per player: 1 worker pawn, ownership discs. 1 first-player marker (fixed — it does not pass).

4Anatomy of the Game

The Action Grid — the Loop

Four action cells in a 2×2: A Market (Source) & B Brewhouse (Brew) on the top row, C Harbor (Ship) & D Cellar (Age) on the bottom. The clockwise circuit A→B→D→C walks the loop Source→Brew→Age→Ship. Each cell belongs to one row and one column — a line — and every line pairs two adjacent loop-steps (Market+Brewhouse, Brewhouse+Cellar, Harbor+Cellar, Market+Harbor). Around the grid sit 8 perimeter slots, two capping each line.

The Wharf (the perimeter ring)

The 8 slots are the shared commons. A line is a 4-stop pipeline: cap slot · cell · cell · cap slot. Each slot belongs to exactly one line. The wharf holds your deployed casks (public action-buildings + cargo), your ships, and 2–3 neutral buildings seeded at setup. (Recipes & upgrades live on your private board, not a slot.)

The Destinations (where value lives)

The four kontore radiate from the home port; the Hall is the prestige destination. All are open from the start. Each wants a certain quality of beer and pays differently — so there is no single best line of play:

Your Brewery (private, untouchable)

Your brewery board holds the maturation track, which carries casks Brew → … → Ready across your vessels (start 2, cap 4 — each a parallel lane); a recipe rack holds the types you may brew; and upgrade slots hold the Rooms & Modifiers you build (mostly earned by delivering). Rivals can only ever act on your casks once they're on the wharf.

5Setup

  1. Place the main board and destination board. Seed the wharf with 2–3 neutral buildings ⚙ and the warm-start Cogs, spread across different lines so no single line is the obvious opening. All five destinations begin open.
  2. Deal the export roster (Bock / Mumme / Broyhan / Keut) onto the L3–L5 rungs in a random order ⚙. Recipes & ships are an always-available Market supply; for upgrades, shuffle all Upgrade tiles into a face-down deck and deal 4 ⚙ face-up beside it — the Upgrade display. The deck stays face-down as a face-down draw stack and refills the display as tiles are taken (§11).
  3. Set the Sailed-Ships track to its player-scaled length (it scales steeply — the clock fills faster with more players): ~7 / 11 / 15 / 19 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 players ⚙.
  4. Each player takes a brewery board, a worker, ownership discs, and a starting stake ⚙: 3 , 2 , the Gruit + Hopped recipes (printed), 2 open vessels (lanes 3–4 locked), and a warm start — one built Cog in a wharf slot bound to Bruges, and one Ready Gruit in Vessel 1 (deploy it turn 1).
  5. Each player places their worker on any cell. Give the youngest brewer the first-player marker.

Warm start

Starting with 2 vessels, a built Cog, and a Ready cask means a first voyage is reachable by turn 2–3.

6A Turn

Turn 1 only: place your worker on any cell and activate one of its two lines (no move). From turn 2, in order:

  1. Move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent cell (A↔B, A↔C, B↔D, C↔D — never the diagonal). Mandatory.
  2. Activate the row OR the column of your worker's new cell.
  3. Resolve the line's up-to-4 stops — cap · cell · cell · cap — in any order:
    • Each cell: take its action. Cells are never blocked — a rival's worker on a cell does not stop you. Players interact through wharf slots and loaded casks.
    • Each cap slot: if it holds a building, you may use its action — a cask's signature action, a ship's free load, or a neutral building. All resolve on your turn, for you; there are no out-of-turn gains.

Both cells always fire

Activating a line triggers both of its cells. Your worker's position only determines which line is legal. Because base verbs are always available and wharf buildings only add, you are never stalled waiting for the board to align — a populated ring makes your turns bigger.

Play passes clockwise. When every player has taken a turn, the round ends and a new round begins. The first player is fixed for the whole game — turn order does not rotate (one fewer thing to track). Any first-player edge will be handled by seat compensation ⚙ (e.g. extra starting goods for later seats) — TBD.

8The Cask & Brewing

Cask tiles have two roles: they mature privately, then serve as both cargo and a public action-building on the wharf. Each cask moves through three states:

  1. Maturing (private, in a vessel) — brewed at step 0, ageing toward Ready. Safe; scores nothing.
  2. On the wharf (public, a shared slot) — once Ready, deploy it to an open slot you choose. It is now your cargo-in-waiting and a public action-building whose signature action fires when its line runs (for whoever runs it). If the wharf is full, a Ready cask remains in its vessel until a slot opens.
  3. Delivered (scored, gone) — loaded onto a ship and shipped to a destination, where it scores for its owner and leaves. A cask is only ever one state; shipping converts it.

Brewing — types & quality progression

Casks are brewed, not bought, from a shared pool with fixed global counts — the rare export casks are a contested resource, and a player's cap is "what's left to brew." Beer has two layers: a type (a quality rung) and a recipe (permission to brew it, on your rack). A loaded recipe crawls your maturation track — casks age 1 step automatically every turn, and the Cellar gives a pool of advance points to push them faster, so more vessels = more throughput.

The first two beer types are fixed; higher tiers vary by game. L1 Gruit → L2 Hopped is fixed and historical (hops is the preservation tech that opens the sea routes). The export tiers L3–L5 are dealt each game from the historical Hanse export beers — Bock (Einbeck), Mumme (Braunschweig), Broyhan (Hannover), Keut (Low Countries) — so the most efficient quality progression differs every game.

Which type to brew

Each type carries a different signature action and reaches different destinations. Gruit is cheap and fast but only reaches Bruges; export types are slow and scarce but unlock the richer destinations and the Hall.

9The Wharf

The 8 perimeter slots hold your deployed casks (cargo + a public action), your ships (single-use; consumed when they sail), and 2–3 neutral buildings seeded at setup. Every slot belongs to exactly one line. (Recipes & upgrades live on your board, not a slot.)

When a line fires, the active player may use each building on it: a cask fires its signature action; a ship offers a free load; a neutral building gives its base action (e.g. Market Stall +1 good, Cooper advance a cask, Crane a free load). All resolve on the active player's turn — cask & neutral actions are free and public. When a rival uses your cask's action, you still own the cask and will still score it when shipped.

Shipping a rival's cask

On your turn you may load a rival's wharf cask onto your ship (only wharf casks — never their brewery). When it sails, the destination is your ship's binding (it must be legal for that cask's quality). The owner still scores the delivery and picks its benefit, and you take the freed slot, the filled ship (advancing the end clock), and a small loader bonus ⚙.

10Ships & Destinations

Ships — owned, single-use carriers

  1. Build (at the Market): a ship goes into a wharf slot, bound to a destination, with capacity Cog 2 / Hulk 3 ⚙, inheriting that destination's quality gate.
  2. Load (the Harbor verb, always available — or a bonus free load when the ship's line fires): put one wharf cask aboard that meets the gate — your own or a rival's. (A cask must be deployed to the wharf first; the Charter is the only way to ship straight from a vessel.)
  3. Sail (the instant it is full, or you launch it early): every cask aboard is delivered to the bound destination (it scores for its owner), and the ship tile is consumed onto the Sailed-Ships track — single-use; build another to ship again.

Charter Voyage

The Harbor always offers a Charter (no tile — an outside hull): pay 2 G ⚙ to send one Ready cask — from a vessel or the wharf — on an immediate single-cask voyage to a destination it qualifies for. It delivers normally (scores + the destination benefit) and advances the Sailed-Ships clock like any voyage. Less efficient than using your own ship per cask, so owning ships is the better approach — but a Charter prevents you from being stuck when the wharf is full with no ship of your own or a Ready cask is blocking a vessel.

Destinations

All destinations are open from the start; they differ by a quality gate (higher-value destinations require better beer) and a unique benefit (see §4). The kontore pay trade value + majorities; the Hall pays prestige for taking a cask out of commerce. Most delivered casks at a kontor = its majority bonus (Bergen rewards this most). The cask's owner always scores the delivery and picks any offered benefit — even when a rival shipped it.

Delivery Benefits

Delivering to London / Bergen / Novgorod hands you a brewery upgrade from the display at no goods cost. The loop reinforces itself: deliver → earn an upgrade → brew better → deliver better. You must still brew to have casks to load.

11The Brewery & Upgrades

Your brewery is private and safe. It holds your vessels (start 2, cap 4 — throughput), your recipes, and your upgrades. Upgrades improve your brewery and are where strategies diverge. They come from the Market's face-up Upgrade display (§7) — a row of 4 ⚙ tiles dealt from a shuffled face-down Upgrade deck (the draw stack), which refills the row from its top as tiles are taken: buy one (pay its goods cost) or earn one free by delivering to London / Bergen / Novgorod (§10). Two families — Rooms (capability) and Modifiers (asymmetric perks).

Buying Upgrades (4–5 G)

Delivering to London / Bergen / Novgorod hands you a display tile free — on top of the voyage's delivery value and majority. Buying yields only the upgrade, so it is priced at a comparable 4–5 G ⚙ (about a kontor delivery's worth of goods). Buying is a real option for bootstrapping or grabbing a specific tile, but the primary path is deliver → earn an upgrade → brew better.

Goals (§14) reward different brewery/delivery strategies, so the winning approach varies by game.

12Goods & Storage

grain and hops are the only currency — recipes, brew inputs, ships, upgrades, loader benefits. You may hold up to 8 goods ⚙ (raised by the Warehouse room). There is no money and no spendable prestige — delivered points are score only.

13End of the Game

The game ends when the Sailed-Ships track fills.

  • Every voyage — a ship sailing (full or launched) or a Charter (§10) — moves one slot onto the shared, visible Sailed-Ships track. When the track fills, the game ends. Track length scales with player count (the more players ship, the faster the clock fills): ~7 / 11 / 15 / 19 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 players ⚙. The more ships sail, the faster the game ends — a full Hulk's 3-cask burst still advances only one slot. A round limit (≈25 ⚙) prevents the game from running too long.

This is a visible, player-steerable clock: a volume player races to sail ships fast and end the game; a prestige player keeps a steadier pace to buy more delivering turns. When it fires, finish the current round so all players have had an equal number of turns, then score.

14Scoring

No separate reach/standing tracks. You score from what you delivered:

  1. Delivery value — sum your delivered casks' values at each destination (kontore = trade value; the Hall = prestige, scaling with quality).
  2. Majorities — the route-majority bonus at each kontor (most delivered casks; ties split ⚙).
  3. Goals — resolve your goal tiles; only your best few score ⚙.

Highest total wins. Tiebreak: most goods, then most casks on the wharf.

The goal pool (⚙)

A small pool (shared draft or per-player) rewards different strategies — most distinct types delivered, most casks to one kontor, most Q3+ delivered, most ships sailed, most to the Hall, most kontore held, a fullest brewery, a flat safe goal. Only your best few count. Goals are designed so a single-strategy approach fills few of them. See the visualizer for the full list.

152-Player Variant

With fewer workers the wharf feels loose, so lock some perimeter slots (down to ~6 ⚙) to tighten the slot competition that drives "ship a rival's cask to free a slot." The Sailed-Ships track is shortest at 2p (~6 slots ⚙), so the clock stays brisk. Everything else plays identically.

16Glossary

  • The loop — Source → Brew → Age → Ship, the four cells walked as a clockwise circuit.
  • Cell — one of the four shared action spaces (A Market · B Brewhouse · D Cellar · C Harbor).
  • Line — a row or column; a 4-stop pipeline. Firing one triggers both its cells plus the slots at each end.
  • Wharf — the 8 shared perimeter slots; holds deployed casks, ships, and neutral buildings.
  • Cask — a brewed beer that moves through three states: maturing (vessel) → on the wharf (cargo + a public action) → delivered (shipped → scores).
  • Deploy — place a Ready cask from your brewery onto an open wharf slot.
  • Ship — owned infrastructure (Cog 2 / Hulk 3) bound to a destination; loads casks and sails when full, then is consumed to the Sailed-Ships track.
  • Charter Voyage — a Harbor option (no tile): pay 2 G to ship one Ready cask (vessel or wharf) on a single-cask voyage now. Less efficient per cask than a Cog/Hulk, but prevents being stuck when the wharf is full or a vessel is blocked.
  • Destination / kontor / the Hall — where a ship delivers. Kontore pay trade value + majorities (volume); the Hall pays prestige (scarcity).
  • Majority — most delivered casks at a kontor; a scoring bonus.
  • Recipe — permission to brew a type (type + brew cost), on your rack. Start with Gruit + Hopped.
  • Upgrade — a Room (capability) or Modifier (asymmetric perk) on your brewery, mostly earned by delivering.
  • Neutral building — a shared base action seeded in the wharf at setup.
  • Sailed-Ships track — the shared end clock; each voyage fills one slot. When full → finish the round → score.

17Quick Reference

Your turn

  1. Move worker to an orthogonally adjacent cell (mandatory).
  2. Activate its row or column.
  3. Resolve 4 stops (cap · cell · cell · cap), any order. Both cells fire; cells are never blocked; you may use any wharf building on the line (on your turn).

The loop

Market (Source: goods/recipes/ships/upgrades) → Brewhouse (Brew a cask) → Cellar (Age to Ready → deploy to the wharf) → Harbor (Ship a cask onto a ship → sails when full → delivers).

Where to ship

Kontore = trade value + majorities (volume). The Hall = prestige (scarcity). Gate by quality; owner scores & picks the benefit even on a rival's ship.

Game ends

The Sailed-Ships track fills (each voyage = 1 slot) → finish the round → score: Deliveries (by destination) + Majorities + Goals (best few).
