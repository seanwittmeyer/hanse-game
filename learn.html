Brewhouse of the Hanse

Learn to play in one page — then go brew.
2–5 players · ~15–20 min each · Components · Full Rulebook · Play

1The story

It's around 1350, and the Hanseatic League runs the trade of northern Europe. You've just learned to brew with hops — and unlike old gruit ale, hopped beer survives a sea voyage. For the first time, beer is something you can export.

You are a merchant brewing house: a family firm in a League city. You'll buy grain and hops, brew casks, ship them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts — the kontore at Bergen, London, Bruges, and Novgorod — and build a name for your house.

2Your goal — and the one big choice

You win with the most points, and there are two ways to earn them that pull against each other. Every cask you brew poses the same question:

Reach

Deploy the cask out into commerce — it spreads your presence across the trade routes to the kontore. The path of scale & volume.
vs

Standing

Enshrine the cask in the Hall — take it out of commerce forever as pure reputation. The path of scarcity & prestige.

You can't do both with the same cask, and your space and time are tight — so the game is about how far you lean toward reach or standing (or thread between them). That single tension is the whole game.

3How you get there — the loop

Everything flows through four actions. Think of it as a little factory:

Take grain & hops, or buy a tile (a trade route, a ship, an upgrade) or collect a recipe card (which pays a big one-time boon).

Load a recipe and brew it down your track: cheap gruit is fast; hopped beer takes longer but travels & can be enshrined.

Deploy a finished cask: straight to Bruges, or load it on a ship bound for a far kontor. It becomes a presence marker — your reach. reach

4

Hall

Or enshrine a cask (a fresh one, or one of yours already deployed) into the Hall — out of commerce, pure standing.

The rhythm: the Market and Brewhouse build you up; the Harbor and Hall cash you out. Acquire → brew → then deploy for reach or enshrine for standing.

The market is the timer. Each beer type has a value. When you enshrine a cask you bank that value as standing — but every cask of a type that's sold (deployed or enshrined) nudges its value down, while buying its recipe or paying at a Fair nudges it up. So the whole reach-vs-standing choice becomes a question of timing: cash your type while it's high, before rivals flood it.

4The grid — where you act

All four actions sit on a shared 2×2 grid. On your turn you move your worker to a neighbouring cell, then fire that cell's row or column — which triggers both cells on that line. So a turn always does two things.

The two builders sit on one diagonal and the two cash-outs on the other, so they never share a line. The neat result: every line pairs one builder with one cash-out — and builders pump the market while cash-outs realize it, so you can pump a type then enshrine it high in one turn. Reach or standing each turn, never both at once.

Around the grid are 8 slots. Your deployed casks live here (that's your reach), alongside route lanes, ships (single-use carriers to the far kontore), and Fairs. Here's the spicy part: any player can enshrine a deployed cask on a Hall line — they grab the slot, you get the standing (but less than you were holding out for). So slots keep churning, and "you're taking my space" becomes a real, friendly tug-of-war. (Recipe cards you collect don't go in a slot — their brew strips tuck under the bottom edge of your board.)

5Your player board — the brewery

The grid is shared; your brewery board is yours alone. It's where brewing actually happens and where you specialise.

Brew & vessels

Casks crawl your track (Load→Ferment→Age→Ready). Beer ages a step on its own every turn, and a Brewhouse action gives you advance points to push brews along faster (allocate them however you like). You start with one vessel and can build up to three — more vessels = more casks brewing at once. But an Extra Vessel is a Room, so going wide eats a Floor slot.

The Floor & twins

Your Brewhouse Floor has just four multi-use slots, each holding a Room (permanent) or a working Cask (temporary) — so every parked engine-cask is a room you didn't build. Installing a Ready cask as working is free but needs an open Floor slot — if the Floor is full, that cask clogs its vessel. Rooms also power your twins — private fallbacks for Market/Harbor/Brewhouse when a rival blocks the public cell.

6How a game ends — and how you win

The game has a visible clock: the shared Sailed-Ships track. Every ship that sails adds one slot to it, and when it fills, the round finishes and the game is over — the more the table ships, the sooner it ends, so it's self-accelerating. (A backstop catches a slow table: once a player-scaled total number of casks have been enshrined, that ends it instead. Whichever fires first.) So a reach player can race to end it by sailing ships fast, while a standing player keeps a steadier pace to buy more enshrining turns.

Then everyone tallies up — and you score on every axis you touched:

The two big piles

Reach = your presence (deployed casks) × each route's value, plus majority bonuses for leading a route. Standing = your standing track — the market value you banked each time you enshrined.

The bonuses

Goals — each enshrined cask carries a goal (your best 3 score). The trick: goals reward the axis you didn't bank, so they nudge you to blend reach and standing. Most total points wins.

The trick: goals reward the axis you didn't just bank — a pure-standing hoard draws goals that need reach to pay off. That's the game nudging you to blend the two rather than tunnel one.

And the words:

Hanse / KontorThe Hanseatic League — the medieval trade network. A kontor is one of its foreign trading posts (Bergen, London, Bruges, Novgorod).
Gruit vs. HoppedTwo beers. Gruit is cheap, fast, local — and can never be enshrined. Hopped beer takes longer to brew but travels and earns standing.
Goods grain and hops. Your only currency — spent on tiles and brewing. (There is no money in this game.)
CaskA brewed beer (a double-sided tile) that lives in one of three states: working (installed in a Brewhouse Floor slot and assigned to a station, which it soups up whenever you use it), deployed (a board slot or aboard a ship = your reach), or enshrined (the Hall = standing).
LineA row or column of the grid. Activating one fires both its cells plus the slots at each end.
SlotOne of the 8 spaces around the grid, holding a deployed cask, a route lane, a ship, or a Fair. When its line fires it does its thing (action, skim, load a ship, or pump).
PresenceYour markers along a trade route. More presence on higher-value routes = more reach points.
StandingEarned, unspendable reputation from enshrined casks. It only ever goes up — it's score.
EnshrineMove a cask (fresh, or one already deployed) onto the standing stack for permanent standing. Any player can enshrine a deployed cask on a Hall line — the owner always gets the points.
Route & quality gateThe four sea lanes. Richer, farther routes (like Novgorod) only accept higher-quality beer.
GoalA scoring condition printed on a cask's standing face — it rewards you at game end for the shape of your empire.
TwinThe private version of a grid action on your brewery board, used when a rival blocks the public cell.
Recipe cardsCollected at the Market — each pays a big one-time boon, then its brew strip tucks under your board's bottom edge as a permanent recipe. You can only brew a type you hold a strip for; Gruit is always brewable. Soft cap of 6 strips.
Demand marketEach beer type's current value. Enshrining banks that value as standing. It falls as a type is sold (deploy/enshrine) and rises on recipe-buys and Fair pumps — so timing matters.
HallCell C — where you enshrine casks for standing (the prestige sink). Distinct from the kontore, the reach destinations your Harbor ships to.
