## 1 · The verdict

**Yes — conditional, and for the first time the conditions are dials and validation, not
structure.** This is the strongest board this game has had. The v4.5 board's headline —
"named for a component it barely lets you play" — is closed: the die is now manipulated by
9 of 17 buildings, three station-adjacent verbs, two specialists, and an order row that
prices every face, and the data shows the manipulation *matters* (die-6+ deliveries land in
77% of games; the lading row is the strongest win-signal ever measured in this design at
+20 to +37 points of win rate). Lane fairness at 4p is the best ever measured (28/28/23/21
against fair 25). Loss is legible, the trap lines are self-punishing rather than
rule-banned, and the exploit ledger has stayed closed across ~2,700 sim games. What keeps
the yes conditional: **4p pace sits outside the game's own 12–25 band half the time**
(11.9-round average — the one spec the build violates), **the hops economy flipped from
slack to binding in a single day of letters** (10–18 blocked-want events per game,
human-unvalidated), **the marquee beer brews at 5–8%** (Bock is now an event, not a line —
which must be a decision, not an accident), and **the BOM/price question has been open
since v3.2d, two boards ago.** None of these is a redesign. All of them are checkable
within one human table and one spreadsheet.

**The delta verdict (did the #24 program land?):** Yes, measurably. Every v4.5 must-fix
shipped and did what it claimed: the mint is cut and commission-spam now *loses* (26–34%
win rate vs baseline — the trap became a lesson); auto-age is cut and aging is a real
axis (the Cellar/Abbey/Braumeister economy is the second-most-used verb family); the dice
pass and the order row created the mid-game arc the #24 table missed (ladings ≥6★ win
48–65% of their games). The letters then went further than the program: the power ladder,
the recipe tariff, the aging law, the open floor. The one letter that shipped
validation-light (v45h) was re-validated for this review and holds.

## 2 · The panel

**The Critic — yes, the first unqualified "it earns its weight" from this seat.** The die
grammar is now a genuine signature: one component is maturation, gate, value, presence,
and clock, and the game finally *plays* that sentence — rack it, assay it, pump it with
hops, stamp it at the tollhouse, launder it from a Bock into a Gruit. The order row is the
Hall's death answered properly: a variable economy in fifteen tiles instead of a board.
Two reservations, both identity-grade. First, **Bock at 5–8% of brews**: a brewing game
whose best beer is nearly extinct under competent-but-greedy play is either making a
statement ("the masterwork is rare") or hiding a tuning miss — the designer must *name*
which, because the box art will promise Bock. Second, **the Assay's minus is rules-text
without a use**: 0.0 uses across every corpus including the MC tiers that sample it.
*Flips to strong yes when:* the Bock identity is named, and the ▼ either finds its play
or leaves the tile.

**The Buyer — closest to stock in five boards.** The pitch survived every streamline:
"your barrel is a die — brew it, age it, ship it through a harbor your rivals also use,"
and now the teach has two sentences I can actually use on a floor: *grain buys
infrastructure, hops buy beer* and *the prize is the pick, never the waiver.* The terse
tile pass matters commercially — the components finally read like a produced game. Two
blocks: a **4p con demo that ends in 11 rounds** under-delivers the weight promise
(GWT-weight buyers count rounds), and **the BOM question from v3.2d has never been
re-run** — v4 cut the Hall, 12 privileges, contracts, cubes, and 32 tokens while adding 15
lading tiles, so the math has almost certainly *improved*, but "almost certainly" is not a
number. *Flips when:* one clocked con cycle at 4p in-band, and the BOM sheet re-run
against the v4.5h manifest.

**The Optimizer — yes, on the numbers.** The full ladder passes at scale for the first
time since v4.3 (trader>journeyman 60.8% at n=120; GM>trader 89.6% pooled; CM>GM 70.8%
pooled), the chaos runs neither collapse nor converge, and the deep lines are real: the
quality persona played greedily wins 17–22%, played with search wins ~90% — that spread
*is* the skill ceiling. No new exploit in ~2,700 games; the one degenerate-looking line
(hull spam) is self-punishing and nameable. Watchlist, not blockers: the hops triple-sink
could produce human stall-turns the bots don't feel (starved-turn rate 16–18% — fine for
bots, unknown for people), and 2p margins run wide (avg 13–16 — blowout-adjacent).
*Flips to strong yes when:* a human table confirms binding-hops reads as delicious, not
stuck.

**The Bridge Player — the best on-ramp this game has ever had.** Open floor from turn one
(no locked slots to explain), terse tiles speaking one icon grammar with the casks, a
3-seat default that hands a newcomer the designer's own line to watch (the quality GM) and
a nemesis to fear. Two first-game bruises: **"I won the Bruges prize and still have to
PAY?"** needs its teach sentence said out loud before the first delivery, and a new player
who spent all hops on brews will find the recipe row locked — the *why you bank hops*
lesson lands turn three, which is acceptable but should be in the teach. *Flips when:*
the two sentences are in the printed teach script.

**The spread:** four yeses of different strengths, gating on validation and identity
naming. No seat says "not yet." That has never happened in this archive.

## 3 · Strengths (mechanical, evidenced)

- **The order row is the mid-game arc, proven.** Lading ≥6★ correlates with winning at
  +20 to +37 points across both profile corpora — the strongest signal in the data — and
  claims run ~1–2.5/game against a row of 3: contested, not automatic. The v4.5 board's
  "no arcs between the opening and the final scramble" is answered by a component.
- **The trap teaches.** Three-plus commissions *lowers* win rate (26–34% vs baseline).
  A legible, self-punishing mistake is exactly the GWT-class lesson loop.
- **The capstone census is the right shape.** A 6★+ delivery in 77% of games (~3/game,
  the everyday firework), a 7–8★ Novgorod in 52% (the earned roar), a 5-beer flight in
  52% (the collector's arc), a Bonded send-off in 26% (the shared event), a 5★ lading in
  2% (the once-a-campaign story). One delight guaranteed, one legend rare.
- **Lane fairness at 4p: 28 / 28 / 23 / 21.** Best ever measured; no negation anywhere
  (the §8 pole test passes).
- **The currency grammar is teachable design.** Grain = infrastructure, hops = beer;
  every fee in the game follows one of two sentences. This is the kind of compression
  v3.4 asked for, achieved economy-wide.
- **The skill ceiling is demonstrated, not asserted.** Quality-greedy 17–22% vs
  quality-searched ~90% on the same values: mastery is worth ~70 points of win rate.

## 4 · Weaknesses and cut corners

- **4p pace violates the game's own band** — 11.6–11.9 avg, ~50% in 12–25. Greedy racers
  under-read human pace (standing caveat), but three letters pushed the same direction
  (pool 12, de-mint, open floor) and nothing pushed back. The pool sweep already says 13
  holds the band at every count.
- **The hops economy went from slack to binding in one day** (recipes Q−2 + Hop Exchange
  + Abbey, no faucet change). Blocked-want events 10–18/game. Bots route around it;
  humans might experience it as the good kind of hunger — or as a stall. Unvalidated.
- **Bock 5–8% of brews / Keut still last at ~8%.** The tariff did its job on the rush and
  possibly half a job too many on the style. (Keut note: its fee *improved* at v45e; its
  problem is the 2-step aging against Broyhan's 1 — a structure question parked since the
  flow probe first flagged it.)
- **Assay ▼: 0.0 uses everywhere.** Ruled, implemented, printed — and inert.
- **Racking/Tollhouse live on the GM's search and the human imagination** (0.0–0.3 and
  ~0.1–0.2 uses/game under greedy). Defensible for qty-1 power tiles — but two human games
  where nobody racks means the launder engine needs a printed example, and that test
  hasn't run.
- **Validation velocity.** Eight KEY letters in ~48 hours; the full oracle trailed the
  live build most of the day. This review closed the gap, but the process debt was real —
  the v45h letter shipped on a 6-game crash check.
- **Carried, still open from v3.2d (two boards):** the BOM-vs-price math; the time/teach
  claims on `learn.html` remain unvalidated marketing.

## 5 · The depth dial

**Too thin:** the Assay's minus; Keut's identity (fee fixed, steps didn't move); the
Flight's floor now that the covers are off (2 beers pay nothing — if human branching
stalls, the low rung is the dial, designer-held). **Too hot / unvalidated-hot:** the 4p
clock; the hops triple-sink; 2p margins. **Just right, protect:** the lading row, the
aging law (Q3+ never starts Ready — the Cellarman leak closed), the grain/hops split, the
majorities, the commission trap, the power-tier fees. **Skill converts** — provably, at
~70 points of win rate between greedy and searched play of the same strategy. **Loss is
legible**: every losing profile in the corpus is nameable from the endgame components
(parked dice, claimed tiles, the flight fan, the bank track), and the score-breakdown
audit exists on the table, not in memory. The component-state hard line holds across all
eight letters — nothing shipped this cycle asks a player to remember anything.

## 6 · The comp face-off

**vs Great Western Trail:** Hanse now *draws* on the charge/discharge architecture GWT
was cited for — the aging engine loads, the order row re-prices, the building ladder
tiers cheap-utility-to-costly-power exactly on the GWT pattern, and the moving lading row
gives the "re-solve the puzzle live" feel. Hanse *wins* interaction (shared hulls, sniped
orders vs GWT's near-solitaire deliveries) and theme-fusion (the die IS the barrel; GWT's
cattle are currency). Hanse still *loses* the co-built track: GWT's players reshape the
route network itself; Hanse's geography (8 slots) matures but never re-routes. And at 4p
Hanse currently *loses on runtime-to-weight ratio* — a GWT buyer expects the evening the
weight promises. **Why keep Hanse and shelve GWT:** the barrel-die and the order row are
a tighter, warmer signature than GWT's deck-thinning — *if* the pace lands in-band.

**vs Distilled:** Hanse *wins* depth decisively and now also competes on ritual — the
launder (rack an aged Bock's soul into a Gruit), the Abbey's 3-hops miracle, and the big
lading snipe are table ceremonies Distilled's batch-building was praised for. Distilled
still *wins* teach time and first-game warmth. The five-alarm test (deeper than
Distilled?) passes without argument. **Why keep Hanse:** it's the one that rewards the
fifth play.

## 7 · Pull diagnosis

**Depth pull, aligned — for the first time with no subsystem at war with it.** The v2.8
board found churn attacking legibility; the v4.5 board found randomness (auto-age,
un-steered piles) diluting authorship. At v45h the variance that remains is *authored*:
the export deal is public, the pile tops are steerable, the lading row is face-up and
contested — Orléans-class "variance you take credit for," serving depth rather than
fighting it. Retention is not hostage to the expansion calendar; the tabled toggles are
dessert. One honest asterisk: the *fantasy* pull thesis (Obsession-style niche devotion)
is carried by the brewing identity, and that identity currently under-delivers its
flagship beer — see the Bock decision below.

departure-sequencing (the skill v3.2d praised as deciding a game by 24 points), privilege
authorship, and the Hall's second value axis are *gone*, not reformed. The v3.2d build

own pillars (medium weight, GWT–Distilled band, component-carried state), v45h is the

the tabled Hall seam is where that debt is parked. The trajectory across five boards:
structure leaks → finishing list → elegance trims → identity gap → **identity delivered,
dials open.** That is ground, made in order.
