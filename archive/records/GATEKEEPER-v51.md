# GATEKEEPER-v51 — standing review #3 (2026-08-21)

**Scope:** the game as of **v5.1 “Wharf Hands”** (`KEY hanse-v51`), judged as a game.
Weighted toward whether the v5.0 “Open Wharf” + v5.1 program landed against its brief
(the 2026-08-18 4p human playtest: *“less tight, less a-vs-b, more fluid where needed”*).
**Evidence:** the full canon read + the v5.0/v5.1 batteries (300/count, A/B’d across
corpora), PATHWAYS 100/lane, the study archive. **No human table has played v5.0 or
v5.1** — every post-v4.17b read is sim-only; claims that only a table can settle are
marked as such throughout.

---

## 1 · The verdict

**Yes-but: the strongest build of this game to date, and the closest it has been to
shelf-ready — but it is one human-table cycle away from a “yes,” and two subsystems the
v5.1 pass touched now carry flags that would hold the purchase order.** The v5.0 program
demonstrably landed: the a-vs-b complaint is structurally gone (every line reaches all
four verbs at graded strength), the demand layer finally lives on the object players race
(the hulls), pace moved ~+3 rounds INTO the target band at every count (best 4p band on
record), and Novgorod — dead for ten versions — trades at ~20% share. That is a
transformation, not a tune. What holds the verdict at yes-but: **(1)** the rider
conversion cut the mason’s-mark economy ~40% and with it the payout of the game’s own
“author the wharf” signature lane; **(2)** the 3p seat spread has drifted to P1 42.7%
under the new grammar — a finding no watch list names; **(3)** roughly a third of the
building shelf still never fires in any corpus, so “17 of 19 — every wharf a different
economy” runs on six proven tiles and a speculative tail. All three are fixable with
dials that already exist. None should ship unread.

## 2 · The panel

**The Critic — weak yes, trending strong.** The die-is-the-cask is a genuinely ownable
signature: one component is maturation, gate, value, presence, clock, AND the building
ledger — no comp owns that compression. The Manifest ON the hull you race to fill is
better design than any order row, and the card recycling pristine is component-state
discipline most published euros lack. What withholds the strong yes: the rider pass
halved the reward for the builder fork exactly as the primary/alt grammar made
building the main way lines differentiate. The depth story now depends on authorship
being worth it; today the math says it barely is. *Flips to strong yes when the marks
pay like the fork they’re printed as.*

**The Buyer — yes on the hook, hold on validation.** “Your beer is a die: brew it low,
age it up, sail it away — the pips are your points, the body is your presence, and your
13 dice are the whole game’s clock” sells in one sentence to someone who owns 200 games;
the Hanseatic brewing niche has no incumbent. Production shape is sane (mid-box: ~150
tiles, 52 dice, 4 boards). The hold: no human has played the new grammar. A grammar
rewrite validated only by greedy bots doesn’t get stocked, however clean the batteries.
*Flips to yes after 2–3 human tables at 3–4p confirm the fluidity brief landed with the
audience that filed it.*

**The Optimizer — yes, with two flags.** This is now a proper depth-pull game: fully
deterministic, all-public state, a starved 13-die economy where every commitment is
countable, and variance you author (search-brew turned the last drawn randomness into a
chosen tile). Loss is legible: the wasted die, the hulk topped off for a rival, the Bock
held one round too long — all nameable. Flags: **(a)** 3p seat wins 42.7/32.3/25.0 — a
17.7pp P1 edge under mirror-greedy play, grown across v5.0→v5.1 (4p echoes it: P4
19.7%); **(b)** the lifter lane at 61/50% (2–3p PATHWAYS) — the Almoner retirement
removed the majority lane’s bump lever while lifted play kept every tool; that
approaches negation. Watch: the alt grammar softens the early placement puzzle (every
line does everything); the design trusts riders/hulls/seats to re-sharpen it — the
theory is sound, the table must prove position still matters by mid-game. *Flips both
flags → the loudest yes on the panel.*

**The Bridge Player — yes, for the first time.** The v4 line punished new players for
standing on the wrong station; v5.0 fixed the exact thing a first table quits over. A
“wrong” line still advances something, the warm start puts the first sail in the first
two turns, the first-game variant trims the roster, and learn.html teaches the loop
honestly. Remaining bumps: the recipe fee charged at Bruges reads as a gotcha once
(“my prize costs money?”), a forfeit London prize stings, and the rider scope rule
(“station action only, once per activation”) is the edge a learner gets wrong for three
turns. None are walls. *Flips to enthusiastic with a teach that leads with the die’s
five jobs and a rider rule simplified to “it fires when its verb happens here.”*

## 3 · Strengths (mechanical, not vibes)

- **The die-is-the-cask compression** — five jobs, one component; the entire game state
  auditable off the table. Scoring §11 is a bookkeeping-free audit; rare at this weight.
- **The public 13-die clock** — trigger control is a strategy, runway is countable
  across the table, and the empty-tray race gives the endgame a nameable tension.
- **Manifests on hulls** — demand, contested object, and timing unified; boarding order
  matters; once-per-line makes order inside one sail a real decision; in-play scoring
  improved loss legibility over the end-heavy v4 ledger.
- **The berth race** — interaction without take-that; topping off a rival’s hulk is the
  signature “I did this to you” moment, always visible coming.
- **Search-brew + census stacks** — authored variance upgraded to full agency; supply is
  a visible loop (delivered tiles come home), not an abstraction.
- **The echo grammar as a measured pace dial** — 0/0 ≈ live pace · 2/2 = the old fast
  game · 1/1 the print; reported, never silently retuned. Tuning discipline.
- **Fee-rides-the-item + the grain/hops split** — clean, teachable, printed where paid.

## 4 · Weaknesses and cut corners

- **The builder fork underpays.** End pips 4.5/5.2/4.6/player (was ~5.4), ticks −40%
  (2.5/5.3/6.9 vs 4.3/7.8/11.4). A die parked at a Kontor scores 1–6★ + majority weight
  + clock; a die on a building peaks near 5★, carries no majority weight, and depends on
  traffic the builder doesn’t control. 83–85% of builds arrive as free London prizes —
  almost nobody *chooses* to pay for this line. §11’s “legible fork: build vs brew” is
  printed on the box; the numbers say brew, then brew.
- **The speculative shelf.** In the v5.1 corpus: Rich Berth 0.0 buys · Customs 0.0–0.1
  boards · Capstan 0.0–0.1 · Chandler 0.0–0.1 · Tollhouse 0.0–0.1 · Rack 0.1–0.3 ·
  Abbey 0.1–0.3 per game. Known greedy blind spots — but the v4.12 GM oracle only ever
  revived Racking, and the v5.1 buffs plus BOTH new tiles (Ropewalk · Weigh House)
  shipped onto that same shelf with no oracle read. A third of the deck is
  design-by-hypothesis.
- **Seat order at 3p.** 42.7/32.3/25.0 (fair 33.3); v5.0 read 38.7/36.7/24.7; v4.9b was
  dead flat (34/33/33). The drift tracks the v5 grammar and is in no §Open watch. The 4p
  tail (P4 19.7%) rhymes.
- **The lifter lane at 2–3p** (61/50%) — reported, correctly not silently retuned, but
  the mechanism is identifiable (presence placement lost its diceless channel; lifted
  play kept Kiln/Hopex/Tollhouse/Customs) and needs an answer, not only a watch.
- **2p remains the weak count** — winner margins avg ~16 (blowout-flavored), Novgorod
  historically ~33% dead at two, majorities skip 2nd; §Open #8 has never had its own
  design pass.
- **Rules-mass edges** — the rider scope carve-out, bonus-after-sail timing, and
  once-per-voyage claims are each a table-lawyer moment the teach must carry.
- **Kit hygiene** — version-label drift on COMPONENTS.md / print.html / index.html /
  STYLE.md (fixed with this review; see the commit).

## 5 · The depth dial (corner-cutting vs over-difficulty, resolved)

- **Too thin:** the builder lane’s payout · the dead shelf’s tail · 2p texture · the
  Broker/Brewer’s Mate as possibly auto-pick 1-good seats (unprobed).
- **Too punishing (mostly resolved):** the v4-era tightness WAS the punishing pole and
  v5.0 addressed it structurally. What remains is spike-shaped: prize forfeits, the
  Bruges fee surprise, and a leader racing the tray (throttled now that presence flows
  only through casks — but the #25-style “door slammed while my engine peaked” feel is
  still reachable; only a table weighs it).
- **Where skill converts:** berth-race timing · reading manifests before brewing ·
  lift-routing · trigger control · authorship placement. GM/CM beating greedy 77–87%
  says the gradient is real.
- **Legible loss: yes, structurally.** All state public, all scoring auditable, most
  scoring in-play. Residual illegibility: end-game majority resolution (readable all
  game — acceptable) and clock-race losses (legible but can feel unfair — the standing
  felt-problem).

## 6 · The comp face-off

**vs Great Western Trail** — wins: theme distinctiveness · component compression (GWT
needs a deck, discs, and three tracks to say what one die says here) · table time ·
auditability. Loses: layered clocks (GWT runs three charge/discharge engines; this has
one clock + one demand layer — respectable post-v5.0, still thinner) · richness of the
co-built board (8 slots + 17 tiles vs the long track). Draws: the indirect,
structural interaction model. *Keep-it case:* an hour shorter, a fresher theme, and the
same “the board we built beat me” story — credible as the SECOND game in the niche for
a GWT owner, not yet a replacement.

**vs Distilled** — wins: depth and legible loss outright (card-market variance vs a
fully deterministic, all-public system) — the “out-depth Distilled” mission is
structurally accomplished. Loses: production charm and the object-fantasy (“I bottled a
whiskey” beats “my die parked at Bergen” emotionally; the recipe cards carry some of
this, thinner). Draws: theme niche. *Keep-it case:* the player who bounced off
Distilled’s luck has a home here. The five-alarm check (less deep than Distilled): a
comfortable no.

## 7 · Pull diagnosis

Running on **depth pull, as intended** — legible loss, starved economy, public state,
authored variance — with a healthy variety layer (the 3-of-4 export deal · the 17-of-19
building deal · shuffled census stacks · the Manifest deck) that freshens setup without
carrying retention. v5.0 moved the game *deeper* into depth pull by converting the last
drawn randomness into choice. The expansion toggles are a business option, not life
support. One caution: fantasy pull is the weakest engine here and the one Distilled
wins on — cheap contests exist (beer-named manifest lines, port-flavored delivery
moments) if positioning ever wants them.

## 8 · The path forward

**Must-fix before shelf-ready:**
1. **The human-table cycle for v5.0/v5.1.** The two-version grammar rewrite is
   table-unvalidated; the brief came from a table and the answer must return to one.
   *Test:* 2–3 games at 3–4p with the v5.0 complainants; pass = “less tight” volunteered
   unprompted + no rider-rule table-lawyering after game one.
2. **Re-fund the mason’s mark.** The v5.1 evidence bar (“re-tune on evidence only”) is
   met: ticks −40%, pips below the old mint. Recommended move: widen rider scope from
   “station action only” to **any matching action resolved on its line** — it simplifies
   the printed rule AND raises tick rates; fallback: +1 start face on the utility
   riders. *Test:* A/B 300/count; pass = end pips ≥ ~5.5 and the PATHWAYS builder lane
   ≥25% at 3–4p, with chosen (non-London) builds up.
3. **Diagnose the 3p seat drift.** Instrument first (what does P1 reach first —
   manifest-bearing warm hulls? the first search-brew?). *Test:* 300-game read with
   seat-rotated openings; pass = within ~±4pp of fair or a named, dialable cause.
4. **The lifter/majority rebalance at 2–3p.** The majority lane needs its lever back or
   the lifter needs a price. Candidates: a majority-flavored/Kontor-named Manifest line
   (already a recorded dial) · presence perks weighted up on Keut/Duckstein. *Test:*
   PATHWAYS 200/lane; pass = no lane >45% at any count.

**Would elevate it over the comps:**
5. The dead-shelf oracle read — GM/CM arms on Rich Berth · Ropewalk · Weigh House ·
   Customs before a human table (the v4.12 Racking lesson cuts both ways: greedy zeros
   are not verdicts, and three corpora of zeros before printing is due diligence).
6. A 2p pass with its own identity (the fourth Kontor at two · the margin profile ·
   maybe a 2p-only demand twist) — currently the count you don’t demo.
7. The fantasy layer — beer-named manifest lines, port-flavored delivery moments:
   Distilled’s one edge, cheaply contested.
8. A Broker / Brewer’s Mate / Chandler value probe (the SPECIALIST-VALUE-STUDY grammar)
   before the next print.

**Nice to have:** Jopenbier’s value question (§Open #14 options stand) · the Tastings’
slam-share table read (opt-in — can wait) · the Maltings 20th tile.

**If only one thing gets done: the human-table cycle (item 1), with the rider-scope A/B
(item 2) prepared so the same tables can feel both settings.** Everything else in this
review is a dial; those two decide whether v5 is the game.
