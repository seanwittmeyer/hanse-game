1The Brewing House

Hamburg was called the brewhouse of the Hanse. You are a merchant brewing house in the League, c. 1350.

The pivotal innovation of your age is hopped beer. Unlike perishable gruit ale, hopped beer survives a sea voyage — so beer becomes a shippable export. You will source grain and hops, brew casks, mature them, and push them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts (the kontore: Bergen, London, Bruges, Novgorod) — or enshrine your finest, locally, into the brewers' Hall. Whoever has shipped and enshrined the most valuable casks when the last ship sails wins.

Source → Brew → Age → Ship → (repeat)

  • You brew casks, mature them, and ship them to destinations for points. Where you ship is the key decision: VOLUME the kontore (value + majorities) vs PRESTIGE the Hall (quality, out of commerce).
  • Both stations on an activated line fire — the worker only gates which line is legal. (Hover a station to light its two lines.)
  • Lines pair two stations: Market+Brewhouse Brewhouse+Cellar Harbor+Cellar Market+Harbor.
  • The 8 slots are a living, composable ring: deployed casks (cargo + a public action), neutral ships, and owned buildings that modify the cask or ship docked on them (value or transform). Half your line evolves — the two stations are fixed, the two slots grow as you build; landing a building beside a line you fire often is the core play. Base actions always work; slot tiles only add.

Three rules constraints

No dice (fully deterministic) · No hidden draws (everything you acquire is taken from a face-up supply) · No money (goods are the only currency; delivered points are score only).

3Components

  • 1 Main board — the Wharf: a 2×2 of four stations (A Market · B Brewhouse · D Cellar · C Harbor) ringed by 8 slots, plus the shared Sailed-Ships track (the end clock).
  • 1 Destination board — the four kontore + the Hall, each with its quality gate, delivery value, benefit, and (kontore) a majority track (§4, §10).
  • 4 Player brewery boards — each private & untouchable: vessel lanes (start 2, cap 3 — casks held here power your Floor), the maturation track, a recipe rack, a few improvement slots, and goods storage (cap 8).
  • The tile supply — cask tiles (a shared pool with fixed counts; an ownership disc marks one on a slot or delivered), ship tiles (the neutral ship deck — Cog · Hulk with destinations printed), building tiles (the owned family — value + transform; they replace the old neutral buildings, goals, and most upgrades), recipe tiles, and a few brewery improvements. Each family is shown in its section below.
  • Goods cubes — grain (G) and hops (H), the only currency. Per player: 1 worker, ownership discs. 1 first-player marker (fixed — it does not pass).

4Anatomy of the Game

The Stations & Lines

Four stations sit in a 2×2: A Market (Source) & B Brewhouse (Brew) on top, C Harbor (Ship) & D Cellar (Age) below. You don't simply circle the board — you move where the board is best. Eight slots ring the stations, two beside each line. A line is its two stations plus any tiles in their two slots (slot · station · station · slot); each slot belongs to exactly one line. (Recipes & improvements live on your private board; buildings sit on the slots.)

The Destinations (where value lives)

The four kontore + the Hall are all open from the start. Each wants a certain quality of beer and pays differently — so there is no single best line of play. A kontor delivery is worth a small base + whatever value-buildings the cask shipped through, so the demand you author on the Wharf (not a fixed table) is what makes a delivery pay. The Hall pays a fixed prestige ladder by quality — the steady, beginner-safe lane.

The majority is the stable, board-agnostic floor — every kontor pays a tiered, ranked majority by delivered-cask count, 1st / 2nd / 3rd: Bruges 4/2/0 · London 5/3/1 · Novgorod 8/5/2 · Bergen 9/5/2 ⚙ (Bergen the rich anchor). Base delivery values are low so the in-game points live in the demand you build; the Hall's prestige is a fixed ladder 3/5/7/9 by quality. 2-player games skip 2nd (winner-take-all); ties split the occupied tiers. Each kontor's benefit is distinct: Bruges 2 goods (liquidity) · London take a Building (the engine) · Bergen a free Reach, +1 presence (feeds its rich majority) · Novgorod refine a maturing cask +1 age — and Novgorod's value scales with quality (Q3·2 / Q4·4 / Q5·6). The cask's owner always scores the delivery and takes any benefit.

Your Brewery (private, untouchable)

Your brewery board holds the maturation track (casks crawl Brew → … → Ready across your vessels — start 2, cap 3, each a parallel lane), a recipe rack, and a few improvements. A cask held in a vessel powers your Floor (its action, run as a private line) — so it works before it ships, and hold vs deploy is a live decision. Rivals can only act on your casks once they're deployed on a slot.

5Setup

  1. Place the main board and destination board. Shuffle the ship deck; deal 2 ships to slots (spread across different lines) and turn the next three face-up by the Market (a ship market of 3). The slots otherwise start empty — players author them with buildings. All five destinations begin open.
  2. Deal 3 of the 4 export beers ⚙. Each carries a fixed quality — Broyhan Q3 · Keut Q3 · Mumme Q4 · Bock Q5 — so dropping one varies the ladder: drop a Q3 → the full Q3→Q5 climb; drop Mumme → no Q4; drop Bock → no Q5. Recipes & ships are an always-available Market supply; shuffle the Building deck and deal 4 ⚙ face-up — the Building display (refills as tiles are taken, §9). Set out each quality's cask-action pile face-up (you brew toward the visible top action, §8).
  3. Set the Sailed-Ships track to its player-scaled length (it scales steeply): ~6 / 10 / 13 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 players ⚙.
  4. Each player takes a brewery board, a worker, ownership discs, and a starting stake: the Gruit + Hopped recipes (printed), 2 open vessels (lane 3 locked behind Extra Vessel), 2 charter contracts, 1 Building tile in hand (an authorship play for turn 1), and one Ready Gruit in Vessel 1 (deploy it turn 1).
  5. Starting goods: 3 , 2 — equal for every seat ⚙ (v1.7 removed the old +1 /later-seat compensation — under strong play it over-corrected). Turn order is fixed; the first player's natural turn-1 edge (choose the line, place + activate the warm Gruit, no toll) plus the free opening placement balance it.
  6. Each player places their worker on any station — your opening placement is free (no occupancy toll, §6). Give the first-player marker to any player (it does not pass).

Warm start

Starting with 2 vessels, two shared hulls already on the Wharf, and a Ready cask means a first voyage is reachable by turn 2–3.

6A Turn

Turn 1 only: place your worker on any station (free) and activate one of its two lines (no move). From turn 2, in order:

  1. Move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent station (A↔B, A↔C, B↔D, C↔D — never the diagonal). Mandatory. If you move onto a station a rival already occupies, pay 1 ⚙ to the supply (the occupancy toll) — or instead tap your Floor (use one maturing cask's action; §11).
  2. Activate the row OR the column of your worker's new station.
  3. Resolve the line's up-to-4 stops — slot · station · station · slot — in any order:
    • Each station: take its action. Stations are never closed — a rival's worker never stops you using one.
    • Each slot: you may use what it holds — a cask's signature action, a ship's free load, and any owned building's effect (it modifies the cask/ship docked on it). An empty slot's action is Deploy (shown "{building?} Deploy"): set one Ready cask onto any open slot. Combo: deploy onto another empty slot still pending on this line and its Deploy becomes that cask's action — take it this turn. All resolve on your turn, for you — no out-of-turn gains.

Both stations always fire

Activating a line triggers both stations; your worker only determines which line is legal. Because base actions are always available and slot buildings only add, you are never stalled — a populated Wharf makes your turns bigger.

Play passes clockwise. The first player is fixed for the whole game — turn order does not rotate. Starts are equal (v1.7 removed the +1 G/later-seat compensation); the seat balancer is the free opening placement plus the first player's natural turn-1 edge (§5).

8The Cask & Brewing

The tile the whole game turns on — half your impact is what you brew and how you use each cask. Three states: maturing (private vessel — it powers your Floor, working before it ships) → on a slot (cargo + a public action, contestable) → delivered (shipped → it scores and leaves). So hold vs deploy is a real call: a held cask keeps its action on your Floor but clogs the vessel; deploying frees the space and makes it public cargo. Beer is two layers — a quality rung + a recipe (permission to brew it); casks are brewed, not bought, from a shared pool, so the rare export casks are contested. Casks age 1 step automatically every turn, plus the Cellar pool.

9The Slots

The 8 slots are a living, composable ring of deployed casks (cargo + a public action), shared ships (neutral hulls that sail away and return to the deck), and owned buildings you place. A slot stacks building → ship → cargo (or building → cask): a building modifies the occupant docked on it — a value building makes its cask/ship's delivery pay more (the demand), a transform building changes the cask/ship (quality, cargo, route). Owned, but shared: the owner benefits most, but a rival may route through too — paying the owner a small wharfage in points (never a goods toll). When a line fires, the active player uses what its two slots hold, on their turn. Slots never lock: placing a building on an occupied slot replaces it — the displaced tile returns to its owner's hand, and if a rival overbuilt it, the owner also scores +3★ (developer) at game end (it served its purpose). The 8 slots are a constant contest over prime positions; nothing is destroyed.

The demand die (v1.2): a value building's bonus is carried on a reusable d6 — when a cask ships through it, set a die in the ship's berth to the bonus and its pips are the ★ banked on delivery (max 5); the die returns to the pool afterward (a stranded cask never banks). A quality transform (Malt Kiln / Hop Yard) rides a +1-quality marker instead; ship value buildings (Rich Berth / Festkeller) resolve live when the hull sails. So nothing is remembered in your head — the cask carries its earnings.

The race for cargo space

Everyone loads the same neutral ships — and you may also load a rival's cask, choosing its destination (the owner scores it + banks the destination benefit when the ship sails; you take a 1 G loader bonus now and free the slot). Commissioning grants a free load (a secured berth) and feeds the merchant goal; loading commits a cask but pays nothing yet; filling the ship sails the hull — and only then does each cask deliver, its owner banking that destination's benefit (goods / a Building) and its points, resolved in load order. Filling earns no bonus — the contest is berths on the rich routes and assembling a full cargo.

10Ships & Destinations

Shared hulls. Ships are neutral: hull (Cog 2 / Hulk 3) and a kontor destination printed on the tile, drawn from a shuffled ship deck (20 hulls ⚙ — 5 each for Bruges / London / Bergen / Novgorod; 11 Cog / 9 Hulk) that feeds a ship market of 3 ⚙ face-up at the Market. Ships sail only to the four kontore — the Hall is never a ship destination (it is reached locally, by Enshrine, below). Commission (Market, 2 G ⚙): place any one of the three face-up ships on an open slot and free-load one of your Ready casks (slot or vessel) aboard — the commissioner's prize is the secured berth (and it feeds the merchant goal), not an instant reward. Everything is paid on delivery: a ship sails only when it is full — there is no early launch — and only then does each cask deliver, its owner banking both that destination's benefit (goods / a Building) and its points (value ★ + majority). Benefits resolve in load order (the ship's first / second / third berths). Loading therefore banks nothing on its own — it is a commitment, and filling-and-sailing is the payoff. Filling earns no bonus. Each voyage advances the Sailed-Ships marker, and the hull returns to the bottom of the deck.

Enshrine — the Hall

The Hall is not a ship destination — it is a local Harbor action. Enshrine: take a Ready cask (Q2+) that is already deployed on a slot and withdraw it into the Hall — no ship, no fare. It scores the Hall's printed prestige ladder (Q2→3 · Q3→5 · Q4→7 · Q5→9 ⚙) and, like any voyage, advances the Sailed-Ships clock. You may enshrine one cask per Harbor visit (two with the Trophy Room, §11). Because the cask must first sit on a public slot, it stays contestable — a rival may hijack-load it to a kontor before you enshrine. The Hall pays no in-game benefit (prestige only; the Patron's Favor modifier adds 1 good — §11).

Charter Voyage

The Harbor always offers a Charter (no tile — an outside hull): spend a charter contract + a flat 2 fare ⚙ to send one Ready cask — from a vessel or a slot — on an immediate single-cask voyage to a kontor it qualifies for (Charter is kontore-only; the Hall is reached by Enshrine, not chartered). Each house starts with 2 contracts and can buy more at the Market (one Market acquire, 1 G each, unlimited supply) — the scarce contract is the throttle. It delivers normally and advances the clock, but is strictly worse per cask than a Cog/Hulk — so filling the shared ships stays the better plan; the Charter just keeps the tight slots from ever deadlocking (Move → Market → buy a contract → charter is always a legal escape).

Delivery Benefits

Each kontor pays a distinct benefit on delivery: Bruges 2 goods (liquidity) · London take a Building (free, to hand — you choose from the display; the engine loop: deliver → author → deliver better. If your cask is delivered on a rival's turn, the choice is queued to the start of your next turn) · Bergen a free Reach (+1 presence at a kontor you've delivered to, feeding its rich majority) · Novgorod refine a maturing cask (+1 age), and its value scales with quality. The Hall pays prestige (the local Enshrine).

11The Brewery & the Floor

Your brewery is private and safe: your vessels (start 2, cap 3), your recipes, and a few improvements — each a private one-time buy for goods at the Market (`4–5 G` ⚙; not a contested tile — every house may buy the same ones). The big capability / "get-ahead" layer now lives publicly, on the slots — as the Buildings you author (§9); only a few inherently-private perks stay here. Granary vs Hop Garden is a real choice now (v1.6): grain runs operations (tolls, tiles, ships), hops climbs the quality ladder (Mumme/Bock lean hops).

The Floor — your private line

The casks held in your vessels power your Floor: on your turn you may work the Wharf (move + a grid line) or work your Floor — take your held casks' actions as a private line, without fighting for board position. A bare brewery is a weak Floor; a full one (more vessels, more action-bearing casks held) is a strong private engine — so the Floor scales with what you've built, and a held cask has value before it ships. This is why hold vs deploy matters, and why Extra Vessel finally pays.

12Goods & Storage

grain and hops are the only currency — recipes, brew inputs, ships, buildings, loader benefits. You may hold up to 8 goods ⚙. There is no money and no spendable prestige — delivered points are score only.

13End of the Game

The game ends when the shared Sailed-Ships track fills — a visible, steerable clock:

  • Every voyage fills one slot on the track — a ship sailing (only when full), a Charter, or an Enshrine.
  • Track length scales with player count: ~6 / 10 / 13 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 players ⚙ (it fills faster with more players).
  • Backstop: a round limit (≈25 ⚙) ends a slow table even if the track hasn't filled.
  • When it fills → finish the current round (so everyone has had equal turns) → score (§14).

Steer it: a volume player races to sail ships fast and end the game; a prestige player keeps a steadier pace to buy more delivering turns.

14Scoring

No goal tiles, no separate tracks — you score from what you delivered: two in-game sources, two end-game.

  1. Hall — fixed (in-game): each enshrined cask scores the printed prestige ladder 3 / 5 / 7 / 9 by quality. The steady, beginner-safe lane.
  2. Kontore — variable (in-game): each delivered cask scores its small base + the value-buildings it shipped through (the demand you authored). A premium cask rewards the climb — a value building pays its printed ★ for Q1–Q3, but a Q4 cask banks +2★ and a Q5 +3★ on top (no cap). The skill lane.
  3. Majorities (end-game) — at every kontor, by delivered-cask count, 1st / 2nd / 3rd take the tiers: Bruges 4/2/0 · London 5/3/1 · Novgorod 8/5/2 · Bergen 9/5/2 ⚙. 2-player games skip 2nd; ties split the occupied tiers. The stable floor.
  4. The Flight (end-game) — your range: count the distinct beers you delivered and score (beers − 1)², min 3 → 3→4 · 4→9 · 5→16. There are always exactly five beer types (Gruit + Hopped + the 3 dealt exports), so the full flight is always reachable — not how high, but how complete the range of the house. The range lane's reward.
  5. Developer (end-game) — +3★ ⚙ for each of your buildings a rival overbuilt (it served its purpose; you're paid for authoring a slot worth covering). Self-displacement pays nothing. The crowded-ring / authorship reward — bites most at 4p.

Highest total wins. Tiebreak: most goods, then most casks on slots.

The five paths to win (each a complete lane — you needn't braid): Prestige (the Hall, fixed) · Demand (route casks through your value-buildings) · Volume (kontor majorities) · Range (the Flight) · Authorship (build the board, tax the traffic).

152-Player Variant

With fewer workers the slots feel loose, so lock some slots (down to ~6 ⚙) to keep the race for cargo space tight. The Sailed-Ships track is shortest at 2p (~6 slots ⚙), so the clock stays brisk. Majorities are winner-take-all at 2p (2nd place is skipped). Everything else plays identically.

★Expansions (opt-in · mix freely)

A modular, opt-in expansion, switched on by a New Game checkbox. Off → the base game is exactly as above. On → three new specialty beers join the export draft, so you deal 3 of 7 (vs the base 3 of 4), with a guarantee of ≥1 base Q4+ (Mumme/Bock) so the quality climb still matters. The point: turn the roster from a quality ladder you climb into characters you main. Each specialty beer is pinned — its slot-action is fixed & printed (the base climb beers still draw from the steerable pile, so the Orléans-lite agency is untouched; this simply generalizes Gruit's pin) — and carries one always-on signature, so leaning a beer pays during play, not just at scoring.

The Inland Road (Option B, full · its own toggle)

The heaviest toggle: a whole second destinations board that challenges the four kontore. Beyond the sea lie the great inland brewing towns — a contested overland network reached by Caravan (a local Harbor action: a deployed cask travels overland, no boat). The Caravan is clock-neutral — overland trade never fills the sea clock. The road is a trunk + an eastern fork, and every town holds one charter, claimed first-come (a land-grab — you pick which open town your cask claims):

16Glossary

The Wharf
The whole core area: four stations (Market · Brewhouse · Cellar · Harbor) ringed by 8 slots.
Station · Slot · Line
A station is one of the four action spaces; a slot is one of the 8 spaces ringing them (a deployed cask, a ship, and/or an owned building); a line is a row or column = its two stations plus the tiles in their two slots.
Occupancy toll
1 G ⚙ paid to the supply when you move onto a station a rival occupies (opening placement free). Never blocks.
Seat compensation
Later seats start with +1 G each — the fixed-turn-order balancer.
Cask · Deploy
A brewed beer in one of three states: maturing (vessel) → on a slot (cargo + a public action) → delivered (shipped → scores). Deploy = set a Ready cask onto an open slot.
Ship · Charter
A Ship is a neutral hull (Cog 2 / Hulk 3) with its destination printed, from the shared ship deck; anyone loads their own casks (loading commits, pays nothing yet); it sails only when full, and only then does every cask deliver — each owner banking that destination's benefit + its points, in load order — before the hull returns to the deck. A Charter ships one Ready cask now for a charter contract + a flat 2 G fare (start with 2; buy more at the Market, 1 G) — less efficient, but a deadlock guard.
Destination · Kontor · Hall
Where a ship delivers. Kontore pay trade value + majorities (volume); the Hall pays prestige (scarcity).
Majority
Tiered, ranked by delivered casks at a kontor (1st/2nd/3rd take the tiers; 2p skips 2nd). Concentrated at Bergen.
Recipe · Building · Wharfage

The goal: ship valuable casks before the Sailed-Ships track fills — kontore for value + majorities (volume), the Hall for prestige (quality). Costs read as 2; gains as +2 .

Shipping & voyages
Sail (auto, when full)A ship sails the instant it is full — never partly loaded: every cask delivers (each owner scores + banks the benefit, in load order), then the ship moves to the Sailed-Ships track. A hull you can't fill waits for more cargo, or you charter the stranded cask out.
Enshrine (the Hall)Harbor. Withdraw a deployed Q2+ cask from a slot into the Hall — no ship, no fare. Scores the prestige ladder (Q2→3 · Q3→5 · Q4→7 · Q5→9 ⚙); advances the clock. 1 per Harbor. No in-game benefit.
CharterHarbor. Spend a charter contract + a flat 2 fare ⚙ to ship one Ready cask (vessel or slot) on a single-cask voyage to a kontor. Start with 2 contracts; buy more at the Market (1 G each). Advances the clock; less efficient than your own ship.

18Quick Reference

Your turn

  1. Move to an orthogonally adjacent station (mandatory) — pay 1 G to the supply if a rival is already there.
  2. Activate its row or column.
  3. Resolve the line (slot · station · station · slot), any order. Both stations fire; stations never closed; use the buildings in the line's two slots (on your turn).

At the Wharf

Market (gain goods, or acquire a recipe / a Building [+place] / commission a ship / a charter contract) → Brewhouse (Brew — take the top of a quality pile) → Cellar (Age to Ready → deploy to a slot) → Harbor (Ship to a kontor → sails only when full → delivers; or Enshrine a deployed Q2+ cask into the Hall). Or work your Floor instead of the grid.

Where to ship

Bruges (+1, 2 goods, maj 4/2/0) · London (+1, take a Building, maj 5/3/1) · Bergen (+1, free Reach, maj 9/5/2 — the anchor) · Novgorod (value scales 2/4/6 by quality, refine +1 age, maj 8/5/2). A kontor delivery = base + the value-buildings you routed it through. Enshrine a deployed Q2+ cask into the Hall (no ship/fare) for fixed prestige — 3/5/7/9 by quality. Each cask owner scores & takes the benefit (in load order) when the shared hull sails full.

Game ends

The Sailed-Ships track fills (each voyage = 1 slot — a full sail, a Charter, or an Enshrine) → finish the round → score: Hall (fixed) + kontor deliveries (base + your buildings) + Majorities (tiered; 2p skips 2nd) + the Flight (range) + Developer (+3★ per building a rival overbuilt).
