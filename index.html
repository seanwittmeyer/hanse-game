1The Brewing House

Hamburg was called the brewhouse of the Hanse. You are a merchant brewing house in the League, c. 1350.

The pivotal innovation of your age is hopped beer. Unlike perishable gruit ale, hopped beer survives a sea voyage — so beer becomes a shippable export. You will source grain and hops, brew casks, mature them, and push them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts (the kontore: Bergen, London, Bruges, Novgorod) — or enshrine your finest, locally, into the brewers' Hall. Whoever has shipped and enshrined the most valuable casks when the last ship sails wins.

Source → Brew → Age → Ship → (repeat)

  • You brew casks, mature them, and ship them to destinations for points. Where you ship is the key decision: VOLUME the kontore (value + majorities) vs PRESTIGE the Hall (quality, out of commerce).
  • Both stations on an activated line fire — the worker only gates which line is legal. (Hover a station to light its two lines.)
  • Lines pair two stations: Market+Brewhouse Brewhouse+Cellar Harbor+Cellar Market+Harbor.
  • The 8 slots are a living, composable ring: deployed casks (cargo + a public action), neutral ships, and owned buildings that modify the cask or ship docked on them (value or transform). Half your line evolves — the two stations are fixed, the two slots grow as you build; landing a building beside a line you fire often is the core play. Base actions always work; slot tiles only add.

Three rules constraints

No dice (fully deterministic) · No hidden draws (everything you acquire is taken from a face-up supply) · No money (goods are the only currency; delivered points are score only).

3Components

  • 1 Main board — the Wharf: a 2×2 of four stations (A Market · B Brewhouse · D Cellar · C Harbor) ringed by 8 slots, plus the shared Sailed-Ships track (the end clock).
  • 1 Destination board — the four kontore + the Hall, each with its quality gate, delivery value, benefit, and (kontore) a majority track (§4, §10).
  • 4 Player brewery boards — each private & untouchable: vessel lanes (start 2, cap 3 — casks held here power your Floor), the maturation track, a recipe rack, a few improvement slots, and goods storage (cap 8).
  • The tile supply — cask tiles (a shared pool with fixed counts; an ownership disc marks one on a slot or delivered), ship tiles (the neutral ship deck — Cog · Hulk with destinations printed), building tiles (the owned family — value + transform; they replace the old neutral buildings, goals, and most upgrades), recipe tiles, and a few brewery improvements. Each family is shown in its section below.
  • Goods cubes — grain (G) and hops (H), the only currency. Per player: 1 worker, ownership discs. 1 first-player marker (fixed — it does not pass).

4Anatomy of the Game

The Stations & Lines

Four stations sit in a 2×2: A Market (Source) & B Brewhouse (Brew) on top, C Harbor (Ship) & D Cellar (Age) below. You don't simply circle the board — you move where the board is best. Eight slots ring the stations, two beside each line. A line is its two stations plus any tiles in their two slots (slot · station · station · slot); each slot belongs to exactly one line. (Recipes & improvements live on your private board; buildings sit on the slots.)

The Destinations (where value lives)

The four kontore + the Hall are all open from the start. Each wants a certain quality of beer and pays differently — so there is no single best line of play. A kontor delivery is worth a small base + whatever value-buildings the cask shipped through, so the demand you author on the Wharf (not a fixed table) is what makes a delivery pay. The Hall pays a fixed prestige ladder by quality — the steady, beginner-safe lane.

The majority is the stable, board-agnostic floor — every kontor pays a tiered, ranked majority by delivered-cask count, 1st / 2nd / 3rd: Bruges 4/2/0 · London 5/3/1 · Novgorod 8/5/2 · Bergen 9/5/2 ⚙ (Bergen the rich anchor). Base delivery values are low so the in-game points live in the demand you build; the Hall's prestige is a fixed ladder 3/5/7/9 by quality. 2-player games skip 2nd (winner-take-all); ties split the occupied tiers. Each kontor's benefit is distinct: Bruges 2 goods (liquidity) · London take a Building (the engine) · Bergen a free Reach, +1 presence (feeds its rich majority) · Novgorod refine a maturing cask +1 age — and Novgorod's value scales with quality (Q3·2 / Q4·4 / Q5·6). The cask's owner always scores the delivery and takes any benefit.

Your Brewery (private, untouchable)

Your brewery board holds the maturation track (casks crawl Brew → … → Ready across your vessels — start 2, cap 3, each a parallel lane), a recipe rack, and a few improvements. A cask held in a vessel powers your Floor (its action, run as a private line) — so it works before it ships, and hold vs deploy is a live decision. Rivals can only act on your casks once they're deployed on a slot.

5Setup

  1. Place the main board and destination board. Shuffle the ship deck; deal 2 ships to slots (spread across different lines) and turn the next three face-up by the Market (a ship market of 3). Deal 2 neutral green Buildings from the deck onto open slots (v2.5 — they serve any dock; no owner — overbuilding one still costs the 1 G ground rent, pays nobody, and the tile is discarded). The remaining slots start empty — players author them. All five destinations begin open.
  2. Deal 3 of the 4 export beers ⚙. Each carries a fixed quality — Broyhan Q3 · Keut Q3 · Mumme Q4 · Bock Q5 — so dropping one varies the ladder: drop a Q3 → the full Q3→Q5 climb; drop Mumme → no Q4; drop Bock → no Q5. Recipes & ships are an always-available Market supply; shuffle the Building deck and deal 4 ⚙ face-up — the Building display (refills as tiles are taken, §9). Set out each quality's cask-action pile face-up (you brew toward the visible top action, §8).
  3. Set the Sailed-Ships track to its player-scaled length (it scales steeply): ~6 / 10 / 13 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 players ⚙.
  4. Each player takes a brewery board, a worker, ownership discs, and a starting stake: the Gruit + Hopped recipes (printed), 2 open vessels (lane 3 unlocked by the Coppersmith), 2 charter contracts, and one Ready Gruit in Vessel 1 (deploy it turn 1). (v2.2: no starting building — buildings are always bought/earned from the display and placed at once.)
  5. Starting goods: 3 , 2 — equal for every seat ⚙ (v1.7 removed the old +1 /later-seat compensation — under strong play it over-corrected). Turn order is fixed; the first player's natural turn-1 edge (choose the line, place + activate the warm Gruit, no toll) plus the free opening placement balance it.
  6. Each player places their worker on any station — your opening placement is free (no occupancy toll, §6). Give the first-player marker to any player (it does not pass).

Warm start

Starting with 2 vessels, two shared hulls already on the Wharf, and a Ready cask means a first voyage is reachable by turn 2–3.

6A Turn

Turn 1 only: place your worker on any station (free) and choose one of its lines (no move). From turn 2, in order:

  1. Move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent station (A↔B, A↔C, B↔D, C↔D — never the diagonal). Mandatory.
  2. Choose one of THREE lines (v2.2): the station's row, its column, or your Floor — the standing private 3rd line (§11). The 1 occupancy toll ⚙ is paid to the supply only when you activate a public line while a rival shares your station — the Floor is never tolled.
  3. Resolve the line's stops — a public line's slot · station · station · slot, or your Floor's cask actions + Wilds — in any order; every stop is optional:
    • Each station: take its action. Stations are never closed — a rival's worker never stops you using one.
    • Each slot: you may use its occupant — a cask's signature action (as modified by the building under it — e.g. the Workshop makes it Wild), or a ship's free load. Buildings are passive modifiers, never stops. An empty slot's action is Deploy (shown "{building?} Deploy"): set one Ready cask onto any slot without a ship or cask — or, spoilage (v2.9), deploy a Ready Q4+ cask onto a slot holding a Q1 cask (anyone's): the stale ale is returned to the box and the premium cask takes the berth. You may use a cask you just placed as long as it isn't on the stop you spent to deploy it — deploy onto another empty slot still pending on this line and its Deploy becomes that cask's action. All resolve on your turn, for you — no out-of-turn gains.

Both stations always fire

Activating a line triggers both stations; your worker only determines which line is legal. Because base actions are always available and slot buildings only add, you are never stalled — a populated Wharf makes your turns bigger.

Play passes clockwise. The first player is fixed for the whole game — turn order does not rotate. Starts are equal (v1.7 removed the +1 G/later-seat compensation); the seat balancer is the free opening placement plus the first player's natural turn-1 edge (§5).

8The Cask & Brewing

The tile the whole game turns on — half your impact is what you brew and how you use each cask. Three states: maturing (private vessel — it powers your Floor, working before it ships) → on a slot (cargo + a public action, contestable) → delivered (shipped → it scores and leaves). So hold vs deploy is a real call: a held cask keeps its action on your Floor but clogs the vessel; deploying frees the space and makes it public cargo. Beer is two layers — a quality rung + a recipe (permission to brew it); casks are brewed, not bought, from a shared pool, so the rare export casks are contested. Casks age 1 step automatically every turn, plus the Cellar pool.

9The Slots

The 8 slots are a living, composable ring of deployed casks (cargo + a public action), shared ships (neutral hulls that sail away and return to the deck), and owned buildings you place. A slot stacks building → ship → cargo (or building → cask): a building modifies the occupant docked on it — a value building makes its cask/ship's delivery pay more (the demand), a transform building changes the cask/ship (quality, cargo, route). Three tile types, three colours (v2.4.1): a PRIVILEGE (bright blue tile) pays its owner only (a rival docking there banks nothing); a BUILDING (green tile) serves whoever docks; the third type — the purple SPECIALISTS — lives on your private brewery board (§11). No payments between players; the contest is squatting on a rival's privilege (deny it), overbuilding it, and the occupancy toll. When a line fires, the active player uses what its two slots hold, on their turn. Slots never lock — overbuild costs the ground rent (v2.9): placing a building on an occupied slot costs the builder 1 G ⚙ (paid to the stores; can't pay → occupied slots aren't legal targets) and displaces the building there — the tile flips to its owner's floor as a Wild worth +3★ at game end if still there (floor full → returned to the box, nothing banks). The 8 slots are a constant contest over prime positions — but eviction has a price.

The demand die (a real d6): a value building's bonus is carried on a reusable d6 — when your cask ships through your value building (v2.3 — privileges pay their owner only), set the die in the ship's berth to the building's printed ★ plus the quality premium (Q4 +2 · Q5 +3), max 6, set not accumulated; only buildings modify dice — the destination is the cask's starting value. A ship-slot value building (Rich Berth / Festkeller) bumps the same die at the sail — a die-less cask takes one at the bump value, and the one die never exceeds 6. The die returns to the pool after delivery (a stranded cask never banks). A quality transform (Malt Kiln / Hop Yard) rides a +1-quality marker instead — a work, it serves anyone. Delivery = starting value + the die — nothing is remembered in your head.

The race for cargo space

Everyone loads the same neutral ships — and you may also load a rival's cask, choosing its destination (the owner scores it + banks the destination benefit when the ship sails; you take a 1 G loader bonus now and free the slot). Commissioning grants a free load of one of your deployed casks (a secured berth); loading commits a cask but pays nothing yet; filling the ship sails the hull — and only then does each cask deliver, its owner banking that destination's benefit (goods / a Building) and its points, resolved in load order. Filling earns no bonus — the contest is berths on the rich routes and assembling a full cargo.

10Ships & Destinations

Shared hulls. Ships are neutral: hull (Cog 2 / Hulk 3) and a kontor destination printed on the tile, drawn from a shuffled ship deck (20 hulls ⚙ — 5 each for Bruges / London / Bergen / Novgorod; 11 Cog / 9 Hulk) that feeds a ship market of 3 ⚙ face-up at the Market. Ships sail only to the four kontore — the Hall is never a ship destination (it is reached locally, by Enshrine, below). Commission (Market, 2 G ⚙): place any one of the three face-up ships on an open slot — or onto a slot whose cask can board it (v2.6 dockside pickup: that cask loads at once; a rival’s cask follows the rival-loading rules, and a hull filled by the pickup sails immediately) — and free-load one cask aboard — any player’s deployed cask (a rival’s → the rival-loading rules) or a Ready cask straight from your own vessels (v2.8: commission is the one universal vessel-direct door past deploy-first) — the commissioner's prize is the secured berth plus that direct door, not an instant reward. Everything is paid on delivery: a ship sails only when it is full — there is no early launch — and only then does each cask deliver, its owner banking both that destination's benefit (goods / a Building) and its points (value ★ + majority). Benefits resolve in load order (the ship's first / second / third berths). Loading therefore banks nothing on its own — it is a commitment, and filling-and-sailing is the payoff. Filling earns no bonus. Each voyage advances the Sailed-Ships marker, and the hull returns to the bottom of the deck.

Enshrine — the Hall

The Hall is not a ship destination — it is a local Harbor action. Enshrine: take a Ready cask (Q2+) that is already deployed on a slot and withdraw it into the Hall — no ship, no fare. It scores the Hall's printed prestige ladder (Q2→3 · Q3→5 · Q4→7 · Q5→9 ⚙) and, like any voyage, advances the Sailed-Ships clock. You may enshrine one cask per Harbor visit. The Hall demands a public showing — the cask must first sit on a slot, so it stays contestable: a rival may hijack-load it to a kontor before you enshrine (the Quaymaster — the invested exception — may enshrine straight from the vessels). The Hall pays no in-game benefit (prestige only).

Charter Voyage

The Harbor always offers a Charter (no tile — an outside hull): spend a charter contract + a flat 2 fare ⚙ to send one of your deployed Ready casks (v2.8 deploy-first; the Quaymaster also reaches your vessels) on an immediate single-cask voyage to a kontor it qualifies for (Charter is kontore-only; the Hall is reached by Enshrine, not chartered). Each house starts with 2 contracts and can buy more at the Market (one Market acquire, 1 G each, unlimited supply) — the scarce contract is the throttle. It delivers normally and advances the clock, but is strictly worse per cask than a Cog/Hulk — so filling the shared ships stays the better plan; the Charter just keeps the tight slots from ever deadlocking (Move → Market → buy a contract → charter is always a legal escape).

Delivery Benefits — when gained, owner's choice (v2.2)

Each kontor pays a distinct benefit, resolved by the cask's owner the moment it delivers — whoever's turn it is: Bruges 2 goods, any mix (liquidity) · London choose a Privilege/Building from the Wharf display and place it now — or take a free Specialist instead (v2.4; the engine loop: deliver → author → deliver better) · Bergen a free Reach (+1 presence at a kontor you've delivered to, feeding its rich majority) · Novgorod refine — you choose which maturing cask ages +1, and its value scales with quality. The Hall pays prestige (the local Enshrine).

11The Brewery & the Floor

Your brewery is private and safe: your vessels (start 2, cap 3), your recipes, and the SPECIALISTS — the third tile type (purple): private, one-time buys for goods at the Cellar (`2–4 G` ⚙), hired free by the Q3+ Hire cask action, or taken as London's benefit (v2.4). v82 "Scarce Improvements": the improvements are no longer an open catalog — they're a shuffled deck of n − 1 copies of each of the 7 types (n = players) feeding a face-up display of 4 at the Cellar (the same deck/display as the Buildings). Only the 4 face-up tiles are buyable, so the table competes for them — not everyone can fit every improvement. The big capability / "get-ahead" layer lives publicly, on the slots — the Buildings you author (§9); only a few inherently-private perks stay here. Grain Factor vs Hop Gardener is a real choice (v1.6): grain runs operations (tolls, tiles, ships), hops climbs the quality ladder (Mumme/Bock lean hops).

The Floor — the standing 3rd line (v2.2)

After moving, you always choose among three lines: the row, the column, or your Floor — the actions of every cask held in your vessels plus a Wild per flipped building, any order, all optional, never tolled. A bare brewery is a weak Floor; a full one is a strong private engine — the Floor scales with what you've built, and a held cask has value before it ships. This is why hold vs deploy matters, and why the Coppersmith finally pays.

12Goods & Storage

grain and hops are the only currency — recipes, brew inputs, ships, buildings, loader benefits. You may hold up to 8 goods ⚙. There is no money and no spendable prestige — delivered points are score only.

13End of the Game

The game ends when the shared Sailed-Ships track fills — a visible, steerable clock:

  • Every voyage fills one slot on the track — a ship sailing (only when full), a Charter, or an Enshrine.
  • Track length scales with player count: ~6 / 10 / 13 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 players ⚙ (it fills faster with more players).
  • Backstop: a round limit (≈25 ⚙) ends a slow table even if the track hasn't filled.
  • When it fills → finish the current round (so everyone has had equal turns) → score (§14).

Steer it: a volume player races to sail ships fast and end the game; a prestige player keeps a steadier pace to buy more delivering turns.

14Scoring

No goal tiles, no separate tracks — you score from what you delivered: two in-game sources, two end-game.

  1. Hall — fixed (in-game): each enshrined cask scores the printed prestige ladder 3 / 5 / 7 / 9 by quality. The steady, beginner-safe lane.
  2. Kontore — variable (in-game): each delivered cask scores its destination's starting value + the demand die in its berth (the building's printed ★ + the quality premium — Q4 +2, Q5 +3 — set on the die, max 6). The skill lane.
  3. Majorities (end-game) — at every kontor, by delivered-cask count, 1st / 2nd / 3rd take the tiers: Bruges 4/2/0 · London 5/3/1 · Novgorod 8/5/2 · Bergen 9/5/2 ⚙. 2-player games skip 2nd; ties split the occupied tiers. The stable floor.
  4. The Flight (end-game) — your range: count the distinct beers you delivered and score (beers − 1)², min 3 → 3→4 · 4→9 · 5→16 · 6→25 (the 6th rung exists when the Jopenbier capstone is in play — it counts, v2.2). The base game always has exactly five beer types (Gruit + Hopped + the 3 dealt exports), so the full base flight is always reachable. The range lane's reward.
  5. The floor bonus (end-game, v2.9) — +3★ ⚙ per displaced building still on your floor (the flipped Wilds). Specialists share the same 4 floor slots, so a big private engine spends banked-points capacity; a tile displaced onto a full floor is returned to the box and pays nothing. The crowded-ring / authorship compensation — kept, not spent.

Highest total wins. Tiebreak: the combined quality of casks you've deployed on slots (built but not yet delivered), then most goods.

The five paths to win (each a complete lane — you needn't braid): Prestige (the Hall, fixed) · Demand (route casks through your value-buildings) · Volume (kontor majorities) · Range (the Flight) · Authorship (build the board — your privileges pay you, and a displaced tile keeps paying from your floor).

152-Player Variant

The Sailed-Ships track is shortest at 2p (~6 slots ⚙), so the clock stays brisk with fewer workers on the board. Majorities are winner-take-all at 2p (2nd place is skipped). Everything else plays identically — there's no separate slot-locking rule.

★Expansions (three opt-in toggles · mix freely)

Specialty Beers

A modular, opt-in expansion, switched on by a New Game checkbox. Off → the base game is exactly as above. On → three new specialty beers join the export draft, so you deal 3 of 7 (vs the base 3 of 4), with a guarantee of ≥1 base Q4+ (Mumme/Bock) so the quality climb still matters. The point: turn the roster from a quality ladder you climb into characters you main. Each specialty beer is pinned — its slot-action is fixed & printed (the base climb beers still draw from the steerable pile, so the Orléans-lite agency is untouched; this simply generalizes Gruit's pin) — and carries one always-on signature, so leaning a beer pays during play, not just at scoring.

The Trade Roads (Overland · the big one — replaces the kontor majorities)

An inland trade network — a tree rooted at Hamburg (home). Two roads lead out: West to the Bruges gateway (which branches to London, Bergen, and the Rhineland) and East on the long deep haul to Novgorod. Bruges is the lone no-slots gateway; every other node — the towns AND the London/Bergen/Novgorod kontore — carries Staple-Right slots (the kontore claim theirs when your caravan reaches them, on top of their base sea benefit). You grow it with the deliveries you already make (no new station, no new action; it rides the Harbor's Ship). When it's on, the kontor majorities turn OFF — the contest moves inland.

  • Movement is per VOYAGE — ONE node per owner. Each ship that sails to a kontor (full keystone — demand die, quality premium, the Flight; or a Charter) advances your caravan one node toward it. A 3-cask Hulk = one step. Casks never buy depth — only quality (the gate) and repeated voyages (the clock) do.
  • Each cask claims a Staple Right. Every cask aboard then claims an open slot at the node reached, in load order (first loaded picks first) — scoring the node's ★ + a one-shot bonus. A 2-cask voyage to your frontier town = two slots. Never nothing: a full town pays the line's small overflow.
  • Quality gates depth. You step onto the next node only if a cask aboard meets its quality gate — voyages buy distance, quality unlocks the deep towns. Reach (breadth) and quality (depth) both win.
  • Slots are flavoured by LANE (2–4 per node, scaling n+1 with player count; deep ◆ = high-gate terminals — Pskov scarce at one slot, Frankfurt a rich 4-slot outlet): Rhineland = craft (a recipe · a brew action · +2 age) · London = infrastructure (a Building from the display · a free Improvement · goods) · Bergen = logistics (a charter contract · goods · a vessel) · the East = depth/value (a vessel · +1 quality · big points). Frankfurt is points + a free Enshrine; Pskov is just a delivery — one slot +8★, every later delivery +5★. The East pays the most in points; the Rhineland fuels the climb that gets you there.
  • Bruges = the knowledge on-ramp (the one no-slots node): a delivery there lets you take a recipe OR 2 goods. The Rhine Charter — a Q4+ Charter — skips Bruges, leaping the caravan straight to Cologne.
  • The map: West — Bruges → Cologne → Frankfurt (◆); → Antwerp → London; → Tønsberg → Bergen. East — Visby → Tallinn → Novgorod → Pskov (◆ · Q5). Scoring (replaces majorities): the inland ★ = node delivery points + the slot bonuses you claimed, banked as you play; the Hall stays the non-delivery lane.

Built on a small expansion spine (a registry + hooks) so each module is one clean, self-contained block. All numbers ⚙. See RULES.md §12 · DESIGN.md §9.

16Glossary

The Wharf
The whole core area: four stations (Market · Brewhouse · Cellar · Harbor) ringed by 8 slots.
Station · Slot · Line
A station is one of the four action spaces; a slot is one of the 8 spaces ringing them (a deployed cask, a ship, and/or an owned building); a line is a row or column = its two stations plus the tiles in their two slots.
Occupancy toll
1 G ⚙ paid to the supply when you activate a public line while a rival shares your station (the Floor is never tolled; opening placement free). Never blocks.
Seat compensation
Removed in v1.7. Starts are equal for every seat; the first-player balancer is the free opening placement + P1's natural turn-1 edge.
Cask · Deploy
A brewed beer in one of three states: maturing (vessel) → on a slot (cargo + a public action) → delivered (shipped → scores). Deploy = set a Ready cask onto an open slot.
Ship · Charter
A Ship is a neutral hull (Cog 2 / Hulk 3) with its destination printed, from the shared ship deck; anyone loads their own casks (loading commits, pays nothing yet); it sails only when full, and only then does every cask deliver — each owner banking that destination's benefit + its points, in load order — before the hull returns to the deck. A Charter ships one of your deployed Ready casks (v2.8 deploy-first; the Quaymaster also reaches your vessels) for a charter contract + a flat 2 G fare (start with 2; buy more at the Market, 1 G) — less efficient, but a deadlock guard.
Destination · Kontor · Hall
Where a ship delivers. Kontore pay trade value + majorities (volume); the Hall pays prestige (scarcity).
Majority
Tiered, ranked by delivered casks at a kontor (1st/2nd/3rd take the tiers; 2p skips 2nd). Concentrated at Bergen.
Recipe · Privilege · Building
Recipe = permission to brew a type · Privilege (blue) = an owned slot tile whose bonus value pays its owner only · Building (green) = an owned slot tile that transforms the cask/ship docked on it, for whoever docks. No payments between players at delivery (wharfage: retired, v2.3).
The Floor
The standing 3rd line (v2.2): after moving, choose it instead of the row/column — the actions of the casks held in your vessels + a Wild per flipped building. Never tolled. A held cask works before it ships, so hold vs deploy is a real call. Flipped buildings kept there also score +3★ at game end (v2.9 — the 4 slots are shared with Specialists).
Sailed-Ships track
The shared end clock; each voyage fills one slot. When full → finish the round → score.

18Quick Reference

Your turn

  1. Move to an orthogonally adjacent station (mandatory).
  2. Choose a line: the row, the column, or your Floor (private, never tolled). A public line while sharing the station costs 1 G to the supply.
  3. Resolve the line's stops, any order, all optional. Both stations fire; stations never closed; buildings modify their docked occupant (never a stop of their own).

At the Wharf

Market (gain goods, or acquire a recipe / a Building [display → place at once] / commission a ship / a charter contract) → Brewhouse (Brew — take the top of a quality pile; or Deploy) → Cellar (Age · Tap · buy a Specialist, any order) → Harbor (Ship to a kontor → sails only when full → delivers; or Enshrine a deployed Q2+ cask into the Hall; or Charter a deployed Ready cask — the Quaymaster also reaches your vessels). Or work your Floor, the standing 3rd line.

Where to ship

Bruges (+1, 2 goods, maj 4/2/0) · London (+1, a Privilege/Building placed at once OR a Specialist, maj 5/3/1) · Bergen (+1, free Reach, maj 9/5/2 — the anchor) · Novgorod (value scales 2/4/6 by quality, refine a cask of your choice, maj 8/5/2). A kontor delivery = the destination's starting value + the demand die (building ★ + Q4/Q5 premium, max 6). Enshrine a deployed Q2+ cask into the Hall (no ship/fare) for fixed prestige — 3/5/7/9 by quality. Each cask owner scores & takes their benefit when it delivers, in load order.

Game ends

The Sailed-Ships track fills (each voyage = 1 slot — a full sail, a Charter, or an Enshrine) → finish the round → score: Hall (fixed) + kontor deliveries (starting value + die, never less than 1★) + Majorities (tiered; 2p skips 2nd) + the Flight (range; 6→25 with Jopenbier) + the floor bonus (+3★ per displaced building kept on your floor, v2.9).
