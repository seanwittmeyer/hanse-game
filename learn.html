Brewhouses of the Hanse

Learn the basics, then play.
2–5 players · ~45–60 min at 2p · v0.7 “The Wharf” · Components · Full Rulebook · Play

1The story

It's around 1350, and the Hanseatic League runs the trade of northern Europe. You are a merchant brewing house — a family firm in a League city. You buy grain and hops, brew casks of beer, and ship them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts — the kontore at Bergen, London, Bruges, and Novgorod.

Whoever has shipped the most valuable casks when the last ship sails wins. The whole game is one production loop — Source → Brew → Age → Ship — repeated until the clock runs out.

3The grid — and the circuit you walk

The four steps sit on a shared 2×2 grid, and they're arranged so the loop runs in a circle. On your turn you move your worker one step to a neighbouring cell, then fire that cell's row or column — which triggers both cells on that line. So every turn does two steps of the loop.

Going clockwise — Market → Brewhouse → Cellar → Harbor — walks the loop. You can't do all four every turn; you choose which two-step segment to run. Choose which two actions to resolve each turn. Cells are never blocked — a rival sharing a cell doesn't stop you.

Around the grid are 8 slots — the wharf. Your Ready casks, your ships, and a couple of shared town buildings sit here, and whatever sits on the line you fire gives you a bonus action. More wharf tiles create more bonus actions.

4Your Casks

Each brewed cask moves through three states:

① Maturing

In a vessel on your private board, ageing toward Ready. Safe — no rival can touch it here.

② On the wharf

Once Ready, you set it out in a shared slot. Now it's two things: your cargo waiting for a ship, and a bonus action anyone who fires its line can use. Higher-quality beers give stronger actions.

③ Delivered

Loaded onto a ship and shipped to a destination — it scores there and leaves the board.

The key decision: leave a cask on the wharf (keep using its action) or ship it now (score it, free the slot, before a rival ships it for you).

5Ships & the wharf

You build and own your ships (at the Market) — a Cog carries 2 casks, a Hulk carries 3. Each is bound to a destination when you build it. You load a Ready cask from the wharf onto a ship at the Harbor — or for free whenever a ship's line fires. The instant a ship is full, it sails: every cask aboard is delivered, and the ship is used up and moves to the Sailed-Ships track (the game's clock). Build another to ship again.

Charter Voyage. Ships get used up and the wharf is tight, so the Harbor always offers a Charter: pay 2 goods to send one Ready cask (from a vessel or the wharf) on a single trip now. It scores like any delivery and advances the clock — but it's only one cask, so owning ships is the more efficient option. The Charter is a backup option when your wharf is jammed.

Shipping a Rival's Cask: on your turn you may load a rival's wharf cask onto your ship. They still score it (and pick its bonus) — but on your timing, and you freed the slot and advanced your ship. Your private brewery is always safe.

6Choosing Destinations

Every destination requires a certain quality of beer, and each pays differently. Kontore reward volume; the Hall rewards quality:

Volume

Ship to the kontore — Bruges, London, Bergen, Novgorod — for trade value and majorities (leading a kontor). kontore
vs

Prestige

Send your finest to the Hall — a destination that pays prestige scaled to quality, outside of trade. the Hall

Both use the same action — ship a cask — to different destinations. You may pursue either approach as the board develops. Some destinations also hand you a brewery upgrade when you deliver.

7Your Brewery and Upgrades

Your private board is your brewery. It holds your vessels (you start with two; more vessels = more casks brewing at once), the recipes you can brew (you start with Gruit and Hopped; acquire export recipes to reach higher tiers), and your upgrades.

Rooms & modifiers

Upgrades like an Extra Vessel, an Aging Cellar (faster maturation, and unlocks the top beer), or perks like "when you gain hops, take an extra." Different players build different breweries.

Earned by delivering

You mostly earn upgrades by shipping: delivering to London, Bergen, or Novgorod lets you take one from the Market's face-up upgrade row at no goods cost (or pay to buy one). Delivering improves future turns: deliver → earn an upgrade → brew better → deliver better.

8How a game ends — and how you win

There's a visible clock: the shared Sailed-Ships track. Every ship that sails adds one slot; when it fills, the round finishes and the game ends. The more ships sail, the faster the game ends. A volume player can push to end it quickly; a prestige player keeps a steadier pace to get more delivering turns.

Then everyone tallies up from what they delivered:

Deliveries & majorities

Add up the value of your delivered casks at each destination (kontore = trade value; the Hall = prestige), plus a bonus for each majority you hold at a kontor.

Goals

A few goal tiles reward different strategies — most distinct beers, most to one kontor, most ships sailed, and so on. Your best few score. Most total points wins.
