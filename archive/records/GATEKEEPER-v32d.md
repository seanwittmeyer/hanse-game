# Gatekeeper Review — v3.2d "The Flight on the cards" (KEY `hanse-v32d`)

*Standing review-board verdict, 2026-07-14. Question put to the board: at a **$64 MSRP /
$54 KS** price frame, is the game good enough on gameplay, decision space, theme, and
strategy — refinement and balance still pending? Evidence: the 15-game Cellarmaster corpus
(`playtests/logs/cm-v32d/` — turn-by-turn logs, README, ANALYSIS), the full canon
(`RULES.md` · `COMPONENTS.md` · `learn.html` · `index.html`), the prior review
(`GATEKEEPER.md`, v2.8), and two human playtests on record (DESIGN.md, 2026-07-11/12).
Panel run as four independent reviewers; where they disagreed, the disagreement and its
resolution are recorded. No table data was fabricated; every claim that only a human table
can settle is marked.*

---

## 1 · The verdict

**Yes — conditionally, and the conditions are short.** On the four axes asked: **gameplay,
decision space, and strategy are good enough now**, and that is an evidence claim, not a
vibe — legible loss passes with traced examples, the v2.8 exploit list is verifiably closed,
three strategic archetypes are live with no dominant line, and the game is **deeper than
Distilled**, the comp it will sit beside at $54. **Theme is good enough at the core**
(hopped-beer-as-cargo, deploy-first-as-public-showing, and the hijack economy are genuinely
fused) with abstraction seams — the coin board, the demand die — that the art direction must
carry rather than the rules. What keeps this from a clean strong yes is not capability but
**finishing**: three of 27 deck tiles aren't earning their slot, the teach still omits the
game's signature rule, the 2p pace and the FAME-race *feel* are bot-verified but
human-unverified, and the box as manifested does not clear $64. Every one of those is
cheap relative to what's already built.

**The delta verdict (did the changes land?):** v2.8's board said "not yet" over three
structural leaks — the overbuild carousel, the enshrine treadmill, zero-point deliveries.
All three are closed in the corpus (overbuilds ~0.6/game with rent paid; coins one-shot
with a quality-floored launch; every kontor pays ≥1★). The panel spread inverted
accordingly: v2.8 ran Optimizer-**no** / others yes-ish (an exploitable surface on great
bones); v3.2d runs **Optimizer-yes** with the holdouts gating on presentation and
validation — the most fixable spread there is.

## 2 · The panel

**The Critic — weak yes.** The shared-hull hijack economy is a mechanism neither GWT nor
Distilled owns, it fires constantly, and losses are nameable. But at a $64 shelf beside two
games with almost no filler, the logs surface filler: Rope Walk mints charter contracts
nobody spends (a free stop with no opportunity cost is rules-text, not a decision), two
tiles are dead at strong play, and the Flight added zero separation at the four-way mirror.
*Flips to strong yes when:* every free mint is priced the way ground rent priced overbuild,
and a human table confirms the FAME opening isn't fixed and 2p doesn't grind.

**The Buyer — stock-if-X.** The hook is provably real — rival-loading fires ~2.8×/game, so
the one-sentence pitch is a promise a demo can keep. But the BOM doesn't clear $64 at a
standard retail multiple by the studio's own math, and `learn.html`'s "ten minutes /
45–60 min at 2p" claims are unvalidated marketing that would burn con scheduling. *Flips
when:* one real con-floor cycle with clocked teach/runtime, and a resolved BOM-vs-price
call (diet or reprice).

**The Optimizer — yes, with a watchlist.** Legible loss passes: 2p-1's endgame (a Q5 spent
on a 0★ coin to buy the passage that closed the clock — which handed the opponent the last
word and a 2-point flip-back) reads as "the move that won me the lead guaranteed their
reply"; 2p-2's 24-point loss traces to two Privileges bought and never routed through —
departure-sequencing is a real skill and the whiff is nameable. FAME is a strong heuristic,
not an opening book (first non-Common claims turn 16→48; beaten outright by pure Novgorod
in 2p-5). No 4p lockout, no dominant line, AP earns its keep, no multiplayer-solitaire.
*Flips to strong yes when:* a human confirms the unrouted-Privilege loss reads as "I
misplayed" not "the rules screwed me," and human 2p clocks are checked before touching
the pace dials.

**The Bridge Player — yes with a good teach.** ~17 non-obvious concepts stand between a
stranger and turn 1; the warm start and all-optional stops soften them, and there's an
undersold guided first line (warm Gruit → Harbor → charter dispatch = a self-controlled
first score in ~4 turns). The open wound: the rival-loading teach sentence flagged at v2.8
is *still missing* from `learn.html` while the mechanic fires ~3×/game. *Flips when:* that
sentence lands, a genuine blind teach confirms the Floor reads as refuge not trap, and
demo tables are seat-balanced against the 2–4× skill cliff.

**Panel disagreement, resolved:** the Critic read the 98% FAME claim rate as
opening-book risk; the Optimizer traced actual claim timings and counter-lines and found
spread and a proven refutation. The board sides with the traced evidence — **hot lane, not
solved line** — and sends the *feel* question ("race or lockout?") to the human-table list
where it belongs.

## 3 · Strengths (mechanics, with receipts)

- **The hijack economy couples tempo, denial, and generosity in one gesture.** Loading a
  rival's cask is simultaneously board-clearing, clock-pushing, destination-steering, and
  a 1G fee — and both sides profit (pbp-2p-1 turn 11; ~2.8/game corpus-wide). No comp owns
  this.
- **Departure-sequencing is a real, invisible-until-it-bites skill layer.** The one-read
  die pays only when your cask leaves *your* Privilege's slot — 2p-2's loser paid for two
  and routed zero. That's Lisboa-grade regret in one rule.
- **The dual clock manufactures endgame drama.** 2p-1's finish — score inverting twice in
  three turns around one Bergen anchor, ended by a deliberately spent Q5 — is the kind of
  story reviewers retell. Trigger split across the corpus: ships 10 / presence 5.
- **The exploit list is closed and stayed closed under deep search.** The strongest AI in
  the house found no carousel, no treadmill, no zero-point tempo line in ~800 turns.
- **The turn floor holds.** Zero null/thin turns at strong play; occupancy tolls (32× in
  one 80-turn game) prove the board is being *read*, not soloed.

## 4 · Weaknesses and cut corners

- **Three of 27 deck tiles aren't earning their table space.** Rope Walk: 27 free-contract
  triggers vs 5 charters spent in one game — a zero-decision trickle. Pilot's House: 0 uses
  in 15 games. Open Staithe: 1. A $64-shelf euro should have zero dead cards.
- **The Flight converges at equal skill.** All four mirror players hit flight-16 in 4p-2;
  a whole scoring pillar produced no separation at the top table (flight-16 in 9 of 15
  winner lines). It still *shapes* play as the unlock engine — but don't count it for
  late-game drama, and the (n−1)² ladder is doing engine work, not scoring work, at the
  ceiling.
- **The teach omits the signature rule.** "A rival can ship your barrel" — the game's whole
  pitch — appears nowhere in `learn.html`'s Harbor text. Second review in a row to flag it.
- **The cheap-volume trap is legible but unannounced.** 2p-2 shows all-Q1-spam losing by
  24 to invested play — correct design, but a first-timer's default line; needs one teach
  note ("cheap beer fills hulls; it doesn't win games").
- **Time/teach claims are unvalidated.** "~45–60 min at 2p" vs a 22.4-round strong-play
  average (bot rounds ≠ human minutes, and human playtests ran 8–11 rounds pre-v3.2 —
  but nobody has clocked v3.2d with humans).
- **The manifest is over its price.** ~130 large tiles, 3 shared boards, ~230 bits, 3 bags
  ≈ FOB $10–13 → $64 MSRP is a 3.9–4.7× multiple. Diet or reprice (see §8).

## 5 · The depth dial

**Too thin:** the three named tiles; the Specialists' seven one-line effects read sparse
next to GWT's building texture (adequate, not rich). **Too punishing:** nothing
structurally — the 2–4× skill cliff is the depth audience's feature, but it is a *demo
hazard* (seat-balance or script first games), and the cheap-volume punishment needs its
teach note. **Loss legibility: PASS** — the load-bearing test, with traced examples (§2
Optimizer). **Skill converts:** three archetypes proven at strong play (Hall-rush
highest-ceiling/highest-variance; quality-invested kontor+majority the check; breadth-braid
the mirrors' winner), racer fringe-viable (won once, by 1). No dominant line found by deep
search. **Where only the table can rule:** whether the FAME race feels like a race or a
lockout to the player who arrives second; whether 2p human pace sags the way CM mirrors do
(do NOT touch `SAILED_CAP`/`PRES_POOL` until clocked); whether the unrouted-Privilege loss
reads as skill or as gotcha.

## 6 · The comp face-off (at $64/$54)

**vs Distilled ($60):** Brewhouses **wins depth** — the deploy-first / over-deploy /
departure-sequencing stack out-couples Distilled's card-market variance, and loss is
diagnosable turn-by-turn (the five-alarm test passes, per the Optimizer's read). Distilled
**wins warmth and ritual** — batch-building ceremony and instant theme charm — and teach
speed. At $54 we now *undercut* it. Why keep ours: it's the one that rewards mastery.
**vs GWT ($70–80):** Brewhouses **wins interaction** decisively (their deliveries are
near-solitaire; our every sail is multiplayer) and now **draws board coherence** — ground
rent ended the late-game thrash the v2.8 board dinged us for. GWT **wins systems density**
(deck-building + track + buildings = three interlocked engines to our ~two) and pedigree.
At $54 vs their $75 street, the value question flips in our favor for the interaction
buyer. Why keep ours: table talk. **The honest line for the campaign page:** the tightest
synthesis of the shared-action-piece lineage in years — cashless, consumable, and louder
at the table than either comp.

## 7 · Pull diagnosis

**Dominant engine: depth pull — and it matches intent** (DESIGN.md's pillars say squeeze,
crisp-turns-deep-decisions; the corpus says legible loss + high ceiling). The variety
subsystems (3-of-4 export draft, steerable pile-tops, tile displays) are *authored*
variance in the Orléans mold — serving depth, not at war with it. One watch: the building
display refill is the game's only true RNG faucet, and singleton Privileges can gate an
archetype's timing — record, don't dial. **Retention is NOT hostage to the expansion
calendar** — the spine (Specialty Beers · Jopenbier · Trade Roads) is dessert, exactly as
intended. Underexploited sliver: **fantasy pull** — "master brewer of a Hanse house" is in
the fiction but not yet in the components' voice; the art pass is where that gets cashed.

## 8 · The path forward

**Must-fix before shelf-ready (order matters):**
1. **The deck-integrity pass.** Price or cap Rope Walk (once per round, or 1G, or cap 3
   held contracts); rework or cut Pilot's House and Open Staithe (a 25-tile deck with zero
   dead cards beats 27 with three). *Test: re-run the corpus matrix; every work ≥2 uses/15
   games; contracts held at game end ≤3 median.*
2. **The teach patch.** Add the rival-loading sentence to `learn.html`'s Harbor line (the
   Bridge Player drafted it), the cheap-volume note, and replace unvalidated time claims.
   *Test: a blind reader can state the rival-loading rule after one read.*
3. **The human-validation set** (the questions no sim can answer): three blind teaches
   with clocked teach + runtime; human 2p pace before touching any clock dial; the
   FAME-race feel question at a 4p human table. *Test: teach ≤20 min; 2p ≤90 min; no
   player reports "locked out" of the Hall unprompted.*
4. **Resolve BOM vs $64.** Named diet: fold the Market & Stores board's displays onto the
   wharf sheet edges, casks 2.5"→2.25", audit the 230-bit wood count (goods cubes 100→80
   covers observed play), keep 3 bags. Target FOB ≤$10.50. If the diet guts table
   presence, the price moves, not the game.

**Would elevate it above the comps:**
5. Make the die-set moment *louder* — it's the theme moment (your reputation rides the
   barrel out) and the skill moment (2p-2's whiff); a printed "departure" ritual on the
   Privilege tiles would teach the skill the loser lacked.
6. A Specialist texture pass — one extra clause of asymmetry each, so the seven read as
   people, not +1s (feeds the fantasy sliver §7).
7. The guided-first-line insert (the Bridge Player's 4-turn charter arc printed as "Your
   First Sale") — cheap, and it converts the skill cliff from wall to ladder.

**Nice to have:** narrate/pbp-stats label fixes (already logged in ANALYSIS §6); Studio
listing corrected 2–5p → 2–4p; demo seating script for previews.

**If Sean does only one thing: the deck-integrity pass (#1).** It's the last place the
design itself still reads unfinished to a skeptical buyer — everything else on the list is
validation and dressing on a system the strongest search we own could not break.
