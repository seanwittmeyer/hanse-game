# The Gatekeeper Panel

Four archetypes whose approval actually gates a euro's success. They gate on
different things, so a game can pass one and fail another — and that spread is
itself diagnostic. Give each a distinct, opinionated voice and a concrete "what
would flip my vote" line. Don't blur them into one mushy consensus.

## The Critic
*Model: a heavy-euro reviewer who has played everything and is bored of most of it.*
Cares about: whether the game **earns its weight**; whether the decisions are
genuinely interesting or dressed-up busywork; whether it has a **reason to
exist** next to its comps, or is a competent retread; theme–mechanism fusion.
Allergic to: rules overhead that doesn't pay for itself, "solved" feeling,
decisions that resolve themselves. Flips to yes when there's a signature idea
the genre doesn't already own, executed cleanly.

## The Buyer
*Model: a distribution / retail / con buyer deciding whether to stock it.*
Cares about: the **one-sentence hook**, who exactly buys this, shelf
differentiation, teach-and-demo-ability on a con floor, reprint and longevity
risk, whether it has legs past the launch crowd. Thinks in "who do I sell this
to and how fast does it move." Allergic to: "it's like [popular game] but…"
with no clear edge; games that need ten minutes to explain why they're
interesting. Flips to yes when the hook is sellable to someone who already owns
200 games.

## The Optimizer
*Model: a hardcore BGG euro player who min-maxes and posts strategy threads.*
Cares about: **depth and legible loss** (the core test), AP load, runaway-leader
and kingmaking risk, whether skill reliably beats luck, solvability / dominant
strategies, multiplayer-solitaire drift (does what opponents do actually
matter?). Allergic to: illegible loss, a single dominant line, swing that
overwhelms skill. Flips to yes when there's a deep, diagnosable decision space
where getting better is felt game to game.

## The Bridge Player
*Model: a heavy-curious player who likes a challenge but isn't a grognard.*
Cares about: the **on-ramp** — teach length, first-game feel, whether the heavy
parts are reachable or a wall; whether the first game is fun even when played
badly; whether there's a visible engine-build payoff they can feel without
mastery. Allergic to: punishing-from-turn-one designs, feeling lost, a heavy
front that hides whatever's good. Flips to yes when the first game is satisfying
and the depth is visible without being mandatory to enjoy.

## Using the spread

The *pattern* of pass/fail is the signal:
- Optimizer no / Bridge yes → too thin or too solved; great on-ramp, no ceiling.
  Likely running on comfort or variety pull, not depth.
- Optimizer yes / Bridge no → deep but walled-off; punishing on-ramp. Owns the
  hardcore niche, leaves money and reach on the table.
- Critic no / Buyer yes → sells but doesn't earn its weight; a hook with a
  hollow core, longevity risk.
- Buyer no / Critic yes → a great game nobody can pitch; the most fixable
  failure (it's a positioning problem, not a design one).
- All four no on the same axis → that's your headline, lead the verdict with it.
