# V5-DECISIONS — every ruling from v5.0 to v5.3b, and how the pieces fit (2026-08-22)

**Purpose (designer-ordered):** one document holding the decisions of the v5 line so far —
what each ruling changed, what it replaced, why it was made, and how the details fit the
whole. The per-version implementation records stay canonical for engine detail
(`V5-OPEN-WHARF.md` · `V51-WHARF-HANDS.md` · `V52-GROUNDWORK.md` · `V53-BOURSE.md`, plus
the studies); this is the map.

---

## The game in one paragraph (as it stands at v5.3b)

You run a brewing house on a shared wharf: four stations (Market · Brewhouse · Cellar ·
Harbor) ringed by 8 slots. A turn = move, activate the row or column you stand on, resolve
its two station actions (yours at full strength, the other as its echo) and its two slots
(loads of docked Ships, plus whatever the buildings there do on their own). **The die is
the cask**: brewing stands a tray die at the printed start value, aging turns it up to
quality (READY), boarding reads it against the Kontor's minimum, delivery parks it as
presence and scores **die + the beer's Bourse marker**. Thirteen dice are your whole game —
the first empty tray calls the final round. Around that spine sit four value systems:
**the Bourse** (the beer-price track your own brews crash and your deliveries pump),
**the Kontor prizes** (recipes at Bruges, Ventures at London, specialists + a market shift
at Bergen, the flat +2★ at Novgorod), **the majorities** (parked dice), and **the Flight**
(distinct beers shipped). Public Works are the wharf's die-less furniture, dealt at setup;
Ventures are the only buildings players raise — each face paying the whole wharf a small
public line and its owner a private one.

## v5.0 "Open Wharf" (2026-08-18) — the wharf opens up

Ruled off the 4p human playtest; the brief: *"less tight, less a-vs-b, more fluid where
needed."* Three structures:

1. **CENSUS STACKS.** Each beer's supply became a real, searchable stack of its own
   printed tiles (Gruit ×16 · Hopped ×12 · exports ×6 …). A full Brew **searches and
   chooses** its tile — the load bonus you want became a pick, not a gamble; only the
   alternate Brewhouse drew blind. Delivered tiles return to the bottom; an empty stack
   can't brew. *Replaced:* the six face-up piles with steered tops (v4.7a).
   *(Amended at v5.2b: the search narrowed to the station's full Brew + the Brewery
   Venture; the `Brew 1` load bonus went back to the top tile.)*
2. **MANIFESTS.** The 15-Order row retired whole; a **12-card demand deck** rides every
   non-Bruges hull (3 generic lines; each delivered cask may claim ONE line it satisfies,
   each line once per voyage; the card recycles pristine). Demand became hull-shaped and
   forward-visible instead of a shared row race. Forced reworks: Chronicler +2★/claim ·
   the Exchange re-manifests · hall ⚜ moved to claims.
3. **PRIMARY / ALTERNATE stations.** The worker's own station fires its primary, the
   line's other station its echo: Market 2/1 (3/1 since v5.2b) · Brewhouse search/top ·
   Cellar 3/1 · Harbor commission / Load-1-any. A line stopped being "a station and a
   half" and became two real actions of different weights. *Measured:* +1.5 rounds,
   deeper into the pace band; the echo strength is the pace dial.

*Why it matters to the whole:* v5.0 fixed the **texture** of a turn (choice inside every
activation) without touching the value systems. The buildings & specialists passes were
explicitly deferred — which is what made v5.1–v5.3 necessary.

## v5.1 "Wharf Hands" (2026-08-19) — buildings try to be riders

Ruled off the designer's manifesto (*"the core actions come from the wharf stations…
specialists modify the station actions… the buildings are modifiers and less actions to
take"*): converted buildings became **riders** boosting the matching station action fired
on their line; the shelf turned (Scrivener's Hall, Hiring Post, and — ruled hard — the
**Almoner's Stall** retired: presence placement flows only through casks, always free);
station superpowers rewrote the specialist roster icon-first (+ Broker, Brewer's Mate).

*The correction that followed:* the designer read the riders as *"action buildings that
were reworded"* — buildings modifying **station actions** never matched his model of
buildings modifying **their own slot**. The rider-scope A/B (v5.1r) read NULL and the
whole rider grammar was retired at v5.2. **The kept lesson: a building is a modifier of
its slot's traffic — a docked ship, a loading cask, a sail from here — never of an
action.** The v5.1 oracle also established the standing skill reads: the skilled game
crescendos (~+4–5 rounds), majorities+Flight finish winners, Novgorod runs cold at 2p.

## v5.2 "Groundwork" (2026-08-22) — the two families

The buildings rethink, ruled in one conversation:

1. **Two families.** PUBLIC WORKS (brown — green is a player colour) fully passive on
   their own slot's traffic; the builder as **investor** — a mason's die standing on the
   tile, ticking on any use, **maturing at 6** (+6★, die home, tile demolished).
   VENTURES — the GWT-style private family: an identical **hand of 4 dual-use tiles**
   (L1 face from hand, L2 face over your own L1, the spent side forfeit), owner's colour
   ring, no die, never overbuilt by a rival.
2. **The Ladder** (public → L1 → L2): an L1 placeable only while one of your dice stood
   on a Public Work — fund the commons, earn private ground.
3. **Tile calls:** Customs eased to −1 (*"−2 is already almost broken"*), Rich Berth cut
   (*"meh"*), Ropewalk reworked to the cross-quay load, **Staple Houses ×4** entered
   (the *Stapelrecht* destination premium), the Exchange/Racking/Assay/Abbey/Granary/MQ
   ideas folded into the Venture faces.

**v5.2b — the same-day letter** off the first hands-on read:
- **The Ladder became the ground itself** — an L1 had to REPLACE a Public Work you
  invested in (pips banked, die home). *(Lived one letter — see v5.3.)*
- **`Brew 1` load bonus = top tile** (the search is the station's edge).
- **Market primary Source 3** (*"the game is slower because of the lack of resources"*) —
  the first pure faucet widening of the line; measured ~½–1 round faster, +3–5★ totals.
- **The Bergen fallback** confirmed and printed: no seat to fill → 2 goods (1G+1H).

*What the full v5.2b oracle then showed (ORACLE-STUDY-v52):* **Ventures were dead at
every tier** — even deep search wouldn't pay fee + a standing work + its ticks + the
forfeited +6 for a face needing three activations to pay. Weigh House, Ropewalk,
Tollhouse and the Staples never fired. Buildings arrived in the last two quarters —
engines landed after the game could use them. The pool sweep priced the runway (14 dice =
+1.6 rounds, best band on record). And the live 2p game surfaced the **London multi-prize
door-slam** (3 casks = 3 free builds = a tray dumped and the game ended) plus the
designer's core read: *"it feels long… I want round 13 to feel like 2/3 of the way
through… I haven't built an engine."*

## v5.3 "The Bourse" (2026-08-22) — the market, and the wharf resolved

Three rulings that answered the oracle and the live game at once:

1. **THE BOURSE — the beer-value market.** One shared track (−1…+3 ⚙), a **price marker
   per in-play beer except Gruit and Jopenbier**, starting 0. Ruled exactly: **"Die plus
   Track. Bulk rise then score. One marker per beer, they can share a single track."**
   Every brew slips its beer −1 (supply); as a Ship lands, each beer rises +1 per cask
   aboard, *then* the casks score die + marker (floor 0). Shifts ride Bergen's prize
   (*"specialist + adjust market by 1"*) and the Venture public lines — never a new
   action. The designer's frame: Lisboa's value tracks / Maracaibo's influence tracks;
   the lane replaces what presence-as-action once held, without the targeted feel-bad.
   **This is the negative-feedback loop that balances the loosening** (Source 3 + the
   freed dice): monoculture crashes its own price; contrarian brewing and timed sails
   pay; Kontor specialization (Staples + track) gets its ground.
2. **PUBLIC WORKS ARE THE START OF THE GAME.** *"You never put a die on them because
   they simply start the game. Randomly choose 3 or 4… rest to the box."* Die-less
   neutral furniture on random slots; no deck, display, build channel, fee or maturity —
   **the v5.2 investor grammar retired after one letter.** The mason's-mark scoring lane
   left with it; the 13 dice now serve casks and presence alone (~2 dice/player of
   runway freed — the maneuvering-space ask answered structurally, and the London
   door-slam died with the build prizes).
3. **THE OPEN GROUND + THE PUBLIC LINES.** An L1 takes **any open slot**; only a full
   wharf lets it replace a Public Work; never another player's L1/L2. And **every Venture
   face prints a public line** (*"a private action for the owner and a public action for
   everyone… the owner gets both. That will grease the wheels"*): age +1 (Rack/Brewery) ·
   +1 good (Counting/Assay/Warehouse) · Bourse ±1 (Factor/Residence) · +2▲ (Staple
   Rights) — a free stop for whoever activates the line. London's prize became a
   fee-waived Venture; the bonus verb reprints *Open 1 Venture*. *Measured immediately:*
   ventures went from 0.1–0.3/game to 1.7–5.3 — the family lives.

**v5.3b — the toll bench.** *"One of the public buildings should have a ±1 bourse."* The
Tollhouse (dead through three versions) reprints: a cask loading at its slot lets the
loader shift any marker ±1. The stamp face retired.

## The systems and their couplings (the whole-game check)

- **The dice (13/player)** feed exactly two sinks now: casks and presence. The clock =
  the first empty tray. Every loosening lever pulls here: Source 3 (richer turns), no
  dice to buildings (longer runway), pool 14 (still open — re-measure on this build).
- **The Bourse couples to:** brewing (crash), delivery (rise-then-score), Bergen (shift
  rider), the Tollhouse (loader shift), four Venture public faces (±1/+2▲), the Staples
  (a matching sail + a high marker is the designed combo), and the Flight (breadth play
  now also diversifies your market exposure). It deliberately does NOT couple to: gates,
  presence, majorities, the clock — the die alone reads there.
- **Public Works couple to:** setup variance (the 3–4 drawn tiles ARE this game's wharf),
  loads/sails at their slots, and the late-game ground market (a full wharf lets L1s eat
  them). No dice, no ownership — pure commons.
- **Ventures couple to:** the goods economy (grain fees), London (the fee-waiver prize),
  every player's line choices (the public lines make rivals' visits pay you nothing but
  make YOUR ground attractive), and the Bourse (three shift faces). The hand of 4 is a
  per-player budget: four wide L1s, two full climbs, or between.
- **The Kontore divide the acquisition economy:** Bruges = recipes (fee H=Q−3, paid even
  there) · London = Ventures (waived) · Bergen = specialists + the market shift (fallback
  2 goods) · Novgorod = the flat +2★ premium behind the die-3 gate. Manifests ride every
  non-Bruges hull on top.
- **Specialists modify YOUR stations and flows** (the v5.1 keeper) — they never touch the
  building families, so the two rosters can't collide.
- **Expansions:** the specialty beers and Jopenbier join the Bourse by the same rule
  (tracked unless Gruit/Jopenbier); the Tastings hall pours Ready casks without touching
  the market (a poured beer crashed its price at brew and never sells — an intentional
  tension to watch); the Trade Roads stay tabled.
