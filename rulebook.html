Brewhouses of the Hanse

Rulebook · 4 pages, 2 sheets, print double sided (flip on the long edge)
Brewhouses of the Hanse
A merchant brewing house on the Baltic, about 1350
2–4 players
90 minutes
12–25 rounds
Brew the beer the League wants, and get it to the right Kontor first.

1The Goal

You run a brewing house at the Wharf. Every turn you source grain and hops, brew them into casks, age those casks, and load them onto Ships bound for the four Kontore of the Hanseatic League.

A delivered cask scores ★ equal to its quality die, and that die stays parked at the Kontor as your presence there. The player with the most ★ at the end wins.

The heartRipe beer is worth more, but ripening takes turns you could have spent shipping. Everything in the game is that trade.

3What You Own

  • 13 quality dice in your colour. This is your whole runway for the game, and it is public. A die leaves your tray to become a cask or to place presence — and never comes back. No die ever stands on a building (v5.3).
  • A hand of 4 dual-use Venture tiles in your colour ring — your private buildings, waiting for ground (§8).
  • A player board: 3 vessels for aging casks, and 2 seats for Specialists.
  • Recipe cards, which are permission to brew a beer. They sit beside your board.
  • Goods: grain and hops . They are the only currency in the game.
RememberNo die in your tray means no brew and no placing presence. An empty tray ends the game, so spending dice is spending time.

4Setup

Each player takes

  • 3 grain and 2 hops . Your storage cap is 8 goods for the whole game.
  • The Gruit and Hopped recipe cards, placed on the collected side, to the left of your board.
  • 13 quality dice in your colour, and 1 score disc on space 0 of the score track.
  • The 4 Venture tiles ringed in your colour, as a hand beside your board.
  • A warm start: 1 Ready Gruit cask in your first vessel, its die set to 1.

The shared table

  • Lay out the Wharf and the Destinations board with all four Kontore open.
  • Deal 3 of the 4 export beers for this game. Return the recipe cards and cask tiles of the undealt export to the box.
  • Stack the cask tiles in 6 face-up stacks, one per beer. Shuffle each stack first. The stacks are searchable: a full Brew looks through the stack and picks its tile. A delivered cask’s tile returns to the bottom of its stack.
  • Shuffle the Ship deck and deal a face-up display of 4.
  • Public Works: shuffle all 13 tiles, draw 3 (2 players) or 4 (3–4), and stand them on random slots. The rest go back to the box — nobody builds one during play, and no die ever stands on one.
  • The Bourse: lay the strip beside the supply and set one price marker per beer in the game except Gruit and Jopenbier on 0.
  • Shuffle the Specialist deck and deal a display of 4.
  • Shuffle the 12 Manifest cards into a face-down deck on the Destinations board. Deal the top card face-up to every Ship not bound for Bruges as it enters play, the display and the warm slots alike. Bruges Ships sail plain.
  • Warm the slots: place 1 Hulk bound for Bruges and 1 more Ship from the deck on slots of different lines. These setup pieces belong to nobody.
  • Each player puts a worker on any station. Opening placement is free and may be shared. Choose a first player, who stays first all game.
Why dealOnly 3–4 of the 13 Public Works stand this game and only 3 of 4 exports are dealt, so no two wharves run the same economy. Read what was drawn before you plan.

6Your Turn, Step by Step

  1. Move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent station. Never diagonally. Sharing a station with rivals costs nothing. On your first turn you stay where you set up.
  2. Choose a line: either your station’s row or its column. A line is 2 stations and their 2 slots.
  3. Resolve the line. Work through its 4 parts in any order. Every part is optional, and each may be used once.
A line offersThe station your worker stands on fires its PRIMARY action; the line’s other station fires its ALTERNATE. Each of its 2 slots offers a load of the Ship if one is docked there — and the buildings work on their own: a Public Work fires passively on its slot’s traffic, and a Venture on the line offers its public line to you (its ringed line to its owner alone). An empty slot does nothing, so build one worth visiting.
Read it liveIf a Ship is commissioned or warped onto a slot on your line, or you build there, that slot opens this same turn. Each part still resolves at most once, and a Ship that sails away closes its slot.

7The Cask and Its Die

One die does five jobs. It is the cask’s age, its readiness, its ticket aboard, its ★ value, and a beat of the game clock. Follow one Bock through its life:

Brew Set the die to the tile’s printed start. A Bock starts at 2.
Age Each step turns it +1. Never past the beer’s quality.
Ready Die reaches the quality, here 5. Now it can load.
Board The Ship’s minimum reads the die as it boards.
Park Score 5★, and the die stays as presence.
Dice never turn on their ownAging only happens when something ages it: the Cellar, an Age +2 load bonus, or a Venture or Specialist that says so. A forgotten cask sits at its start value forever.

Buildings, Ships and DeliveryWhere the ★ comes from

8The Slots, the Buildings and the Bourse

The 8 slots ring the stations. Each holds up to 1 building underneath and 1 Ship on top. Buildings come in two families, and using either is always free.

Public Works — brown, the wharf itself

Nobody builds them — they start the game. Setup stands 3–4 at random on random slots; the rest stay in the box. A Public Work is die-less furniture: every face fires passively on its own slot’s traffic, for whoever’s cask or Ship it is. The two ephemerals sail away with the Ship at their slot; otherwise a Public Work leaves only when a full wharf forces an L1 to redevelop it. Read what came out at setup — those tiles ARE this game’s wharf.

Ventures — your colour ring, the family you build

Your hand of 4 dual-use tiles. Each face prints two lines: a simple public line on top — gain 1 good · age +1 · shift the Bourse — that fires as a free stop for whoever activates a line through the slot, and the ringed owner line below, for you alone. You collect both. Playing one side of a tile forfeits the other.

  • L1 (fee 1 grain ⚙): from your hand onto any open slot. Wharf full? It may replace a Public Work (the worn tile is boxed). Never another player’s L1/L2.
  • L2 (fee 2 grain ⚙): only by overbuilding your own L1 with a second hand tile — the spent L1 is boxed, the ground is kept. An L2 is never displaced.
  • The London prize opens either level free; the fee otherwise rides the Open 1 Venture load bonus.

The Bourse — the price of beer moves

Die + track
One shared track, −1 · 0 · +1 · +2 · +3, with a price marker per beer except Gruit and Jopenbier (they trade off-book). A delivered cask scores its die + its beer’s marker, never below 0.
  • Every brew of a tracked beer slips its marker −1 (floor −1): new supply, softer price.
  • As a Ship lands, each tracked beer’s marker rises +1 per cask of it aboard (cap +3) — the rises land first, then every cask scores. A big delivery pumps its own price.
  • Shifts: Bergen’s prize moves any marker ±1; the Ventures’ public lines print ±1 and +2▲ shifts.
At the tableBrew what the wharf is not brewing — your own volume crashes your beer — and time your sails into a risen market. The marker moves at brews, landings and shifts only; it pays at the moment of sale, so nothing needs remembering.

9Ships, Loading and Sailing

Ships are neutral. Each is bound for a printed Kontor and prints its minimum. They come from a display of 4, and every Ship not bound for Bruges carries a face-up Manifest card from the moment it enters the display.

To load

Resolve a slot with a Ship docked at it. Take 1 Ready cask from your own vessels whose die meets the Ship’s minimum, seat it in the lowest empty berth, and fire the tile’s printed load bonus. The vessel it leaves is open again.

Order of eventsThe minimum reads the die as it boards, which means after this slot’s lifts. If the load fills the Ship, it sails and delivers first, and the cask’s load bonus fires after that.

To sail

A Ship sails the moment it is full. A Skute sails on its first load.

Each cask aboard then delivers in boarding order. For each one, in turn:

  1. Score ★ equal to its die.
  2. Park the die at the Kontor, pips showing. It is now your presence there.
  3. Take the Kontor’s prize. Every cask pays its Kontor’s prize.
  4. You may claim 1 line of the Ship’s Manifest the cask satisfies. Each line pays once per voyage.

The Ship returns to the bottom of its deck, its Manifest slides under the Manifest deck, and every delivered cask’s tile returns to the bottom of its beer’s stack.

The berth raceCasks are private until they board, so nobody can touch your beer. What rivals take is the berth: topping off a Ship sails everyone’s cargo on your clock. If a rival tops off a Ship carrying your cask, you choose your prize right then, on their turn. Nothing is held over.

Presence

Your presence at a Kontor is your parked dice there. They decide the majorities scored at the end of the game.

Some effects let you place presence. Take a tray die and park it at face 1 at a Kontor you have already delivered to. It scores 1★ and counts toward majority. It is always free.

Manifests

Every non-Bruges Ship carries a Manifest card: three generic demand lines — a named starter, a quality tier, a die face, or a combo — each worth 1★ to 4★.

As the Ship sails, each delivered cask may claim one line it satisfies; the ★ score at once. A die line reads the parked face, after lifts and before Novgorod’s premium. The card then returns under the deck, pristine.

At the tableOne line per delivered cask, and each line pays once per voyage. Several casks on one Ship claim in boarding order, so earlier casks pick first. Claiming is always optional.

Specialists, Ending and ScoringPlus the expansion beers

11Specialists

Specialists are private purple tiles that change how you work — each is a station superpower, bending one of your stations or flows. You have 2 seats, both open from the start.

  • They are earned, never bought. Bergen’s prize seats one free. The Gain 1 Specialist load bonus seats one at the tile’s printed fee.
  • You may never hold two of the same Specialist.
  • Each tile prints exactly what it does. Most work quietly, every turn, forever.
Refill timingThe Specialist display refills at the end of the turn, so a taken tile leaves a gap for the rest of that turn. The Ship display refills at once.

12Goods and Storage

Grain and hops are the only currency. There is no money and no spendable prestige.

Your storage cap is 8 goods. Anything gained above 8 is lost.

13Ending the Game

The game runs on one clock: the first empty tray.

The moment a player commits their last quality die, that player’s tray reads 0 and the final round is set. Finish the round so every player has had the same number of turns, then score.

A die counts as committed when it is
on a cask in a vessel, riding a berth on a Ship, parked at a Kontor, or standing on a building. It does not have to be parked. Your dice always add up: tray plus in play plus parked equals 13.
At the tableSailing ends nothing. Ships are pure logistics. If play somehow runs long, the game also ends after round 25.

The Expansion Beers optional

Two independent modules you may add at setup, in any combination. With both off the base game is unchanged. Everything else runs exactly as written.

Every expansion beer is pinned: all of its cask tiles print the same load bonus, and that bonus is the beer’s signature. Choosing to brew it is the whole decision. On the Bourse: a dealt specialty beer takes a price marker at 0 like any export — brews crash it, arrivals lift it, its casks score die + marker. Jopenbier alone trades off the Bourse (below).

Module 1 · Specialty Beers

Setup deals 3 of 7 exports instead of 3 of 4, guaranteeing at least one of Mumme or Bock so the quality climb still matters. All three specialty recipes are free to gain.

GoseQ2 · 1 step · 2
Gain any 3 goods. Brewed from grain alone, with no hops at all. The better Gruit when the grain is running.
ZerbsterQ3 · 1 step · 3
Parti-gyle. As its cask boards you may brew a free Gruit into an open vessel, costing no goods but taking a tray die, Ready at 1. Then load any cask, the new Gruit included.
DucksteinQ2 · 1 step · 11
Place 1 presence, free. And smoke-hardy: its die turns +1 as it boards, cap 6. A Q2 beer that reaches Novgorod.

Module 2 · Jopenbier, the capstone

The Q6 vintage of Danzig, and the longest climb in the game. It is never dealt. Instead it is always available at every recipe channel for its fee of 3.

Brew it for 24. Its die starts at 2 and wants 4 aging steps. Ready at 6, it delivers 6★ anywhere and 8★ at Novgorod, and counts as a sixth beer for the Flight. It trades off the Bourse: no price marker, no crash, no rise — the 6 is contract-solid.
