1The Brewing House

Hamburg was called the brewhouse of the Hanse. You are a merchant brewing house in the League, c. 1350.

The pivotal innovation of your age is hopped beer. Unlike perishable gruit ale, hopped beer survives a sea voyage — so beer becomes a shippable export. You will source grain and hops, brew casks, mature them, and push them across the Baltic and North Sea to the great foreign trading posts (the kontore: Bergen, London, Bruges, Novgorod) — or withdraw your finest into the brewers' Hall. Whoever has shipped the most valuable casks when the last ship sails wins.

Source → Brew → Age → Ship → (repeat)

  • You brew casks, mature them, and ship them to destinations for points. Where you ship is the key decision: VOLUME the kontore (value + majorities) vs PRESTIGE the Hall (quality, out of commerce).
  • Both stations on an activated line fire — the worker only gates which line is legal. (Hover a station to light its two lines.)
  • Lines pair two stations: Market+Brewhouse Brewhouse+Cellar Harbor+Cellar Market+Harbor.
  • The 8 slots hold a churning mix of deployed casks (cargo + a public action), owned ships, and 2–3 neutral buildings. Base verbs always work; slot buildings only add.

Three rules constraints

No dice (fully deterministic) · No hidden draws (everything you acquire is taken from a face-up supply) · No money (goods are the only currency; delivered points are score only).

3Components

  • 1 Main board — the Wharf: a 2×2 of four stations (A Market · B Brewhouse · D Cellar · C Harbor) ringed by 8 slots, plus the shared Sailed-Ships track (the end clock).
  • 1 Destination board — the four kontore + the Hall, each with its quality gate, delivery value, benefit, and (kontore) a majority track (§4, §10).
  • 5 Player brewery boards — each private & untouchable: vessel lanes (start 2, cap 4), the maturation track, a recipe rack, upgrade slots, and goods storage (cap 8).
  • The tile supply — cask tiles (a shared pool with fixed counts; an ownership disc marks one on a slot or delivered), ship tiles (Cog · Hulk), neutral building tiles, recipe tiles, upgrade tiles (Rooms + Modifiers), and goal tiles. Each family is shown in its section below.
  • Goods cubes — grain (G) and hops (H), the only currency. Per player: 1 worker, ownership discs. 1 first-player marker (fixed — it does not pass).

4Anatomy of the Game

The Stations & Lines

Four stations sit in a 2×2: A Market (Source) & B Brewhouse (Brew) on top, C Harbor (Ship) & D Cellar (Age) below. You don't simply circle the board — you move where the board is best. Eight slots ring the stations, two beside each line. A line is its two stations plus any tiles in their two slots (slot · station · station · slot); each slot belongs to exactly one line. (Recipes & upgrades live on your private board, not a slot.)

The Destinations (where value lives)

The four kontore + the Hall are all open from the start. Each wants a certain quality of beer and pays differently — so there is no single best line of play. Delivery value is flat per kontor regardless of quality (only the Hall scales with Q), so a high-quality cask is wasted at Bruges and shines at Novgorod or the Hall.

Majorities are tiered & ranked (Lacerda-style): by delivered-cask count, 1st / 2nd / 3rd place take a kontor's majority tiers; 2-player games skip 2nd (winner-take-all); ties split the occupied tiers. The rich majority is concentrated at Bergen (its monopoly identity) — Bergen no longer grants an upgrade; its whole draw is the majority. London is the accessible engine kontor (an upgrade). The cask's owner always scores the delivery and takes any benefit — even when a rival shipped it.

Your Brewery (private, untouchable)

Your brewery board holds the maturation track (casks crawl Brew → … → Ready across your vessels — start 2, cap 4, each a parallel lane), a recipe rack, and upgrade slots (Rooms & Modifiers, mostly earned by delivering). Rivals can only act on your casks once they're on a slot.

5Setup

  1. Place the main board and destination board. Seed the slots with 2–3 neutral buildings ⚙ and the warm-start Cogs, spread across different lines so no single line is the obvious opening. All five destinations begin open.
  2. Deal 3 of the 4 export beers ⚙. Each carries a fixed quality — Broyhan Q3 · Keut Q3 · Mumme Q4 · Bock Q5 — so dropping one varies the ladder: drop a Q3 → the full Q3→Q5 climb; drop Mumme → no Q4; drop Bock → no Q5. Recipes & ships are an always-available Market supply; for upgrades, shuffle all Upgrade tiles into a face-down deck and deal 4 ⚙ face-up — the Upgrade display (refills from the deck as tiles are taken, §11).
  3. Set the Sailed-Ships track to its player-scaled length (it scales steeply): ~7 / 11 / 15 / 19 slots for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 players ⚙.
  4. Each player takes a brewery board, a worker, ownership discs, and a starting stake: the Gruit + Hopped recipes (printed), 2 open vessels (lanes 3–4 locked), and a warm start — one built Cog on a slot bound to Bruges, and one Ready Gruit in Vessel 1 (deploy it turn 1).
  5. Starting goods (with seat compensation): 3 , 2 , plus +1 for every seat after the first ⚙ — so seats P1…P5 start with 3 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 4 G. (Turn order is fixed, which gives the first player a small edge; the free opening placement + this +1 G neutralize it.)
  6. Each player places their worker on any station — your opening placement is free (no occupancy toll, §6). Give the first-player marker to any player (it does not pass).

Warm start

Starting with 2 vessels, a built Cog, and a Ready cask means a first voyage is reachable by turn 2–3.

6A Turn

Turn 1 only: place your worker on any station (free) and activate one of its two lines (no move). From turn 2, in order:

  1. Move your worker to an orthogonally adjacent station (A↔B, A↔C, B↔D, C↔D — never the diagonal). Mandatory. If you move onto a station a rival already occupies, pay 1 ⚙ to the supply (the occupancy toll — capped at what you hold, never blocks).
  2. Activate the row OR the column of your worker's new station.
  3. Resolve the line's up-to-4 stops — slot · station · station · slot — in any order:
    • Each station: take its action. Stations are never closed — a rival's worker never stops you using one.
    • Each slot: if it holds a building, you may use its action — a cask's signature action, a ship's free load, or a neutral building. All resolve on your turn, for you — no out-of-turn gains.

Both stations always fire

Activating a line triggers both stations; your worker only determines which line is legal. Because base verbs are always available and slot buildings only add, you are never stalled — a populated Wharf makes your turns bigger.

Play passes clockwise. The first player is fixed for the whole game — turn order does not rotate. The free opening placement and the +1 G per later seat (§5) are the seat balancers.

8The Cask & Brewing

The tile the whole game turns on, moving through three states: maturing (private vessel) → on a slot (cargo + a public action) → delivered (shipped → it scores and leaves). Beer is two layers — a type (quality rung Q1–Q5) + a recipe (permission to brew it). Casks are brewed, not bought, from a shared pool with fixed counts, so the rare export casks are contested. A loaded recipe crawls the maturation track — casks age 1 step automatically every turn, plus the Cellar pool. Click a tile to flip it.

The ladder is a fixed spine (Gruit → Hopped) + a variable export: the four historical Hanse export beers carry a fixed quality (Broyhan/Keut Q3 · Mumme Q4 · Bock Q5) and 3 of the 4 are dealt each game, so the climb differs every game. 9The Slots

The 8 slots hold your deployed casks (cargo + a public action), your ships (single-use; consumed when they sail), and 2–3 neutral buildings seeded at setup. When a line fires, the active player may use each building in its two slots: a cask fires its signature action, a ship offers a free load, a neutral building gives its base action. All resolve on the active player's turn. When a rival uses your cask's action, you still own the cask and score it when shipped.

Shipping a rival's cask

On your turn you may load a rival's deployed cask (on a slot) onto your ship — never their brewery. The destination is your ship's binding (it must be legal for that cask's quality). The owner still scores the delivery and picks its benefit; you take the freed slot, the filled ship (advancing the clock), and a small loader bonus ⚙.

10Ships & Destinations

The merchant fantasy. Build & own ships at the Market onto a slot, bound to a destination (inheriting its gate). Lifecycle load → fill → sail: load via the Harbor verb or a bonus free load when the ship's line fires (your own cask or a rival's); the instant it is full it sails — every cask delivers, and the ship is consumed onto the Sailed-Ships track.

Charter Voyage

The Harbor always offers a Charter (no tile — an outside hull): pay 2 G ⚙ to send one Ready cask — from a vessel or a slot — on an immediate single-cask voyage to a destination it qualifies for. It delivers normally and advances the clock, but is strictly worse per cask than a Cog/Hulk — so owning ships stays the better plan; the Charter just keeps the tight slots from ever deadlocking.

Delivery Benefits

Delivering to London / Novgorod hands you a brewery upgrade from the display at no goods cost (Bergen pays its big majority instead; Bruges pays goods; the Hall pays prestige). The cycle reinforces itself: deliver → earn an upgrade → brew better → deliver better.

11The Brewery & Upgrades

Your brewery holds your vessels (start 2, cap 4), your recipes, and your upgrades — where strategies diverge. Upgrades come from the Market's face-up Upgrade display (a row of 4 ⚙ dealt from a shuffled face-down deck, refilling as taken): buy one (pay its goods cost) or earn one free by delivering to London / Novgorod. Buying Upgrades (4–5 G)

Delivering to London / Novgorod hands you a display tile free — on top of the voyage's value. Buying yields only the upgrade, so it is priced at a comparable 4–5 G ⚙: a real option for bootstrapping, but the primary path is deliver → earn an upgrade → brew better.

12Goods & Storage

grain and hops are the only currency — recipes, brew inputs, ships, upgrades, loader benefits. You may hold up to 8 goods ⚙ (raised by the Warehouse room). There is no money and no spendable prestige — delivered points are score only.

13End of the Game

The game ends when the Sailed-Ships track fills. Every voyage — a ship sailing (full or launched) or a Charter — moves one slot onto the shared, visible track. Length scales with player count (~7 / 11 / 15 / 19 for 2 / 3 / 4 / 5p ⚙); a round limit (≈25 ⚙) backstops a slow table. This is a visible, steerable clock: a volume player races to sail ships fast and end it; a prestige player keeps a steadier pace. When it fires, finish the current round (equal turns for all), then score.

14Scoring

No separate reach/standing tracks. You score from what you delivered:

  1. Delivery value — sum your delivered casks' values at each destination (kontore = trade value; the Hall = prestige, scaling with quality, Q×2).
  2. Majorities — at each kontor, by delivered-cask count, 1st / 2nd / 3rd place take that kontor's tiers (Bergen 10 / 6 / 3 ⚙ is the rich one; Bruges/London 2, Novgorod 3 — minor, 1st-place-only). 2-player games skip 2nd (winner-take-all); tied players split the sum of the tiers they occupy ⚙.
  3. Goals — resolve your goal tiles; only your best few score ⚙.

Highest total wins. Tiebreak: most goods, then most casks on slots.

152-Player Variant

With fewer workers the slots feel loose, so lock some slots (down to ~6 ⚙) to tighten the competition that drives "ship a rival's cask to free a slot." The Sailed-Ships track is shortest at 2p (~7 slots ⚙), so the clock stays brisk. Majorities are winner-take-all at 2p (2nd place is skipped). Everything else plays identically.

16Glossary

  • The Wharf — the whole core area: four stations (Market · Brewhouse · Cellar · Harbor) ringed by 8 slots.
  • Station / Slot / Line — a station is one of the four action spaces; a slot is one of the 8 spaces ringing them (a deployed cask, a ship, or a neutral building); a line is a row or column = its two stations plus any tiles in their two slots.
  • Occupancy toll — 1 G ⚙ paid to the supply when you move onto a station a rival occupies (opening placement free). Never blocks.
  • Seat compensation — later seats start with +1 G each (the fixed-turn-order balancer).
  • Cask — a brewed beer in one of three states: maturing (vessel) → on a slot (cargo + a public action) → delivered (shipped → scores). Deploy = place a Ready cask onto an open slot.
  • Ship / Charter — a Ship is owned infrastructure (Cog 2 / Hulk 3) bound to a destination, consumed when it sails; a Charter is a Harbor option (pay 2 G to ship one Ready cask now), less efficient per cask but a deadlock guard.
  • Destination / kontor / the Hall — where a ship delivers. Kontore pay trade value + tiered majorities (volume); the Hall pays prestige (scarcity).
  • Majority — tiered, ranked by delivered casks at a kontor (1st/2nd/3rd take the tiers; 2p skips 2nd). Concentrated at Bergen.
  • Recipe / Upgrade / Neutral building — permission to brew a type / a Room or Modifier on your brewery (mostly earned by delivering) / a shared base action seeded on a slot.
  • Sailed-Ships track — the shared end clock; each voyage fills one slot. When full → finish the round → score.

18Quick Reference

Your turn

  1. Move to an orthogonally adjacent station (mandatory) — pay 1 G if a rival is already there.
  2. Activate its row or column.
  3. Resolve the line (slot · station · station · slot), any order. Both stations fire; stations never closed; use the buildings in the line's two slots (on your turn).

At the Wharf

Market (Source: goods/recipes/ships/upgrades) → Brewhouse (Brew a cask) → Cellar (Age to Ready → deploy to a slot) → Harbor (Ship → sails when full → delivers).

Where to ship

Bruges (+2, goods) · London (+3, an upgrade) · Bergen (+3, the big majority 10/6/3) · Novgorod (+5, an upgrade) · the Hall (Q×2, prestige). Gate by quality; owner scores & picks the benefit even on a rival's ship.

Game ends

The Sailed-Ships track fills (each voyage = 1 slot) → finish the round → score: Deliveries + Majorities (tiered; 2p skips 2nd) + Goals (best few).
