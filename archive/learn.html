Brewhouses of the Hanse

Learn to play in about ten minutes.
2–4 players · ~45–60 min at 2p · v5.1 “Wharf Hands”

1The Pitch

It's 1350. You run a merchant brewing house in a Hanseatic League city. Gather grain and hops, brew beer, age it, and sail it to the great trading posts — Bruges, London, Bergen, Novgorod. Every cask you brew is one of your 13 dice: it starts low, ages up, sails away, and lands at a Kontor showing its ★. Your dice are the clock — the moment someone commits their last die (an empty tray), the end is set. Most ★ wins.

2Setup

Each player starts with:

  • 3 grain, 2 hops
  • A player board with 3 vessel slots and 2 Specialist seats — all open from the start
  • Two recipe cards: Gruit & Hopped (Gruit starts flipped — checkmark up, already brewed) — and a Ready Gruit in vessel 1, its die showing 1
  • 13 quality dice in your colour, kept in public view — every brew takes one from your tray, every build stands one on the tile, and every delivery parks one at a Kontor. The 13 are your whole game

Shared: deal 3 of the 4 export beers face-up, fill the displays (Ships 4 · buildings 4 · Specialists 4), stack the cask tiles into six shuffled stacks (one per beer), deal a Manifest card to every non-Bruges Ship, seed a big Ship at Bruges plus one more, and deal 2 neutral buildings onto slots. Pick a start player (fixed all game). Play.

3The Wharf

Everything happens on a shared 2×2 grid of four stations, ringed by 8 slots — each slot can hold a building and a Ship (never casks; your beer stays home until it boards).

On your turn: move your worker one step to an adjacent station and choose the station's row or column. A line fires both its stations — the one under your worker at full strength (PRIMARY), the other as a lighter echo (ALTERNATE) — plus the two slots beside them: load the Ship docked at each (its buildings fire on that traffic by themselves), and your own private stops if you built them. Any order, everything optional. Sharing a station with a rival costs nothing — move where the board is best.

5The Die Is the Cask

① Aging

In a vessel, the die turning up toward the beer's quality. Private — no rival can touch it.

② Delivered

Loaded onto a Ship, sailed, and parked at the Kontor — the pips are your ★ (1–6), the die itself is your presence there.

A Gruit starts at 1 and is Ready the moment you brew it; a Bock starts at 2 and wants three steps to reach 5. Only buildings push a die past its quality — a Malt Kiln under the Ship turns it +1 as it boards (max 6) — so cheap beer has the most room to grow. Every cask also carries a printed load bonus (the station's full Brew searches the stack, so the bonus is your pick; the alternate and the Brew 1 bonus take the top tile): it fires the moment the cask boards — Gain 2 goods, Age +2, Gain 1 recipe, even Open 1 Venture. (The gain bonuses cost the item’s printed price — Ventures and Specialists arrive free as Kontor prizes; a recipe’s hop fee is paid even at Bruges.)

6Ships & the Four Kontore

Ships are neutral and pre-addressed: a Skute holds 1 cask, a Cog 2, a Hulk 3 — and each prints its commission fee: 2 grain, 1, or free, by size (the quick single-berth boat is the dear one; the big Hulk is free but needs three casks before it moves). Loading takes a Ready cask from your own vessels whose die meets the Kontor's printed minimum — Bruges 1+, London 2+, Bergen 2+, Novgorod 3+. A full Ship sails at once (a Skute sails on its very first load), and every cask aboard delivers: points, the parked die, and the Kontor's prize — Bruges a recipe — you still pay its printed fee in hops (quality − 3: Keut and Broyhan are free, a Bock recipe costs 2) · London a building (built at once — your die stands on it) · Bergen a Specialist for every cask (each cask you deliver pays its Kontor’s prize). At Novgorod there is no prize tile: every die you park there scores +2★ on top. Every non-Bruges Ship also carries a Manifest — a demand card with three lines (“Gruit”, “Q3+”, “die 5+”…, each worth 1–4★). As the Ship sails, each delivered cask may claim one line it satisfies, ★ on the spot — but each line pays only once per voyage, so boarding order matters. The card then slides back under the deck, and the delivered cask’s tile returns to the bottom of its stack — the beer supply circulates.

7Buildings — Everyone's, but You Get the ★

Buildings come in two families. Public Works (brown) are the wharf itself: 3–4 stand at random from setup — nobody builds them, no die ever stands on them — and every face fires by itself on its own slot's traffic, for whoever's cask or Ship it is: the Kiln lifts any boarding cask, the Ropewalk throws a line to a second Ship, a Staple House pays every cask on a matching sail. Read what came out before you plan — those 3–4 tiles ARE this game's wharf. Ventures are the buildings you build: your hand of four dual-use tiles, ringed in your colour, no die. Each face prints two lines: a simple public line at the top (gain a good · age a cask · nudge the Bourse) that pays whoever works a line through the slot — your private house greases the whole wharf — and the ringed line below that serves you alone (the owner collects both). An L1 takes any open slot; once the wharf is full it may replace a Public Work; an L2 climbs only your own L1 (the spent tile is boxed). A rival can never build over yours.

And the price of beer moves (v5.3 — the Bourse). Every beer except Gruit and Jopenbier has a price marker on one shared track (−1 … +3, starting 0). A delivered cask scores its die + its beer's marker. Every brew of a beer slips its marker (you flooded the market); every cask landing at a Kontor lifts it — the rises land before the voyage scores, so a big delivery pumps its own price. Bergen's prize and the Ventures' public lines let you shift any marker — brew what the wharf is not brewing, and sell into the market your rivals pumped.

Specialists are the private version of the same idea — a guild of 15 designs rotates through the display, and you only ever seat two. Each is a station superpower: it changes how YOUR stations and flows fire. Five are steady helpers (extra goods, faster starts, double loads); ten are stronger one-of-a-kind hires: free recipes, free commissions, a turn-start aging drip when three casks brew at once, +2★ every time you place a presence die, a grain↔hops swap riding your Source, freight when rivals sail your beer, a +2★ kick every time you claim a Manifest line, a Broker whose Market echo is a full Source 2, a Brewer's Mate whose Brewhouse echo searches the stack — and one that scores printed end-game ★ (Kontore flooded with your dice).

8Game End & Scoring

One clock, in plain view: your 13 dice. The moment someone commits their last die — an empty tray, even with dice still riding Ships — the end is set; finish the round, then score. (Dice never come back; every brew and every delivery spends your runway. A 25-round limit backstops a slow table.)

You score: every parked die (its pips, 1–6) + everything you scored in play (Manifest claims, Tollhouse stamps, 1★ per placed presence — always free) + every die standing on your buildings (its pips — the mason's marks) + majorities at each Kontor (most parked dice) + the Flight — your distinct beers shipped (the completed cards on your board’s right side — a card moves there when that beer first loads onto a Ship): 3→4★, 4→9★, 5→16★. Most ★ wins.
