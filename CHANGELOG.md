## v0.11 Batch B — ships are neutral, destination-bound shared hulls (2026-06-13)
The structural change from the v0.11 themes, **committed rather than toggled** (`KEY → v31`; the owned-ship world lives at v30 — git is the variant system; full rationale in `DESIGN.md` §21 "Batch B"). All surfaces updated **except `printables.html`** (deliberately stale — queued for the printables batch).
- **The rule ⚙:** ships come off a shuffled **ship deck** (~14 hulls, destination + capacity printed, next face-up by the Market; 2 dealt at setup — the new warm start). **Commission** (Market, 2 `G`) places the face-up ship on any open slot. **Load your own casks only**; full ⇒ sails — every cask owner scores + benefit, the **filler takes a 2 `G` dispatch fee**; early launch needs your cask aboard; the voyage advances the **Sailed-Ships marker** and the **hull returns to the deck bottom**. The **"ship a rival's cask" rule is cut**; **Royal Patent** → charter fare −1 `G` (min 1, buy 4 `G`); goal g4 → "ship you **filled or launched**".
- **Why:** the designer's table notes (ownership tracking finicky, rival-ship loads confusing/unused, loading needs a reason) + FINDINGS-v26 B1 (the old interaction measured anti-optimal). The new interaction is the **race for cargo space** — berth claiming, fill timing, the dispatch fee, commission placement.
- **The big finding:** printed destinations **removed the ship-binding strategy axis** — heuristic tiers converged (journeyman 51% over apprentice at first) while the **Guildmaster kept winning 95–100%** (depth relocated to placement/load-selection/timing, not vanished). The Trader was rebuilt around the new center (**ship-selection scored as a delivery**: value + majority swing + goal marginal), the Apprentice re-handicapped, and the **CEM tuner returned its first ADOPT** (54.1% CI-clear: swing 1.0, goal weight ×2, Hall further end-loaded) — adopted into `AI_W`.
- **Measured:** sim 500 ×2–5p clean (`sim-results-v31.txt`, pace 14–16 in band); ladder restored (`ai-ladder-v31-nogm.txt` N=600: 62.2 / 66.8 / 71+ ; GM rung 92.5–100% across runs, `ai-ladder-v31.txt`); first voyage ~round 4 (lighter warm start); **charter mix rises at 4–5p** (~12/16 per game vs 7/10 owned-world — shared hulls miss cargo more often; the A2′ fare row taxes it; **deck mix per count is the ready dial** ⚙); hull-recycling added after deck exhaustion surfaced in sims; trader corpora re-cut (`analysis-trader-v31.txt`).

## v0.11 Batch A — three tested rule changes from the first human playtests (2026-06-12)
The first balance batch driven by the human-playtest + simulation convergence (`DESIGN.md` §21 "v0.11 candidates"). **One lever per change**, each with its own `KEY`, gate suite, and target-metric measurement; all surfaces (RULES · COMPONENTS · index · learn · printables · play) updated together.
- **A1 — the Towncrier reined in (`KEY → v27`).** Was: draw any goal, free, no limit (playtesters banked 6+). Now: **pay 1 `G` ⚙ to draw; hand cap 4 ⚙; at the cap the crier grants 1 good instead** (designer's variant — the building never goes dead). *Measured:* avg goals held at end **4.5 → 3.4**; goal points hold at ~⅓ of winner score.
- **A2′ — the charter fare became a SHARED MARKET PRICE (`KEY → v30`, supersedes A2 same-day).** Designer call after testing A2: instead of a per-player counter, the fare is **set by the Sailed-Ships track position** — one fare row **printed under the clock** (GWT-hiring-market style, radically simplified), per player count ⚙: 2p `2·2·3·2·2·4·5·5` (designer's row) · 3p `2·2·2·3·2·2·3·3·4·5·5` · 4p `2·2·2·3·2·2·2·3·3·3·4·4·5·5·5` · 5p analogous, max `5 G` (deadlock guard intact). Cheap stretches *welcome* charters; the closing rush pays full freight; one price for the whole table and zero per-player bookkeeping (the A2 fare disc is retired unused). `play.html`'s Voyage panel now prints the fare digits under each track slot with the current fare highlighted. *Measured (sim 500 + the requested 30×2/3/4p runs, `analysis-trader-v30.txt`):* robustness clean, ladder PASS, heuristic charter counts ≈ unchanged (they charter from need); the behavioral test of the endgame pricing belongs to the GM and the table.
- **A2 (superseded) — the per-player escalating fare (`KEY → v28`).** Was: flat `2 G`, unlimited (designer: "felt like cheating"; the GM oracle out-chartered its own fleet at 3–4p). Now: **`2 G` +1 `G` per charter you have already taken, capped at `5 G` ⚙** (a personal fare disc; the cap keeps the deadlock guard intact). *Measured honestly:* heuristic bots charter from need, so table-wide counts dip only slightly (4p 7.6→7.1, 5p 10.3→9.6), and a small GM probe still charters at this steepness — the **structure** is in; `CHARTER_STEP`/`CHARTER_MAX` are one-number dials if human play wants more bite.
- **A3 — the export premium (`KEY → v29`).** Was: kontor value flat regardless of quality (only the Hall scaled). Now: **a delivered Q4 cask scores +1★, a Q5 +2★, at any kontor ⚙** — the climb pays on the same voyage that builds majorities. *Measured:* the GM's Q4+ brewing **doubles** (0.3 → 0.7/game at 2p, n=24 directional), traders 1.0→1.2–1.3, winners' never-brewed-Q4+ rate 41%→37%, **persona lean balance unchanged** (volume/prestige near fair at 2–5p, `sim-results-v29-personas.txt`).
- **Verified:** sim 500 ×2–5p clean ×2 runs (`sim-results-v29*.txt`), ladder PASS (`ai-ladder-v29.txt`), render smoke + printables checks green, and the post-balance **CEM tuner re-run says KEEP** (`ai-tune-v29.txt` — the shipped Trader weights still stand, 49.9% best candidate). Next: human games through the Studio to judge feel; the full-budget GM verification of the climb stays on the list.

## playtests/sim-analyze.js — the strategy-analysis harness + first findings (2026-06-12)
New harness: headless cohorts with a **structured event stream hooked on the engine's own functions** (openings → win-rates · timing curves · per-tier action profiles · destination sequencing · win correlates; `TIERS`/`COUNTS`/`SAVE` knobs; GM playout events excluded). First corpora committed: `analysis-trader-v26.txt` (1,200 trader games, 2–4p) and `analysis-gm-v26.txt` (30 Guildmaster-vs-Trader oracle games).
- **Findings (trader corpus):** at 2p the **Brewhouse-first opening beats Market-first 57.4% vs 42.4%** (the start goods already cover a brew — sourcing first is a wasted tempo); winners' separators are **early upgrades** (round 6.6; losers never-rate 3×) and **Bergen presence** (86% of winners deliver there vs 67% of losers); **majority points are the largest winner-vs-loser gap** (+6.2, ahead of delivery +5.5 and goals +3.9); destination arcs match design intent — Bruges early (83%), Bergen/Novgorod mid, **London (62%) and the Hall (86%) late**.
- **Findings (GM oracle, directional n=30):** the search player **opens at the Cellar/colR** (brew+age in one activation off the warm start) where the heuristics open rowT; it **feeds London** (1.0 casks/game vs the traders' 0.2 — the engine-kontor identity works when played well), takes **3.4 upgrades/game**, **avoids gifting rival-loads** (0.6 vs 1.4–2.7), charters freely as tempo — and at 2p it **skips the Q4+ climb almost entirely** (⚙ flag: wide volume may dominate the export climb at 2p; verify at full GUILD_MS before acting).
- **`playtests/FINDINGS-v26.md`** — the compiled findings list + the solvable/broken design-health review: nothing hard-broken, game demonstrably unsolved (policy-dependent openings; 99% GM-over-Trader skill headroom); **two FLAGs** (the rival-load interaction is anti-optimal as designed; the Q4+ climb looks dominated at 2p) and **two WATCHes** (5p charter volume; Bergen-mandatory/London-subtle asymmetry), each with its dial and a verification path.
- Known issue: long Guildmaster cohorts degrade well past the early ~5s/game rate — keep GM runs ≤50 games (stderr heartbeat added); trader/journeyman cohorts run thousands of games per minute.

## play.html — "Send to Studio": playtest ingest to Waterworks Studio (2026-06-12)
Completed games can now be recorded to the **Waterworks Studio** publishing platform (`KEY → v26`). The final-standing panel (and the game-over bar) gains **⇪ Send to Studio**: a dialog takes the per-game **API key once** (saved to `localStorage.playtest_api_key`), plus the spec's optional session fields (notes · what was new · changes to make), and POSTs the playtest.

## AI opponents — Phase 3: the Monte Carlo "Guildmaster" (2026-06-12)
A fourth, expert AI tier (`KEY → v24`): the **Guildmaster** plays **flat Monte Carlo over the canonical engine itself**. At each enumerable prompt it lists the legal options; for each option it clones `(S, UI)` (the undo JSON trick), applies it, hands every seat to the **Journeyman rollout policy**, plays to game end, and scores the final **margin** (own total minus best rival — the anti-kingmaking objective). Round-robin playouts under a time budget (`GUILD_MS` ⚙ 250ms/decision in-page, capped 120ms at instant speed; the harnesses lower it to 40ms for bulk runs); best mean margin wins. Phase 3 of `AUTOMA.md`.
- **Result: a step-change in strength.** At 2p the Guildmaster beats the Trader **99.0%** (n=200, seats swapped), average score **63.4 vs 26.8** — it both out-scores the Trader's normal output (~46) *and* suppresses it, because the margin objective denies majorities and snipes upgrades. This is the flat-MC prediction from the original proposal, measured: the playouts *discover* value (Bergen races, Hall timing, slot/clock pressure) that no hand-written rule encoded.
- **Architecture.** Same-scope global swap with full restore (`aiMC`) — **no engine refactor**: playouts run the live rules, so every future rules revision is absorbed automatically. The option enumerator (`aiGmOptions`) covers move·stops·Market·Harbor·ship-binding·placement·charter·brew·load·deploy·benefit·wild·reach·alms·goal-draw; the remaining prompts fall back to the trader policy (the Guildmaster *is* a trader between MC decisions — `aiIsTrader` includes it). Guards: `aiSimulating` keeps playouts from touching the DOM (`gameOver`), the timers (`maybeRunAI`), or recursing into MC. Rollouts use **Journeyman** (greedy) — ~10× cheaper than trader rollouts and sufficient, since the *scorer* prices majorities/the Hall, not the rollout policy (an all-trader-rollout variant played ~4× slower for no measured strength gain at the test budget).
- **The full ladder now (`ai-ladder-v24.txt`, 2p, seats swapped):** Journeyman > Apprentice **66.2%** (n=1000) · Trader > Journeyman **62.2%** (n=1000, every lean ≥60%) · **Guildmaster > Trader 99.0%** (n=200 at the reduced 40ms budget — the in-page 250ms tier is stronger still). 0 errors everywhere; mixed 3–5p strictly ordered; pace in band. Standard gates clean (`sim-results-v24*.txt`); render smoke includes a Guildmaster game through the real render layer.
- **UX:** pick **AI · Guildmaster** per seat in New Game; it "thinks" ~¼s per decision (a Guildmaster turn ≈ 3–7s at normal speed), narrated in the log like every tier. Same rules, same information, no cheating — the strength is search, not bonuses.
- **⚙ Open:** `GUILD_MS` (the strength-vs-latency dial — it is also the natural *difficulty slider* if sub-Guildmaster MC tiers are ever wanted); whether the Trader→Guildmaster gap (99%) wants an intermediate budget tier; UCT/max-n (the original Phase 3 "graduate if flat MC plateaus" — it has not).

## AI opponents — Phase 2: the offline weight tuner (2026-06-11)
The Trader's policy weights are now a **tuning surface** (`KEY → v23`): centralized in `AI_W` in `play.html`, with a per-seat override on `p.ai.w` — which is how the new optimizer pits two weight sets inside one game. Apprentice/Journeyman read none of it (frozen baselines). Phase 2 of `AUTOMA.md`.
- **New harness: `node playtests/ai-tune.js [gens] [pop]`** — a CEM optimizer over the 10 `AI_W` dims. Each candidate plays the **incumbent defaults** (2p, n=160), **Journeyman** (2p, n=80), and a **4p table of incumbents** (n=60) inside the canonical engine; fitness = win-rate, so every rules revision automatically re-grounds the tuning. A 10-generation run ≈ 2 min (~48k games) + a high-N confirmation with a clear ADOPT/KEEP verdict. **This is the post-balance-pass re-tuning tool** — what was an editing session is now a re-runnable check.
- **Findings (first full run, `ai-tune-v23.txt`).** The Phase 1 hand-tuned weights sit **at a local optimum**: best CEM candidate confirmed at only 50.4% vs the incumbent (CI includes 50% → KEEP). The surface is a **plateau** — CEM drifted weights ±20% with no performance change — so Trader strength is **robust to the weight drift a balance retune would cause**. One consistent directional signal was adopted: `leanVal` 1.15 → **1.05** (the volume lean's big value tilt was a mild handicap; CEM pulled it toward 1 in every generation, and the lean's head-to-head win-rate confirmed it).
- **One structural skill added (found while chasing the gate, not by the tuner):** the Trader now **deploys a Ready cask onto the line it is about to activate** — `aiLine` deploys *before* `chooseLine`, so the cask joins that line's stops and its signature action **fires the same turn** (a free tempo gain on every deploy; previously it took the first empty slot).
- **Verified** (`ai-ladder-v23.txt`, N=1000/pairing): ladder PASS — Journeyman > Apprentice **66.4%**, Trader > Journeyman **62.2%**, Trader > Apprentice **72.1%**; 0 errors everywhere; mixed 3–5p strictly ordered. Standard gates clean (`sim-results-v23*.txt`: 0 crashes, pace 13.9–14.5, ~100% clock, personas unchanged); render smoke green.

## v0.9 — tiered majorities · the London/Bergen identity split · seat compensation (2026-06-07)
Three balance changes, each **sim-validated** on the persona-driven harness (a bot that *commits* to a lean, so the Hall and Bergen are actually played — exposing what the old greedy bot couldn't). Engine (`play.html`, `KEY → v19`) + all docs and pages updated.
- **The headline de-risk:** with lean-committed bots, **volume vs prestige is balanced and viable** and the GWT "blend beats pure" thesis holds (blended winners 23% → ~70%). The earlier "prestige is marginal (3% Hall)" reading was a **greedy-bot artifact**, not a design flaw — the Hall needed no buff.
- **Tiered, ranked majorities (Lacerda-style).** Each kontor's majority now pays **1st/2nd/3rd** by delivered-cask count; **2-player skips 2nd** (winner-take-all); ties split the occupied tiers. The rich majority is **concentrated at Bergen (10/6/3 ⚙)**, with the other kontore minor (Bruges/London 2, Novgorod 3) and value-led. *Why concentrate:* broad tiered majorities at every kontor over-feed the volume lean and **starve prestige** (the lean that contests no majority); concentrating at Bergen fixed the previously-underpowered **majority** lean while keeping prestige viable. *Why not 15/10/5:* the literal Lacerda numbers overshoot our ~45-pt score scale (majority dominates, prestige tanks, scores inflate to ~50); **10/6/3** is the sim-balanced version of the same structure. Result: **all three leans within ~1–3 pts of fair at every count** (the "no pure path wins" ideal, finally measured).
- **London = the engine kontor; Bergen = the majority kontor.** They were near-clones (both Q2, value 3, upgrade). **Bergen no longer grants an upgrade** (its whole draw is the majority); **London** is now the accessible Q2 upgrade destination. Upgrade-earning kontore: **London + Novgorod** (the greedy economy is unchanged — total upgrades/game held steady, since the bot never shipped Bergen anyway).
- **Seat compensation: +1 G to every seat after the first** (`SEAT_COMP ⚙`). Fixed turn order gives P1 a real structural edge (sim: P1 54.7% at 2p, monotonic 38/34/28 at 3p). +1 G flattens it — seat-win-rate spread **2p 9.4→2.2, 3p 10.2→3.6**; 4–5p improved (residual middle/last-seat wobble for human playtest).
- **Verified (N=1000 × 2–5p, persona + greedy):** 0 crashes / 0 deadlocks, pace 14.0–14.4 rounds (in band), ~99–100% clock-ended, score scale unchanged (~45). Harness gained `PERSONAS` (lean-committed bots) and a `TUNE` hook (DEST re-stats). Sim outputs: `playtests/sim-results-v19-final.txt`, `sim-eval-v19-final-personas.txt`.

## v0.8 — occupancy pressure · the "Wharf" naming · fixed-quality exports · all six neutral buildings (2026-06-06 → 06-07)
Incremental refinements on the v0.7 "The Wharf" architecture (full rationale: `DESIGN.md` §21, dated entries).

## v0.7 — "The Wharf": a ground-up reel-in to GWT/Distilled weight (2026-06-05)
A near-total redesign that re-targets the game from "Lacerda-grade" to **Great Western Trail / Distilled** weight — *too much game, the right amount of theme.* Keeps the grid, the perimeter ring, the dual-role cask, the merchant-shipping fantasy, and the theme; **cuts roughly half the rules.** (Full rationale + the pace-model findings: `DESIGN.md` §21.)
- **One legible loop, walked on the grid.** The four stations **are** the loop and the forced-move circuit walks it: **A Market (Source) → B Brewhouse (Brew) → D Cellar (Age) → C Harbor (Ship)**. Every line is two adjacent loop-steps. (The v0.6 build×cash-out diagonal is retired for legibility.)
- **The cask is a dual-role action-tile in 3 states:** **maturing** (private vessel) → **on a slot** (a shared ring slot — your cargo + a **public, chunky, loop-advancing action** by type: Gruit→Source · Hopped→Age · L3→free Load · L4→+presence · L5→Wild) → **delivered** (shipped → scores → gone). Only cask on a slot are public/contestable; the brewery is private.
- **The wharf = the perimeter ring,** a transient mix of **deployed casks + owned ships + 2–3 seeded neutral buildings.** **One fire rule,** everything **on the active player's turn** (no out-of-turn skims — the v0.6 thing that felt wrong).
- **Value lives in *destinations*, not two tracks.** Ship to **kontore** for trade value + majorities (volume), or to **the Hall** for prestige (scarcity). Same verb, different destination. **Aging** is the new value-over-time signal. **Demand market, type frontier, and the Hall station are cut.**
- **Non-destructive interaction:** you may **ship a rival's cask on a slot** on your ship — they score it & pick the benefit; you get the slot/ship/timing. *A 1350 brewmaster doesn't fully control where their casks go.*
- **Deliver → earn an Upgrade → brew better → deliver better** (Distilled-style). Upgrades (Rooms + Modifiers) subsume v0.6's Privileges; **Fairs, route-lane tiles, the recipe card deck/boons/tuck, twins, and aging cubes are cut.**
- **Pace fixes** (the model put v0.6's first voyage at turn 6): **warm start** (1 Cog + 1 Ready cask), **start with 2 vessels**, **base verbs always work**, **all destinations open** → first voyage ≈ turn 2–3. **Recipes reel in to Gruit + Hopped start + plain permission tiles.**
- **Scoring:** delivery value (by destination) + majorities + goals (best few). No reach/standing tracks.
- **The Charter relief valve (added in the `play.html` port).** Porting the engine exposed a hard deadlock the bare wharf model allows (all your ships sailed + the ring full + vessels clogged = no legal move). Fix: at the Harbor you may always pay **⚙2 `G`** to ship **one** Ready cask (vessel *or* wharf) on an immediate single-cask voyage — it delivers and advances the Sailed-Ships clock, but at one cask for the fare it's strictly worse per cask than a Cog/Hulk, so owning ships stays the race. The deadlock guard; folded into `RULES.md`/`DESIGN.md §21·E′`/`COMPONENTS.md`/`TILES.md`.

## Balance lessons carried forward (distilled from the retired sims)
These are *why the current rules look the way they do* — keep them in mind before re-tuning:

1. **Whichever scoring axis is left uncapped becomes the dominant strategy.** Across passes, **tokens → standing-goals → lane-stacking** each ran away in turn. Hence: the **best-3 goal cap**, **capped lane value**, and **no passive faucets**.
2. **Standing structurally out-scored reach** in early builds, because reach was **capacity-bounded** (routes fill, then you can't sell) while standing was **unbounded** *and* uniquely carried **goals**. v0.6's answer: give reach its **own engine** (ships + routes + the self-accelerating Sailed-Ships clock + destination bonuses) and keep the **cycled best-3 goals** (each rewards the axis you *didn't* bank).
3. **The game was throughput-bound** (1 vessel + multi-step brews → only a few casks all game) → **passive aging**, **vessel scaling**, and **big recipe boons** now grease tempo.
4. **The old end-clocks never fired** (heritage threshold too high; the reach/city clock rare) → the **self-accelerating Sailed-Ships track** + the **enshrine backstop**.
5. **The random opening was unfair** (a Tripel start won ~1.7× a Hopped start) → start with **2 random premium recipe cards** (narrowed the swing to ~1.4×).
6. **The three "leanings" are not orthogonal:** *Engine* overlaps *Standing* (you climb the ladder by banking higher casks), and both sell-modes feed the same value-timing. The honest model is **Reach vs Standing, tempo-tuned by engine** — a *pure* extreme should lose to a **blend** (the GWT ideal).
7. **2p wants lower route caps** so majorities settle meaningfully and games don't drag.
8. **(v0.7) Ambition was the bug.** v0.6 chased Lacerda weight; a play-through + a headless pace model (first voyage **turn 6**, goods-/geometry-bound, the optimal line invisible) showed the cost. The fix wasn't another subsystem — it was **cutting** to one walkable loop and making the actions chunky and on-turn. *Lessons #1/#2 (cap the axes; give each path its engine) are now served by **destinations**, not parallel tracks; #3 (throughput) by the **warm start + base-verbs-always-work**.*

> The pre-v0.7 open questions (esp. #2 and #6) are largely **dissolved** by the §21 reframe (one delivery pile, no parallel reach/standing tracks). The new top question is the same as always: a **human playtest** — now of the v0.7 loop's *feel* and pace — before the `⚙` numbers are trusted. See `DESIGN.md` §21 "Still open / next dials (v0.7)."
